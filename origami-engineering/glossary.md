# Glossary — Origami Engineering

**Aligned coupling** — joining two Miura-ori tubes by pure translation (no rotation) in the cross-section plane; flat-foldable but anisotropic and squeezing-prone (Ch 2).

**Bandgap (eigenvalue), β = λ₈ − λ₇** — the gap between the intended rigid-folding eigenvalue (λ₇) and the next-cheapest deformation mode (λ₈); a large bandgap means the structure is stiff for everything except its intended motion (Ch 2).

**Bar-and-hinge model** — simplified structural model capturing fold-line bending, panel bending, and panel stretch/shear; used to compute stiffness/mass matrices for origami assemblages faster than full shell FE (Ch 2).

**Bistable state** — a configuration where the structure can settle into either of two distinct stable shapes depending on fold direction (e.g., square-cross-section snap point at 96.4% extension) (Ch 2).

**Convex / concave vertex** — a mesh vertex is convex if the solid angle around it exceeds 2π, concave if less than 2π (Ch 1).

**Cortes verticais e horizontais (vertical/horizontal cuts)** — cuts in origamic architecture that release a face from the sheet; their orientation is always perpendicular to the zero line's orientation (Ch 3).

**Descriptive geometry** — the geometric discipline underlying both rigid-origami kinematics (Ch 1) and origamic-architecture closure conditions (Ch 3).

**Detalhes construtivos (constructive details)** — small cut-out areas representing architectural detail (windows, ornament) in an origamic architecture model (Ch 3).

**Dobra vale / dobra montanha (valley / mountain fold)** — the two secondary fold types in origamic architecture, defined relative to the zero line; their spacing sets a volume's depth/height (Ch 3).

**DOF (degrees of freedom)** — count of independent motions a mesh/structure retains; for a triangulated disk mesh, DOF = V₀ + 3 + S (Ch 1).

**Eigenmode / eigenvalue** — a structural deformation mode and its associated excitation-energy value from solving Kv = λMv; modes 1–6 are rigid-body motion and are excluded from analysis (Ch 2).

**Face volumétrica / face de sustentação (volumetric face / sustaining face)** — paired faces in origamic architecture: one shows the facade, the other (perpendicular) structurally anchors it (Ch 3).

**Freeform Origami** — interactive software (Tachi, 2010) used to numerically explore constrained rigid-origami configuration spaces (Ch 1).

**Internal coupling** — nesting a smaller "internal" tube inside an external tube so the assembly locks flat at a specific target extension (Ch 2).

**Kirigami** — the paper-cutting technique (a variant/component of origami) used to create new shapes via cuts (Ch 3).

**Linha zero (zero line)** — the master/reference fold line in origamic architecture from which all other volumes, folds and cuts are dimensioned (Ch 3).

**Miura-ori cell** — a four-panel parallelogram unit defined by height a, width c, and vertex angle α; the basis of the tubes studied in Ch 2.

**Opening-angle families (0°, 90°, 180°, 360°)** — the four classes of origamic architecture model, distinguished by the angle at which the model is viewed/opened (Ch 3).

**Origamic architecture** — Masahiro Chatani's (1981) technique compiling origami (fold) + kirigami (cut) + pop-up (mount) to make a self-mounting 3D paper model of a building from one sheet (Ch 3).

**Percentage of extension** — the configuration parameter of a Miura-ori sheet/tube, from 0% (folded flat on Y–Z plane) to 100% (flat on X–Y plane) (Ch 2).

**Plano horizontal / plano vertical** — the two planes formed when an origamic-architecture sheet opens to 90° at the zero line (Ch 3).

**Pop-up** — the mounting/assembly mechanism (commonly seen in children's books/cards) that origamic architecture appropriates for self-standing models (Ch 3).

**Rigid origami** — a folding kinematics in which all deformation concentrates at crease lines while panels remain flat (undeformed) (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Singular flat state** — the fully-unfolded rigid-origami configuration where the constraint Jacobian degenerates, producing a branch point rather than a smooth configuration manifold (Ch 1).

**Squeezing deformation** — a cheap alternative deformation mode (bending only, no stretch) where one end of a tube folds while the other unfolds; competes with the intended rigid-folding mode in non-zipper couplings (Ch 2).

**Vacuumatics** — a negative-pressure double membrane containing loose particles; compressing the particles via vacuum creates friction-based stiffness (Ch 1).

**Waterbomb corrugation ("namako")** — a triangle-based fold pattern with mixed convex/concave vertices, able to form freeform double-curved surfaces (Ch 1).

**Zero line** — see *Linha zero*.

**Zipper coupling** — joining two Miura-ori tubes by rotating one into a zig-zag alignment with the other before bonding adjacent facets; produces a large eigenvalue bandgap and near-isotropic stiffness (Ch 2).

**2.5D** — descriptive term (Ch 3) for origamic-architecture models: neither fully 3D (sides stay open) nor fully 2D (faces are cut/folded into relief).
