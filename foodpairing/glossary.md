# Glossary

**Aldehyde** — functional group of aroma molecules; low odour threshold, scent shifts green→citrus→fatty as carbon chain lengthens (Ch 5).

**Analytical perception** — the brain's ability to isolate individual tastes within a mixture; how taste (unlike smell) is processed (Ch 3).

**Anosmia** — total inability to smell a particular compound (or all compounds); varies molecule-by-molecule and person-by-person (Ch 2).

**Aroma descriptor** — the most granular label in Foodpairing's system (e.g. "pineapple," "smoky"); 70 total, grouped into 14 aroma types (Ch 7).

**Aroma library** — a person's trained set of reference points for recognizing and naming scents, built through deliberate repeated exposure (Ch 6).

**Aroma type** — one of 14 top-level categories (fruity, citrus, floral, green, herbal, vegetable, caramel, roasted, nutty, woody, spicy, cheesy, animal, chemical) used to classify every ingredient's profile (Ch 7).

**Aroma wheel** — a two-ring circular chart visualizing one ingredient's aroma profile: inner ring = 14 types, outer ring = descriptor concentrations (Ch 7).

**Aromatic link** — a shared aroma molecule between two ingredients that explains why they pair well (Ch 1).

**Fungiform papillae** — small tongue protuberances containing taste buds; density determines supertaster/non-taster status (Ch 3).

**Furanone** — caramellic aroma compound (e.g. furaneol) formed by roasting furans (Ch 5).

**GC-MS (gas chromatography–mass spectrometry)** — lab technique separating and identifying an ingredient's volatile compounds (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Gustation** — the sense of taste; limited to five basic tastants, contrasted with the much richer sense of olfaction (Ch 3).

**Headspace** — the air space above a food/drink into which volatile aroma molecules evaporate (Ch 2).

**Hydrophilic aroma molecule** — a molecule with affinity for water; stays dissolved in liquid rather than evaporating into headspace (Ch 2).

**Hydrophobic aroma molecule** — a "water-averse" molecule that dissolves more easily in fat and escapes readily into headspace (Ch 2).

**Inhibition** — an aroma interaction where one molecule suppresses the perceived scent of another entirely (Ch 2).

**Key odorant** — a volatile compound present above its own odour recognition threshold — the only compounds that actually register as flavour (Ch 2).

**Lactone** — cyclic ester functional group; common in milk products, gives coconut/peach/creamy/woody notes (Ch 5).

**Maillard reaction** — non-enzymatic reaction at ~140°C between amino acids and sugars, producing hundreds of new roasted/baked/fried aroma molecules (Ch 5).

**Odour object** — a stored, recognizable "signature pattern" the brain forms for a previously-encountered scent (Ch 4).

**Odour recognition threshold** — the lowest concentration at which a compound can be detected; varies by molecule and by individual (Ch 2).

**Olfaction** — the sense of smell; responsible for up to 90% of perceived flavour (Ch 3).

**Olfactory bulb** — brain structure receiving signals from olfactory receptor neurons; connects directly to the amygdala (emotion) and hippocampus (memory) (Ch 3, Ch 4).

**Olfactory epithelium** — membrane in the upper nasal passage containing ~40 million olfactory receptor neurons (Ch 3).

**Olfactory fatigue** — temporary reduced ability to detect scents after extended smelling; recoverable with breaks (Ch 6).

**Olfactory white** — the generic-smelling result of blending 20+ odorants that evenly span the odour space, regardless of composition (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Orthonasal detection** — aroma perception via directly sniffing air through the nostrils; the primary olfactory pathway (Ch 3).

**Overshadowing (hypoadditivity)** — an aroma interaction where a mixture's intensity equals just one component's intensity (Ch 2).

**Overtoning** — combining multiple ingredients with strong shared aromatic links to build subtle depth without adding contrast (Ch 7).

**Pairing grid** — a table listing a main ingredient plus up to ten candidate pairings, with dotted columns for each of the 14 aroma types (Ch 7).

**Retronasal detection** — aroma perception via air pushed through the nasopharyngeal passage during chewing/swallowing; the secondary olfactory pathway (Ch 3).

**Sotolon** — a furan/furanoid compound smelling of maple syrup/burnt sugar at low concentration and fenugreek/curry at high concentration; has an extremely low odour threshold in water (Ch 2, Ch 5).

**Supertaster** — a person with unusually high fungiform papillae density and heightened taste intensity (~25% of people) (Ch 3).

**Suppression** — an aroma interaction where a mixture registers less intense than its strongest single component, but more intense than the rest (Ch 2).

**Synergy/additivity** — an aroma interaction where combined intensity exceeds the sum of individual molecules (Ch 2).

**Synthetic processing** — the perceptual phenomenon where a mixture of 4+ odorants is perceived as one new unified scent rather than its individual parts (Ch 2).

**Tastant** — a chemical (sugar, salt, acid, etc.) that stimulates a taste receptor; the five basic tastants are sweet, salty, sour, bitter, umami (Ch 3).

**Terpene** — functional group responsible for woody/piney notes in citrus, herbs, and spices; oxygenates into terpenoids (menthol, linalool) (Ch 5).

**Trigeminal sensation** — a sensory channel distinct from taste/smell responsible for temperature, texture, pain, cooling, and tingling (e.g. capsaicin, menthol) (Ch 3).

**Unity-in-variety** — Daniel Berlyne's aesthetic principle: the most successful pairings balance genuine variety against underlying coherence (Ch 7).

**VOC (volatile organic compound)** — a chemical that vaporizes easily at room temperature; ~10,000 identified in food, only a fraction of which are key odorants in any given ingredient (Ch 2).
