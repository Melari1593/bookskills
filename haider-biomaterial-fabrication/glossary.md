# Glossary

Alphabetical index of significant technical terms used across all 10 chapters. Chapter numbers indicate where each term is introduced or most substantively discussed.

**Amphiphilic peptide** — a peptide with distinct polar/nonpolar regions that self-assembles into micelles, vesicles, or nanofibers (surfactant-like peptides, peptide amphiphiles, drug amphiphiles). (Ch9)

**Automatic slowdown (autodeceleration)** — decline in polymerization propagation/termination rate constants as crosslinking restricts reactive-species diffusion; halts polymerization once functional groups are consumed. (Ch7)

**Berry number (Be)** — dimensionless group from polymer solution viscosity/concentration predicting whether chain entanglement will yield bead-free fiber during spinning. (Ch4, Ch5)

**Bioactivity** — a material's ability to actively promote cell adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation via compositional similarity to target tissue. (Ch1, Ch2)

**Biocompatibility** — a material's ability to avoid inducing immune/inflammatory response in the host. (Ch1, Ch2)

**Biodegradability** — controlled material breakdown into non-toxic products on a timeline matched to tissue regeneration. (Ch1, Ch2)

**Bioink vs. biomaterial ink** — bioinks carry living cells and require gentle, cytocompatible processing; biomaterial inks are structural materials (ceramics, thermoplastics, metals) processed under conditions cells could not survive. (Ch6)

**Bioprinting** — 3D printing with living cells positioned via extrusion, jetting, or laser-assisted deposition; not itself a distinct AM category but a cross-cutting cell-delivery application. (Ch1, Ch6)

**Camphene-based freeze-casting** — using camphene as a sacrificial freeze-medium for ceramic/metal scaffolds, sublimed then sintered. (Ch3)

**Centrifugal spinning (CS)** — rotary/rotational-force fiber fabrication using a spinning head instead of an electric field. (Ch4)

**Coaxial electrospinning** — dual-capillary spinneret producing core-shell nanofibers for payload protection/sequenced release. (Ch5)

**Compression molding** — heat + pressure compaction of polymer/porogen mix in a mold; low material shrinkage, relatively low processing temperature. (Ch10)

**Decellularized ECM** — native tissue stripped of cells, reused as a natural scaffold or bioink; retains biochemical signals but often mechanically soft. (Ch1, Ch6)

**Directional (unidirectional) freezing** — applying a temperature gradient to grow aligned ice crystals, yielding anisotropic porous channels after freeze-drying. (Ch3)

**Drug amphiphile** — a hydrophobic drug directly conjugated to a self-assembling peptide, forming filamentous nanostructures without a separate carrier. (Ch9)

**Electrospinning (ES)** — Taylor-cone-based fiber drawing under a 10–50 kV electric field; four variants are blending, coaxial, emulsion, and melt. (Ch1, Ch5)

**Eutectic point** — temperature below which a solution must be frozen to avoid large ice crystals (typically −40 to −80 °C aqueous). (Ch3)

**Extrusion** — melt-compounded polymer/porogen pushed through a die to form tubes, films, or defined cross-sections. (Ch10)

**Fiber bonding** — PGA fibers coated with a dissolvable PLLA solution, heated, then PLLA dissolved away to leave a bonded fiber membrane. (Ch2)

**Fiber mesh scaffold** — top-down 3D scaffold built from individual or interwoven fibers; historically mechanically weak unless process-optimized. (Ch9)

**Fused deposition modeling (FDM)** — material-extrusion 3D printing of softened thermoplastic filament, deposited layer by layer. (Ch1, Ch2, Ch6)

**Gas foaming** — CO2/N2 pressurization then depressurization to create pores without organic solvent; risks closed-pore structure. (Ch1, Ch2, Ch8)

**GelMA (gelatin methacrylate)** — UV-cross-linkable modified gelatin bioink solving native gelatin's slow/unreliable gelation. (Ch6)

**Gas-assisted (foam) injection molding** — blowing agent (chemical or physical) generates pores in situ during injection molding. (Ch10)

**High-compression molding-salt leaching** — combines salt leaching with high-pressure (up to 640 MPa) compression for strong, regular-architecture scaffolds. (Ch8)

**Hydrogen bonding** — directional non-covalent force dominant in protein secondary structure and ligand-receptor binding affinity; central to self-assembly. (Ch9)

**Ice-segregation-induced self-assembly (ISISA)** — directional freezing used to concentrate/organize dissolved components into a hierarchically porous aligned structure. (Ch3)

**Injection molding** — molten polymer/porogen injected into a mold cavity for complex, dimensionally accurate, repeatable geometries. (Ch10)

**Macropore / mesopore / micropore** — pore-size classes (macro >50 nm, meso 2–50 nm, micro 0.1–2 nm); TE scaffolds usually target macroporous regime. (Ch1, Ch2)

**Melt molding** — polymer powder heated above glass-transition temperature and pressed/poured into a mold; solvent-free but high-temperature. (Ch2, Ch10)

**Microcellular foam injection molding** — supercritical fluid dissolved in polymer nucleates microscale pores on pressure drop; no porogen leaching needed. (Ch10)

**Multi-domain peptide** — ABA-motif amphiphilic peptide forming self-supporting gels via β-sheet folding and charge shielding. (Ch9)

**Multiphoton lithography (MPL)** — simultaneous two-photon absorption confines photopolymerization to a sub-micron 3D focal volume, enabling true 3D nanoscale hydrogel/scaffold fabrication. (Ch7)

**Nanogel** — cross-linked polymer network with hydrophilic/hydrophobic groups, volume-responsive to environment; used in protein refolding and antigen delivery. (Ch9)

**Osteoinductivity** — a material's ability to actively promote bone formation; key ceramic property. (Ch1)

**Particulate leaching (salt leaching)** — dispersing a water-soluble porogen (typically NaCl) in polymer, then leaching it out to form interconnected pores. (Ch1, Ch2, Ch8)

**Peptide amphiphile (PA)** — hydrophobic lipid chain covalently attached to a peptide, self-assembling into a hydrophobic core/hydrophilic periphery, often cylindrical nanofibers. (Ch9)

**Phase inversion** — polymer solution precipitated by mixing with water; porosity tunable via concentration/temperature. (Ch2)

**Phase separation** — cryomilled blend of immiscible polymers heated to segregate, then selectively leached for interconnected pores. (Ch10)

**Photocage** — a molecular gatekeeper group that physically blocks a reaction until light-cleaved, releasing a reactive group for conjugation. (Ch7)

**Photoinitiator (PI)** — organic compound converting absorbed light into free radicals to start polymerization; Type-I (self-cleaving) or Type-II (co-initiator dependent). (Ch7)

**Photolabile linker** — a bond (o-nitrobenzyl ester, coumarin, disulfide) that cleaves under light exposure at physiological conditions for controlled release. (Ch7)

**Porogen** — sacrificial phase (salt, gas, particle) used to template pores, removed by leaching, sublimation, or gas evolution. (Ch1, Ch2, Ch8, Ch10)

**Rapid prototyping (RP) / Solid Free-Form Fabrication (SFF)** — CAD-driven fabrication offering precise spatial control (SLA, FDM, SLS, 3DP). (Ch1, Ch2, Ch6)

**Scaffold** — 3D structure serving as a temporary ECM template for cell adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation. (Ch1)

**Selective laser sintering/melting (SLS/SLM)** — powder bed fusion using a laser to fuse successive polymer, ceramic, or metal powder layers. (Ch1, Ch6)

**Self-assembly** — spontaneous molecular organization into ordered structures driven by weak non-covalent forces (electrostatic, hydrophobic, aromatic stacking, hydrogen bonding, Van der Waals). (Ch4, Ch9)

**SLUP (Salt Leaching Using Powder)** — mixes powder (e.g., bioglass) with polymer and salt, avoiding both solvent and heat-treatment steps. (Ch8)

**Solution blow spinning (SBS)** — high-velocity gas shear draws polymer solution into fiber without an electric field. (Ch4)

**Solvent casting** — dissolving polymer in organic solvent, casting into a mold, evaporating solvent to leave a solid membrane. (Ch1, Ch2, Ch10)

**Solvent-casting particulate leaching (SCPL)** — solvent casting combined with a dispersed, later-leached porogen. (Ch2, Ch8, Ch10)

**Spray-Freezing into Liquid (SFL)** — atomizing feed solution into cryogenic liquid at high pressure to produce frozen microparticles before freeze-drying. (Ch3)

**Stereolithography (SLA)** — liquid-reservoir photopolymerization; print bed repeatedly lowered into/lifted from a UV-curable resin bath. (Ch1, Ch6)

**Sublimation** — solid-to-vapor phase change without a liquid phase; the core mechanism of freeze-drying that avoids heat damage. (Ch3)

**Surfactant-like peptide** — polar-head/nonpolar-tail peptide self-assembling into vesicles, micelles, or nanotubes (Zhang's design). (Ch9)

**Taylor cone** — the conical droplet shape a polymer solution forms at a needle tip when electrostatic force overcomes surface tension, marking the fiber-jet origin. (Ch5)

**Thermally-induced phase separation (TIPS)** — solvent removal via phase separation, works only for thermoplastics but allows bioactive-molecule integration. (Ch1)

**Wire-network molding** — removable wire lattice embedded in a mold templates a controlled interconnected pore network, independent of porogen chemistry. (Ch10)
