# Part 3.3.3: Hair Aging: Fundamentals, Protection and Repair
**Authors**: Padmaja Prem, Ph.D. (Combe Incorporated)

## Core Idea
Like skin, scalp and hair undergo intrinsic (genetic/hormonal: graying, androgenic alopecia) and extrinsic (UV, pollution, smoking, grooming, chemical/heat treatments) aging. The consumer-perceivable signs — thinning, loss of color, dryness, loss of shine and manageability — are measurable and are driven largely by oxidative stress, lipid loss and disulfide damage. Anti-aging hair care combines scalp care, gentle regimens, protection (UV, heat) and actives that act on both shaft and living root.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Intrinsic vs extrinsic hair aging**:
  - Intrinsic: lower melanin production (graying), androgenic alopecia, declining sebum, follicle shrinkage, reduced lipid and keratin production.
  - Extrinsic: UV, pollution (cigarette smoke, exhaust, smog), diet, lifestyle, chemical and heat treatments, grooming -> lipid oxidation, disulfide cleavage, amino acid degradation, melanin oxidation/bleaching.
- **Five critical aging attributes**: thinning, loss of color, loss of moisture, loss of shine, loss of manageability.
  - When to use: structuring claims and test plans for anti-aging hair products.
- **Scalp-type regimen model (normal / oily / dry)**:
  - How: normal — shampoo 3-4x/week + light conditioner, warm oil treatment and massage 1-2x/month; oily — daily deep-cleansing (anti-dandruff if needed), avoid leave-in conditioners; dry — shampoo 1-2x/week with oil/moisturizer-containing product, thick cream conditioner left ~10 min, weekly warm oil treatment + massage.
- **Oxidative-stress theory of graying**: H2O2 accumulates (millimolar) in gray/white hair shafts (shown by FT-Raman); catalase and methionine sulfoxide reductases A and B are absent/low; H2O2 oxidizes methionine in tyrosinase and blunts its function.

## Key Concepts
- **Hair structure recap**: follicle (bulb, IRS) + shaft (cuticle, cortex, medulla). Cuticle = thin, colorless overlapping scales governing shine, smoothness, friction/manageability; cortex = strength, color, texture; medulla only in large thick hairs.
- **Melanin**: tyrosine oxidized by tyrosinase. Eumelanin = rice-like granules, brown to black (brown and black subtypes; more eumelanin = darker; small amount of brown eumelanin alone = blond). Pheomelanin = diffuse, yellow to red, also involves cysteine; gives warm/golden/auburn tones in brown hair. Gray hair has few granules; white hair none (whiteness is optical).
- **Thinning**: attributed to rising DHT with age weakening follicles, which shrink (miniaturize), producing shorter, finer hair that falls. Men tend to bald; women thin with frontal preservation, more pronounced after menopause (less estrogen to counter androgens).
- **Hair-loss statistics (American Hair Loss Association)**: ~2/3 of US men have some loss by 35; ~85% significantly thinning by 50; women are 40% of US hair-loss sufferers; 16% of women under 50, 30-40% of women 70+.
- **Fiber diameter**: large increases in first year and teens; peaks ~age 20 in men and mid-40s in women; steeper decline in Japanese vs Caucasian males.
- **Graying theories**: loss of follicle melanocytes, tyrosinase inactivation, decreased stem cell factor, free radical theory (melanocyte apoptosis + oxidative stress in pigmentary unit).
- **Pseudo-catalase evidence**: topical narrow-band UV-activated pseudo-catalase PC-KUS reduced epidermal H2O2 and repigmented eyelashes and skin (Bradford) — supports reversibility via catalase-mimetic molecules.
- **Sebum decline**: sebum forms protective microfilm; production falls with age, particularly in females; deficiency -> fragile, itchy scalp; dry, dull, breakage-prone hair.
- **Cuticle lipids**: fatty acids, ceramides, cholesterol = 0.7-1.3% of hair; integral lipids in the CMC provide hydrophobicity, moisturization, stiffness. Removed by sunlight (oxidation), alcohol-based products, blow-drying, harsh-surfactant frequent washing. Replacing 18-MEA restores hydrophobicity and protects from further damage.
- **Chemical damage**: relaxers (alkaline) and permanent color (ammonia + H2O2) disrupt disulfides; curly hair has more cortical disulfides than straight. Relaxer misuse -> frizz, breakage, thinning, scalp irritation/damage, hair loss. Oxidized lipids = dry, dull; oxidized disulfides = weaker fiber.
- **Photo-aging**: dryness, higher porosity, reduced strength, rougher surface; lipid oxidation, disulfide cleavage, tryptophan degradation, cysteic acid formation; reduced water absorption, more brittle/stiff. Lightening accelerated by moisture. Melanin absorbs and dissipates UV as heat but is bleached; eumelanin more photostable than pheomelanin, so dark/black hair resists better than blond/light brown; protection limited to melanin-rich cortex.
- **Endogenous antioxidant defenses**: SOD, catalase, glutathione peroxidase; vitamins E, C, glutathione, ubiquinone — decline with age while radical production rises.
- **Evaluation methods**: microscopy, DSC, wet and dry combing, protein loss, tensile testing.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Daily cleansers should be mild, gentle, moisturizing; avoid harsh surfactants such as sodium lauryl sulfate; avoid alcohol-containing products that strip lipids.
- Apply heat-protectant before blow-drying; use medium, not high, settings.
- Use UV-protective products (shampoos, conditioners, leave-on sprays) or hats for brittle or color-treated hair; sunscreens also reduce dye fading.
- Gray coverage: demi-permanent color (no ammonia, less H2O2) is gentler but may not cover >50% gray; then permanent color is needed. Hair colorants count as anti-aging products since gray cannot yet be reversed.
- Pair coloring with after-color conditioners and ongoing color-care products.
- Anti-aging claims commonly framed as "N signs of aging" (e.g., breakage, split ends, frizz, unruly grays, lackluster color, thin look, dryness; volume loss, roughness, brittleness).
- Low-MW actives (peptides, AHAs, amino acids, ceramides, certain vegetable oils) can penetrate cuticle and scalp to benefit both shaft and root.
- Nutrition (vitamins A, B, minerals, protein; greens, orange/yellow fruit, whole grains, meat, soy) supports follicle health; ingestible products are part of the category.

## Mental Models
- Hair aging mirrors skin aging: same intrinsic/extrinsic split and oxidative-imbalance logic, so skin anti-aging toolkits (antioxidants, AHAs, peptides) translate.
- Lipids are the shaft's "moisture barrier": anything that strips or oxidizes them (UV, alcohol, harsh surfactants, heat) ages hair visibly.
- Melanin is a sacrificial photoprotector: it protects protein by being bleached, so lighter hair needs more external UV protection.
- Treat both ends: scalp/follicle care for future growth, shaft care for existing fiber.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating "the wrong end"**: focusing only on lengths while ignoring scalp moisture balance.
- **Harsh frequent washing / alcohol products**: strip cuticle lipids, causing dryness and dullness.
- **Chronic relaxer misuse**: breakage, thinning, scalp damage, hair loss.
- **High-heat styling without protection**: lipid loss and damage.
- **Stopping minoxidil**: benefits recur loss without long-term use.
