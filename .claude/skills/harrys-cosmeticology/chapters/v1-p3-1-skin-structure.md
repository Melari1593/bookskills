# Part 3.1: The Skin: Structure, Biochemistry, and Function
**Authors**: Editor Bozena B. Michniak-Kohn; contributors Amy S. Pappert, Philip Wertz, Nripen Sharma, Anna Langerveld, Ada Polla, Barbara Polla, Anne Pouillot, Karen E. Burke, Gopi Menon, Nava Dayan

## Core Idea
Skin is a multi-barrier border organ (permeability, antimicrobial, immune, antioxidant, UV) whose good condition is the formulator's core objective. The stratum corneum (SC) is only ~10% of skin thickness yet provides >80% of barrier function, and the outward flow of cells, water and lipids opposes any attempt to drive actives inward. Actives that never penetrate past the SC can still deliver real benefit by protecting and regenerating it; understanding structure, lipid/protein biochemistry, penetration routes, UV damage and gene expression underpins new ingredient design.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Brick-and-mortar / "smart composite" SC model**: corneocytes (bricks) embedded in lamellar intercellular lipids (mortar); later refined to a composite "smart" material (replacing the old passive "basket weave"/"Saran wrap" view).
  - When to use: reasoning about moisturization, barrier repair, penetration.
  - How: target the lipid mortar (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids), corneocyte NMF, and corneodesmosomes separately.
- **Four passive penetration pathways**: intercellular, transcellular, intrafollicular (transappendageal), polar pores.
  - When to use: choosing a delivery strategy for an active.
  - How: match molecule polarity/size/carrier to route (see Reference Tables); only molecules <500 Da diffuse passively.
- **Five liposome–skin interaction mechanisms**: (1) release of active which penetrates alone; (2) adsorption + fusion altering deeper SC intercellular ultrastructure; (3) direct transfer at SC surface / mixing with SC lipids; (4) intact penetration — only for deformable liposomes (transfersomes); (5) entry via appendages (sweat glands, follicles).
  - When to use: justifying or designing vesicular delivery claims.
- **Penetration vs. protection**: efficacy need not require deep penetration; protecting/regenerating the SC (e.g., with antioxidants) protects epidermis and dermis.
- **Interdependent skin barriers**: permeability, antimicrobial (AMPs), immune (Langerhans cells), oxidant (keratinocyte/sebocyte antioxidants), UV (melanin) — breach of one affects others.
- **Gene-expression (genomics) screening**: microarrays/qPCR as roadmap for mechanism, dose-response, ingredient combinations (additive, synergistic, hormetic).

## Key Concepts
- **Layers/thickness**: epidermis 0.05–1.5 mm (≈100 µm typical); dermis 0.3–3.0 mm (papillary + reticular); subcutis of lipocytes with fibrous septae.
- **Epidermal strata**: basale (germinativum) → spinosum → granulosum → lucidum (palms/soles only) → corneum. Basale + spinosum = Malpighian layer. Keratinocytes ≈95% of epidermal cells.
- **Turnover**: whole epidermis ~42 days; SC ~14 days; terminal differentiation 3–4 weeks. Psoriasis = abnormally fast transit; ichthyosis vulgaris = abnormal retention.
- **Other epidermal cells**: melanocytes and Merkel cells (basal); Langerhans cells (basal, spinous, granular; 3–5% of suprabasal cells).
- **Biomarker keratins**: K5/K14 basal; K1/K10 spinous. Type I acidic (K9–K20), type II basic (K1–K8); 40–70 kDa; keratins are type I/II dimers.
- **Lamellar bodies (Odland bodies, keratinosomes)**: 0.2–0.5 µm (100–500 nm) Golgi-derived organelles from upper spinosum; ~20% of cell volume in top granular cells; ~985 proteins; carry lipids, lipases, proteases, inhibitors, β-glucocerebrosidase, AMPs (β-defensins, cathelicidin). Considered secretory lysosomes ("exolysosomes"); form an interconnected network enabling rapid secretion after tape stripping; also build the acid mantle.
- **Cornified envelope**: Ca²⁺-triggered transglutaminase cross-links involucrin and loricrin (ε-(γ-glutamyl)lysine isopeptide bonds), plus disulfides; ω-hydroxyceramides ester-linked outside. Nucleus/organelles removed by caspases (esp. caspase 14). Granular→corneocyte transition in a few hours.
- **Filaggrin/NMF**: profilaggrin (histidine-rich, phosphorylated) → dephosphorylated, clipped to filaggrin → aggregates keratin into macrofibers → proteolysed to free amino acids = NMF (high osmotic strength). NMF ≈10% of corneocyte mass.
- **SC structure**: 10–15 cell layers (~10 µm). Lipids: ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids (plus cholesterol sulfate, esters, glucosylceramides). Intercellular lipid gaps ≈0.013 µm. Corneodesmosome proteins: desmocollin, desmoglein.
- **Lipid processing**: LB contents (phospholipids, sphingomyelin, glucosylceramides, cholesterol) converted by β-glucocerebrosidase, sphingomyelinase, phospholipase, sulfatase into ceramide–cholesterol–fatty acid lamellae. Elovl4 (very-long-chain FA for ceramides) and SCD are critical for barrier.
- **Rate-limiting enzymes**: acetyl-CoA carboxylase (fatty acids; biotin-dependent), serine palmitoyl transferase (sphingolipids), HMG-CoA reductase (cholesterol). Carbon source: circulating acetate.
- **Desquamation control**: cholesterol sulfate inhibits serine proteases (protects corneodesmosomes) until sterol sulfatase degrades it in outer SC; kallikreins degrade corneodesmosomes.
- **Water gradient**: basal layer 80–85% water → ~35% at granulosum/SC border; surface depends on environment.
- **TEWL**: influenced by epidermal water concentration, cellular integrity, RH, water diffusivity and SC thickness.
- **Melanin**: tyrosinase in melanosomes: tyrosine → DOPA → DOPAquinone; eumelanin (brown/black, indole-5,6-quinone poikilopolymer) vs pheomelanin (yellow/red, via cysteinyldopa). Melanocyte number is equal across skin colors; melanosome number/size/aggregation differ (dark skin: non-aggregated). 1 melanocyte : 36–40 keratinocytes (epidermal melanin unit). Highest density on face/genitalia, lowest on trunk.
- **Langerhans cells**: Birbeck granules (tennis-racquet), langerin (Ca-dependent mannose lectin); migrate to lymph nodes via CCL19; impaired by UVB; decline with age.
- **Dermis**: collagen 75% dry weight, 18–30% volume, 60–70 nm periodicity, rich in Gly/Hyp/Hyl; procollagen carries 300–400 extra amino acids per chain; cofactors vitamin C and copper. Elastin 4% dry weight/1% volume (desmosine unique). Reticulin 0.4%. GAGs: hyaluronic acid, chondroitin sulfate, dermatan sulfate. Mast cells (heparin, histamine).
- **Nerves**: ~1 million afferent fibers; Pacinian (pressure, up to 1 mm), Meissner (touch); sebaceous glands not innervated.
- **Eccrine sweat**: up to 600 glands/cm²; cholinergic; composition (mg%): Cl⁻ 320, Na⁺ 200, lactic acid 35, K⁺ 20, urea 15, NH₃ 5, Ca²⁺ 2, glucose 2, Mg²⁺ 1, amino acids 1, creatinine 0.3.
- **Apocrine glands**: axillae, anogenital, areola, ear canal, eyelids; adrenergic; odorless secretion, odor from bacterial action.
- **Sebaceous glands**: holocrine, absent on palms/soles/dorsum of feet; up to 400–900/cm² on scalp, face, chest, shoulders; cell→rupture ~14 days, cell volume rises up to 150×; androgen-driven (>5× increase at puberty), inhibited by estrogens/anti-androgens (cyproterone acetate). Sebum: triglycerides 57.5%, wax esters 26%, squalene 12%, free fatty acids 10%, minor cholesterol/esters. Epidermal lipids lack wax esters and squalene.
- **Hair**: color from melanosomes from bulb melanocytes; gray/white = fewer melanocytes. Hair and nails ("hard" keratin) lack keratohyalin granules.
- **Cytokines**: keratinocytes make IL-1, 3, 6, 7, 9, 10; IFN-α/β; TNF-α/β; G-CSF, M-CSF; growth factors (bFGF, EGF, KGF, NGF, TGF-α/β, PDGF). Low basal level, upregulated via toll-like receptors.
- **Energy**: spinosum mitochondrial; upper granulosum anaerobic glycolysis → lactic acid.
- **UV damage**: UVB mainly epidermis — direct DNA absorption, pyrimidine dimers ("signature" mutations), plus ROS; UVA reaches dermis — indirect ROS via chromophores, activates metalloproteinases degrading collagen/elastin → sagging/wrinkles. UV is a complete carcinogen (initiator + promoter) and immunosuppressive. UVA + PAHs (e.g., benzo[a]pyrene from combustion, cigarette smoke) synergistically increase oxidative damage.
- **Skin cancer stats (US)**: ~1 million NMSC/yr; 1 in 5 Americans lifetime; BCC ~75%, SCC ~20% (≈2% metastasize), melanoma ~5%. Actinic keratoses carry p53/p16 mutations.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Only molecules <500 Da passively cross the SC; the vehicle itself can facilitate penetration.
- Lipophilic low-MW actives go intercellular; hydrophilic actives prefer the polar pathway; large, poorly soluble molecules and nanoparticles use follicles (1–2% of skin surface, poorly developed SC, lead toward reticular dermis).
- Increasing fluidity of SC lipids facilitates penetration; higher lipid concentration gradient slows hydrophilic diffusion.
- True transcellular "nanochannel" routes may need microneedles or jet (gene-gun) devices.
- Sebum-modifying products: allow for the ~14-day sebocyte cycle before expecting effects.
- Measure TEWL and moisture when developing skin-care products; water loss links to aging and barrier status. TEWL rises with wounds, solvents, surfactants, extreme dryness; affected by humidity, temperature, season.
- Barrier products are good moisturizers, but not all moisturizers are barrier products.
- Use in vitro keratinocyte/reconstructed-skin genomics for early screening, then confirm in vivo — models lack melanocytes, Langerhans cells and a true barrier.
- Use gene expression to show mechanism/efficacy, streamline formulations, explore safety/toxicogenomics (requires immune-cell-containing models).
- Sunscreens (OTC) lower skin-cancer incidence, making photocarcinogenesis a cosmetic concern; actinic keratoses need medical treatment.

## Mental Models
- **Inside-out flow**: differentiation pushes debris, water and lipids outward — penetration fights this current unless molecules are designed to permeate.
- **SC as target, not obstacle**: 10% of thickness, >80% of barrier; protecting it protects everything beneath.
- **Barriers are coupled**: permeability recovery is faster in dark skin; barrier breach upregulates AMPs and alters Langerhans cells.
- **Genomics = hypothesis roadmap**, not proof of biological response.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming conventional liposomes penetrate intact**: generally accepted they do not; only deformable vesicles do.
- **Treating the SC as an inert "Saran wrap"**: it is a metabolically active, enzyme-laden smart composite.
- **Equating no penetration with no efficacy**: SC-level protection is demonstrable benefit.
- **Over-reading microarray data**: expression patterns don't directly equal biological response; follow up with functional assays and clinical work.
- **Relying on in vitro skin models alone**: missing cell types and barrier.
