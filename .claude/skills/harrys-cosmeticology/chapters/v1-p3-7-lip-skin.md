# Part 3.7: Substrate: The Lips — Lip Skin Structure and Function
**Authors**: Philip Wertz, Ph.D. (Dows Institute, University of Iowa)

## Core Idea
The red lip (vermilion zone) is a transitional tissue between facial skin and oral mucosa. It has a thin orthokeratinized epithelium over unusually flexible, highly vascular connective tissue. Its stratum corneum has the same lipid classes and ceramide profile as other skin, yet its barrier is weak and TEWL is high. Sebaceous follicles without hairs supply surface lipids. Lip shape changes clearly with age. The author notes that little research exists, which is an opening for lip-care innovation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Ceramide letter nomenclature**: the amide-linked fatty acid is N (normal), A (α-hydroxy) or O (ω-hydroxy). The long-chain base is S (sphingosine/dihydrosphingosine), P (phytosphingosine) or H (6-hydroxysphingosine). The prefix E marks an ester-linked fatty acid on the ω-OH (e.g., EOS). Example: α-hydroxy acid + phytosphingosine = ceramide AP.
  - When to use: specifying barrier-mimetic lipid blends for lip products and reading ceramide data.
  - How: all 9 types are present in vermilion SC (see table), in proportions similar to other skin.

## Key Concepts
- **Vermilion border vs vermilion zone**: the *border* is the line where facial skin meets the red lip. The whole red part is the *vermilion (zone)*. The terms are often confused.
- **Structure**: thin orthokeratinized epithelium on connective tissue that is more flexible than most skin. The rich blood supply gives the red color.
- **TEWL**: lip corneocytes have a slightly larger projected area than cheek corneocytes, yet lip TEWL is **higher** than cheek TEWL, so the barrier is weak.
- **Hydration map**: the move to non-keratinized internal epithelium is abrupt. The innermost vermilion strip ("submucosa") is surprisingly *less* hydrated than the rest. The upper lip is significantly more hydrated than the lower lip.
- **Sebaceous follicles**: sebaceous glands without terminal hairs along the vermilion border. Same lipid output as major skin glands. They surround every body orifice, which suggests a protective role.
- **Sebum composition (in gland lumen)**: 57% triglycerides, 25% wax monoesters, 15% squalene, 2% cholesterol esters, 1% cholesterol. On the surface, triglycerides partly hydrolyze to free fatty acids, some antimicrobial, most notably lauric acid (C12:0) and sapienic acid (C16:1Δ6).
- **SC lipids**: ceramides, cholesterol and fatty acids set the lip permeability barrier.
- **Aging**: sebum secretion falls with age; vermilion TEWL also falls; hydration is independent of age. The upper lip (nose base to lip) lengthens and the vermilion narrows.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Lips lose water easily. Moisturizers and lipsticks should supply occlusion or barrier lipids to make up for the weak barrier and high TEWL.
- Biomimetic lipid systems can follow the native composition: ceramides (NP-, NH- and AH-rich profile) + cholesterol + fatty acids, and sebum-like squalene, wax esters and triglycerides.
- Hydration is lower on the lower lip and on the inner strip of the vermilion. Target these zones and measure them separately in claims studies.
- Hydration does not decline with age, but sebum, TEWL and lip shape do. Anti-aging lip claims are more about volume/width (narrowing vermilion, lengthening upper lip) than about water.
- Products range from moisturizers to color cosmetics. The author sees understanding how lip skin differs from normal skin as the way forward.

## Mental Models
- **Transitional tissue**: lips are neither full skin nor mucosa. Expect skin-like lipid chemistry but a mucosa-like weak barrier.
- **Same lipids, different performance**: barrier strength depends on more than lipid composition (thin epithelium, corneocyte features).
- **Orifice guard**: sebaceous follicles around all orifices point to sebum's antimicrobial and protective role.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling the whole red lip the "vermilion border"**: the border is only the junction line.
- **Assuming lip hydration falls with age**: the text says hydration is age-independent; sebum and TEWL change.
- **Treating upper and lower lip as equivalent**: the upper lip is significantly more hydrated.
