# Part 2.4: Intellectual Property (IP) Issues – Patents and Trade Secrets
**Authors**: Charles Brumlik, J.D., Ph.D.

## Core Idea
Intangibles drive much of the profit and durable market share in cosmetics; IP is a property right over them that lets owners profit and exclude others, acting as a barrier to entry. Because IP is essentially a license to sue, it is only valuable if enforced or credibly threatened. Choosing the right IP type (patent, design, trade secret, trademark, trade dress, copyright, contracts) and filing at the right commercialization stage is critical, since rights are often lost through bad timing or careless disclosure.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **IP type selection**: match the asset to the right protection.
  - When to use: at each development/commercialization stage.
  - How: new molecule/formula/process/new use → utility patent; ornamental package shape → design patent/industrial design; secret formula, process, customer lists, B2B pricing → trade secret; brand name/logo → trademark; look of product/packaging → trade dress; labels, ads, bulletins, slogans layouts → copyright; partnerships → contractual IP clauses.
- **Patentability triad**: novelty (35 U.S.C. 102; EPC Art. 54), nonobviousness/inventive step (35 U.S.C. 103; EPC Art. 56), utility/industrial applicability (EPC Art. 57).
- **International filing routes**: national filings; PCT (WIPO; 146 countries in 2012; Taiwan not a member) for unified filing and search, then national phases; EPC/EPO (38 states in 2012) single application, search and examination, with designated-state grants; regional bodies (ARIPO 18, OAPI 16, EAPO, GCC, Latin American/Caribbean organizations); Madrid Protocol for trademarks.
- **Freedom to Operate (FTO/FTP)**: country-specific check of whether a product, process or name infringes valid third-party IP (patents on formula, ingredients, process; trademarks).
  - Remedies: design around the claims; prove the claim invalid; license the patent.
- **Partnering Do/Don't checklist**: for contract R&D, contract manufacturing, outsourcing, open innovation.
  - Do: file patents before the joint project; document prior/overlapping work with provable dates and authors; firewall outside confidential information from inventors working outside the project; know partner's patents, publications, partnerships and motivations; keep an IP docketing system; give notice before dropping a patent; know contract limits; maximize value to your company first, then the project.
  - Don't: unprotected sharing; disclose trade secrets unless sure they stay secret forever; publish before filing; publicize timelines/problems/IP practices; accept outside confidential information that could contaminate you.
- **Patent mining**: use patent literature as free technical/competitive intelligence; search by classification codes.

## Key Concepts
- **Patent**: territorial exclusive right traded for public disclosure; generally 20 years from filing; claims define the "fence" of protection; rights to exclude/royalties while maintenance fees are paid (some countries impose compulsory royalties).
- **Utility patent**: covers products (molecule, formulation, article such as aerosol can, point-of-use mixer, razor), processes (synthesis, compounding where step order/conditions are critical, oxygen-free packaging using oxygen scavengers, active-delivery method to blemishes), product-by-process (e.g., fermentation mixtures), new uses (e.g., birch bark extract to reduce wrinkles; BB cream for hair). Term 20 years from filing (+≤1 year from US provisional + office-delay adjustments). ~40% acceptance rate, claims usually narrowed. Number formats: issued US 8,377,427 B2 (7 digits); published application US 20130053342 A1 (year first); reissue prefix RE.
- **US provisional application**: up to 12 months before the regular application; placeholder for term; no enforcement value unless a regular application claims priority, issues, and its claims are fully supported by the provisional and infringed.
- **Design patent / industrial design**: protects only nonfunctional ornamental appearance (bottle and package shapes, textile designs); US term 14 years (14–15 years by country); <14% of patents; number format D676,963 (e.g., USD407982 Vaseline jar).
- **Plant patent**: invented/discovered and asexually reproduced distinct new plant variety (not tuber-propagated or found uncultivated); 20 years; one claim; format US PP 16988; negligible share.
- **Utility model ("patent-lite")**: device shape/structure; lower inventive step, lower fees, little examination; ~10 years; China, Japan, Korea and many others, not the US.
- **First-to-file (US)**: since March 16, 2013 (previously first-to-invent). US term 20 years from earliest non-provisional US filing (17 years from issue if filed before June 8, 1995).
- **Formulation obviousness**: US examiners combine references each disclosing a subset of ingredients to reject broad formula claims; once granted, combination patents are hard to invalidate (TSM teaching-suggestion-motivation test).
- **Publication**: application and PCT ISR become public 18 months after first filing; later office communications are published (USPTO PAIR, EPO Register).
- **File wrapper**: full prosecution history (claims edits, references, office actions); Public PAIR for published, Private PAIR for applicant's pending.
- **National specifics**: Japan — no patents on medical treatment of humans, post-grant opposition, utility models. China — no patents on mental rules, diagnosis/treatment methods, animal/plant varieties, nuclear-transformation substances; utility models; no opposition, only invalidation. India — excludes surgical (incl. cosmetic surgery) treatment methods; new forms/new uses of known substances unpatentable unless enhanced efficacy; pre-grant opposition within 6 months of publication, post-grant within 12 months of grant. EPC — opposition within 9 months of grant.
- **Unitary patent / Unified Patent Court**: unified protection across 25 member countries; court formed December 2012; system expected by 2014.
- **Trade secret**: no application or disclosure; unlimited duration while secret and reasonably protected; no novelty needed; does not stop independent development or reverse engineering; enforced via contract, tort/unfair competition, criminal law; if another patents it, owner may keep limited continued-use rights.
- **Trademark/service mark**: identifies source; ™/SM unregistered, ® registered; registration not mandatory; key test = likelihood of confusion; renew every 10 years, no expiry; first user beats first registrant in disputes; types incl. word, image, stylized, and rare sound/colour/smell (non-functional). US via USPTO by class; EU via OHIM.
- **Trademark classes relevant to cosmetics**: 1 (industrial chemicals), 3 (soaps, perfumery, essential oils, cosmetics, hair lotions, dentifrices), 5 (pharmaceutical, dietary supplements), 8 (razors, hand tools), 10 (medical apparatus), 21 (combs, sponges, brushes, containers), 44 (hygienic and beauty care services).
- **Trade dress**: limited right over product appearance/packaging/presentation; no registration; protected by courts if distinctive; varies by country.
- **Copyright**: original works (labels, package layouts, ads, bulletins); registration not mandatory until enforcement (US registration usually <$100); US term life + 70 years; corporate works 120 years from creation or 95 from publication, whichever is earlier; each right (reproduce, derive, distribute, display, perform) separately assignable in writing; Berne and Universal Copyright Conventions.
- **Infringement types**: direct (35 U.S.C. §271(a): make, use, offer, sell, import without authority); indirect (inducement; contributory = selling components especially made for the invention with no other use; requires a direct infringement); literal (every claim element present); doctrine of equivalents (same function, same way, same result); willful (up to 3x damages plus attorney fees).
- **Patent marking**: packaging must list utility/design patents and applications, or a website listing them; useful for competitive intelligence and authenticity.
- **TKDL (Traditional Knowledge Digital Library)**: documents Indian/Asian traditional remedies in examiner-readable form to block patents on non-original herbal "innovations".

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- File patent applications (or decide trade-secret status) before any publication, publicity or joint project.
- Use a US provisional to secure an early date, but ensure the regular application's final claims are fully supported by it.
- Choose patent vs trade secret: a formula that can be reverse-engineered is poorly protected as a secret; a hidden process can stay secret indefinitely.
- Control every copy of confidential information; use NDAs/CDAs; limit distribution; document compliance; be careful hiring from competitors (contamination risk).
- Before launch, run FTO in each target country covering formula, ingredients, process and trademarks.
- Check domain names against trademarks; verify rights for any content used (fair use unlikely in commercial settings).
- Search tools: USPTO, PATENTSCOPE (PCT), Espacenet (EPO), Patent Abstracts of Japan, Google Patents, FreePatentsOnline, Patent Lens, PatentBuddy, Pat2pdf; paid: STN, SciFinder, Delphion, PatBase, Questel Orbit, MicroPatent, TotalPatent. Trademarks: USPTO TESS, OHIM, JPO, Madrid, Trademarkia. Designs: Hague Express, RCD-Online.
- Classification codes: US 132 (toilet preparations/devices), 424 (drug, bio-affecting and body-treating compositions), 512 (perfume compositions), D28 (design: cosmetic products and toilet articles); IPC/ECLA A61K (medical, dental or toilet preparations) and A61Q (specific use of cosmetics).

## Mental Models
- IP is a license to sue: protection is worth only what you will enforce.
- Claims are a fence; design around fences rather than through them.
- Disclosure is irreversible: once a secret is out, it is usually lost.
- IP timing tracks commercialization; map each stage to the IP to formalize.

## Anti-patterns
- **Publishing or presenting before filing**: destroys novelty and trade secret status.
- **Unprotected sharing with partners**: IP ownership shifts and contamination arises.
- **Relying on provisional applications alone**: they have no enforcement value.
- **Expecting broad US formulation claims**: examiners combine prior art references; draft for defensible combinations.
- **Assuming one patent covers the world**: rights are territorial; file via PCT/EPC in target markets.
- **Continuing after an infringement notice**: risks willful infringement with treble damages.
