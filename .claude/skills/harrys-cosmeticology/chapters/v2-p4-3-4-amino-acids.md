# Part 4.3.4: Amino Acids
**Authors**: Bruce W. Gesslein (Ajinomoto North America)

## Core Idea
The 20 standard proteinogenic amino acids and their derivatives are multifunctional cosmetic ingredients: applied topically they penetrate the epidermis and hair shaft, act as humectants, antioxidants and free-radical scavengers, and can increase collagen production. Acylation, esterification, cyclization and polymerization turn them into mild surfactants, emollients, humectants, film formers and conditioners that preserve the skin's NMF and repair hair. The goal is mitigating the *appearance* of aging in skin and hair, not stopping aging.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Amino acid production routes**: fermentation (microbes fed sugars/syrups; 10–30 enzymatic steps; strains screened from soil with ~100 million microbes per gram; regulatory feedback must be released and pathway enzymes enhanced/blocked — e.g., boost enzymes a and b, delete c to accumulate C), enzymatic conversion (precursor → amino acid with 1–2 enzymes, no microbial growth), extraction (protein hydrolysis; yield limited by source composition). Fermentation wins on mass production at low cost.
- **Derivatization map**: acylate + neutralize → anionic surfactants (acyl glutamates); acylate + esterify → emollients; cyclize (dehydrate) → humectants (PCA from glutamic acid); polymerize + neutralize → polyaspartate (film former, humectant); acylate → functional powders (lauroyl lysine).
- **Aging-sign framework**: skin — wrinkling, elasticity, clarity, hydration, UV damage; hair — breakage, dullness, elasticity (bounce), roughness. Each sign is matched to amino-acid tools and a test method.
- **Hair tensile-damage test protocol**: baseline tensile → chemical treatment (ammoniated peroxide bleach; ammonium thioglycolate perm neutralized with sodium bromide) → shampoo with/without additive → compare to untreated (100%).

## Key Concepts
- **Amino acid**: molecule with amino and carboxylic acid groups; alpha amino acid = amino on the carbon adjacent to the carboxylate; side chain (R) differentiates.
- **Nonessential** (made in body): glycine, cysteine, alanine, tyrosine, arginine, proline, aspartic acid, serine, glutamic acid, asparagine, glutamine. **Essential**: methionine, tryptophan, threonine, phenylalanine, valine, lysine, leucine, histidine, isoleucine. >500 other amino acids exist.
- **MSG origin**: 1908, Prof. Ikeda isolated glutamic acid from seaweed/soy hydrolysate → first industrial amino-acid production.
- **Solubility curve breaks**: temperatures at which an amino acid gives up one mole of bound water.
- **Penetration**: shown by skin stripping (skin) and radio-tagging + amino acid analyzer (hair). Threonine, isoleucine, lysine reach 16 tape strippings; glycine, sodium glutamate, proline, threonine reach 10.
- **NMF composition** (Spier & Pascher): amino acids 40%, PCA 12%, lactates 12%, urea 7%, other (ammonia, uric acid, glucosamine, creatinine, citrates, salts, glucose, organic acids, peptides) ~28%. SC amino-acid content correlates linearly with hydration.
- **Atopic dermatitis**: every standard amino acid in skin is significantly reduced; metabolic conversion to metabolites is reduced in hyperkeratosis; topical amino acids help restore balance.
- **Free radicals / ROS**: singlet oxygen, superoxide, lipid peroxides, H₂O₂, hydroxyl radicals — the main antagonists of skin and hair.
- **AP-1 → collagenase → wrinkle cycle**: UV induces AP-1, which induces collagenase that degrades collagen; Zinc PCA interrupts AP-1.
- **Proline moisture retention**: Na-PCA and sodium lactate absorb most water at 65% RH (15 days), but L-proline retains the most at 35% RH; leucine and isoleucine form a lipid bilayer with water bound to proline that limits moisture loss.
- **Hair damage**: perming/bleaching lowers tensile strength, damages cuticle, leaches collagen, cholesterol, amino acids, fatty acids, ceramides; the cell membrane complex (CMC) "glues" cortex cells.
- **Look Good, Feel Better** (American Cancer Society): example of cosmetics helping patients in treatment.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- AHA stinging: raise pH from ~3 to 4.5 with arginine instead of NaOH or fatty amines, which reduce clarifying efficacy.
- Choose amino-acid surfactants (acyl glutamates, glycinates, alaninates) to reduce NMF loss during cleansing.
- Amino-acid emollients (e.g., cholesteryl/behenyl/octyldodecyl lauroyl glutamate) lower TEWL comparably to ceramide 3/petrolatum over 3 days after damage.
- Lauroyl lysine processing: pre-mix with NaOH pellets in water until clear (pH >13.3) before adding to the batch.
- Example formula targets: facial cream cleanser processed at 75 °C (add sodium myristoyl glutamate slowly); sebum-control cleanser pH 5.7 ± 0.2; cold-process cream cleanser pH 7.0 ± 0.2; facial moisturizer pH 5.5 ± 0.2 (all room temperature, xanthan hydrated first); light moisturizer pH 7.0; toner pH 6.0; conditioning shampoo heated to 75 °C for 15 min, add preservative at 50 °C, pH 5.5; natural conditioner pH 6.0 ± 0.2; leave-on conditioner emulsified at 60 °C, cocktail at 50 °C, EDTA at 45 °C; powder shampoo tumble-mixed.
- Lotion process: heat oil phase to 80 °C, polymer phases to 70 °C, neutralize carbomer with arginine (0.40%) after cooling.
- Typical use levels in examples: Na-PCA 1–2%, Zinc PCA 0.1–1%, glycine 0.36/proline 0.45/alanine 0.15%, amino acid cocktails 0.55–2%, ε-polylysine 1%.
- Test methods: tape stripping; Fudo rheometer (elongation to break, 25 °C, 40% RH); humectant uptake 65% RH / retention 35% RH over 15 + 15 days; bending strength (25 °C, 40% RH); dynamic friction coefficient; SEM of cuticle; sensory panel (n = 6).
- Keep amino-acid reactivity in mind when formulating (reaction tables in the chapter).

## Mental Models
- **Restore what's lost**: aged/dry/atopic skin and damaged hair are depleted of amino acids; replenishing them restores function.
- **Retention beats absorption**: the best humectant holds water under dry conditions (proline), not just absorbs it in humid air.
- **One building block, many functions**: the same amino acid can be active, pH adjuster, humectant or surfactant feedstock depending on derivatization.

## Anti-patterns
- **Neutralizing AHAs with NaOH/fatty amines to reduce sting**: also cuts clarifying efficacy; use arginine.
- **Harsh cleansing (soap, SLES)**: strips up to 70% of SC amino acids and 62% PCA.
- **Using amino acids as sunscreen substitutes**: they augment, not replace, sun protection.
- **Glycerin or butylene glycol for hair smoothness**: increased roughness vs Na-PCA.
