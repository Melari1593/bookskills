# Part 5.6: Epigenetics of Skin Aging
**Authors**: Rebecca James Gadberry (UCLA Extension, Cosmetic Sciences)

## Core Idea
The epigenome — chemical tags "on top of" the genome — decides when, where and how much each gene is expressed, playing genes "like fingers on piano keys". Epigenetic changes, more than DNA mutations, drive aging and disease in the second half of life, and unlike mutations they are reversible and responsive to environment, diet and topical ingredients. Epigenetics may be the unifying theory behind all aging theories. Virtually any ingredient that penetrates past the stratum corneum and changes gene expression is an epigenetic modifier, so formulators must treat ingredients as non-innocuous and research network effects carefully.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two primary epigenetic mechanisms**: (1) DNA methylation; (2) chromatin remodeling incl. histone modification. Emerging: non-coding RNAs (miRNA, siRNA, piRNA, lncRNA). They work in crosstalk as cellular memory.
  - When to use: classifying an "epigenetic" ingredient's mechanism (DNMT inhibition, HDAC/HAT modulation, sirtuin activation, Nrf2 up-regulation, miRNA).
- **"Everything good goes down, everything bad goes up"**: aging = decline of youth-maintaining genes (sirtuins, Nrf2) and rise of aging genes (NF-κB inflammatory family).
- **Ten questions for epigenetically active cosmetic ingredients**: (1) when to reverse a tag and how to know
  - (2) does it change gene/protein expression and visible/feelable skin
  - (3) different effects on different cell groups (each ages at its own rate)
  - (4) timing — day/night, season, cell cycle, gestation (teratogenicity)
  - (5) positive in one cell group, negative in another?
  - (6) off-target gene effects
  - (7) need to penetrate the stratum corneum?
  - (8) speed and durability of changes
  - (9) usage regimen — daily, pulse then stop, sporadic maintenance
  - (10) safety/toxicity testing required.
- **DNA methylation age clock**: age-associated methylation signatures correlate ~0.96 with chronological age; ~80% more accurate than telomere-length clocks; online calculators exist — potential biomarker for rejuvenation claims.
- **Unifying theory (blind men and the elephant)**: random damage, cross-linking, glycation, inflammation, programmed cell death, mutation accumulation, telomere shortening, free radical, mitochondrial, stem cell depletion, garbage catastrophe, membrane hypothesis — all rooted in epigenetic mechanisms.

## Key Concepts
- **Human Genome Project (completed 2003)**: expected 50,000–120,000 genes
  - Found ~20,500 (<2% of the genome)
  - One gene can code up to 1,000 proteins (250,000–1 million possible proteins)
  - >220 cell types share one genome
  - Too few mutations exist to explain disease variety, and environmental influences generally don't act via mutations. The other 98% ("junk DNA") is regulatory non-coding DNA.
- **Silencing**: in adult somatic/terminally differentiated cells (e.g., keratinocytes) ~98% of the genome is silenced (heterochromatic).
- **Speed and persistence**: epigenetic responses can occur within seconds and usually stop when the stimulus ends, but endocrine disruptors, poor nutrition or severe stress can entrench changes for life or generations.
- **DNA methylation**: DNMTs add methyl to cytosine at CpG sites (SAM donor → SAH)
  - DNMT1 (most abundant, maintenance during division), DNMT3A, DNMT3B (de novo). Methylated promoter hides site from transcription factors → gene silenced. Normal tissue: <3% of promoter CpG islands methylated
  - ~80% of scattered non-island CpG sites hypermethylated.
- **Aging methylation pattern**: sporadic hypermethylation of CpG islands (developmental, housekeeping, cell-cycle, apoptosis, metabolism, signaling, DNA repair genes) plus global hypomethylation of non-island sites — global hypomethylation is the most important feature of aging cells. Tumor suppressor gene silencing occurs in almost all cancers.
- **Chromatin**: DNA + nucleosomes (8 histones) + RNA; euchromatin (open, expressed) vs. heterochromatin (condensed, silent); telomeres are hypermethylated heterochromatin yet can be transcribed — chromatin "breathes".
- **Histone modifications**: acetylation by HATs opens chromatin
  - HDACs (≥18 in humans, incl. 7 sirtuins) close it
  - HAT/HDAC imbalance drives aging. Sirtuin targets: p53, FOXO (FOXO3a oxidative-stress protection), TAF1. Methylation (HMTs) stable and heritable but reversible by PAD4, LSD1, JHDMs/JMJDs. Phosphorylation, ubiquitination, sumoylation more transient. Remodelers: Polycomb (PcG), CDKs, NuRD complex (diminishes with age).
- **Longevity-associated genes (LAGs)**: 150 variants of 70 genes found in ~80% of centenarians; ~50 interaction partners in protein interaction networks.
- **Epigenetic drift**: progressive deregulation of marks with age; visible in identical twins (methylomes similar at 3 years, divergent at 50).
- **Skin aging features**: fine lines, dehydration, barrier damage, elasticity loss, dermal thinning, perioral/brow volume loss, hyperpigmentation, age spots
  - Underlying thinner epidermis, disorganized corneocytes, niche stem-cell depletion, less collagen, vascular changes, fewer Langerhans cells, melanocyte changes.
- **DNA repair (DDR)**: mismatch repair (MMR), base excision repair (BER), nucleotide excision repair (NER — intrastrand cross-links, UV CPDs). CPDs form most often at methylation sites
  - Repair may strip epigenetic tags. Heterochromatin protects DNA but blocks repair enzymes. DDR + p53 decide repair, senescence, apoptosis, immune surveillance, tanning.
- **Sirtuin interactome**: the 7 sirtuins are hubs connecting ~25% of the human interactome — meddling with hubs risks catastrophes as well as cures.
- **Skin-specific findings**: young (18–24) vs. old (70–75) epidermis — 75 genes where methylation reverses with age; sun exposure causes small but significant methylation changes in epidermis and dermis.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Many dietary epigenetic modulators may also act topically when combined with penetration enhancers or vehicles that improve absorption.
- Epigenetic activity claim: any ingredient shown to modulate gene expression qualifies; to remove doubt, identify its specific epigenetic mechanism.
- Consider timing (day/night, circadian, cell cycle) and regimen (continuous vs. pulse) for epigenetic actives; evaluate teratogenic risk during gestation.
- Use DNA methylation age as a potential efficacy biomarker; include proteomics (protein-level effects) — genes matter only through proteins.
- Caution from oncology: DNMT-degrading drugs (5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine) incorporate into the genome and can pass epigenetic mutations to daughter cells; DNMT1 shutdown risks widespread off-target effects — same caution applies to personal care.
- Nutrition is second only to environment in epigenetic impact: Jirtle's agouti mice — maternal B12, folic acid, choline, betaine methylated the agouti gene (brown, healthy offspring); BPA (polycarbonate, cosmetic containers, can liners) caused hypomethylation, counteracted by methyl-rich maternal diet.
- Read across disciplines (oncology, nutrition, psychology) — same epigenetic mechanisms apply.

## Mental Models
- Genome = piano, epigenome = pianist; ingredients can change how the music is played without changing the keys.
- Reversibility is the opportunity: epigenetic aging marks can in principle be reset, unlike mutations.
- Hubs bite back: modulating a hub gene/protein (e.g., sirtuins) ripples across the interactome.
- Personalization: each cell type and person has its own epigenome — future products target specific cell epigenomes rather than "one broad brush".

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming ingredients are innocuous**: nearly all penetrating ingredients have epigenetic effects.
- **Targeting one gene in isolation**: genes are network nodes; off-target consequences unknown.
- **Equating DNA mutation with aging cause**: HGP showed too few mutations to explain disease and aging.
- **Ignoring lifestyle in claims**: twin studies show smoking, sun, weight, HRT shift perceived age by years.
