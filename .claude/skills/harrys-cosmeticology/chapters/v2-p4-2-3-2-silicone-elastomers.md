# Part 4.2.3.2: Silicone Elastomer Applications
**Authors**: John Gormley (Grant Industries)

## Core Idea
Silicone elastomers are cross-linked dimethicone polymers forming 3-D networks whose interstitial pore volume (set by cross-link density) can be swollen with solvents or small actives. They deliver the sensory "wow" (silky texture) while functioning as line fillers, soft-focus particles, sebum absorbers/mattifiers, texture modifiers and controlled-release polymers. Unlike linear dimethicone, they swell rather than dissolve, leaving an invisible sponge after volatile solvents evaporate that soaks up sebum for long-lasting matte effect.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Cross-link density vs chain length tuning**: governs swelling, oil absorption and texture.
  - When to use: selecting or specifying an elastomer grade.
  - How: high cross-link density → hard, little expansion; light cross-linking → flexible, more absorbent but less refined texture; optimal balance → ideal expansion, oil absorption and smooth feel. Near-miss combinations cause pilling and powdery dry-out.
- **Solvent-exchange mattifying**: volatile carrier (typically cyclopentasiloxane) loosens sebum and evaporates, exchanging D5 for sebum within the elastomer, extending matte effect.
- **Pore-size matching for delivery**: match interstitial pore size to the active's size to load and release soluble actives.

## Key Concepts
- **Nomenclature**: silicone crosspolymers, dimethicone crosspolymers, dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymers, Polysilicone-11 (CTFA name).
- **Typical chemistry**: hydrogen-functional methicone sites on dimethicone cross-linked with terminal divinyl dimethicone or an organic diene, forming ladder-like rungs between chains. Less common: cross-linking pendant sulfur groups with TiO₂, ZnO or H₂O₂ catalysts, or tin-catalyzed PDMS cross-linking.
- **Modifications**: higher alkyl groups in place of methyl improve compatibility with hydrocarbons and alkyl esters; EO/PO copolyol groups confer self-emulsifying properties.
- **Particle size**: typically 5–50 microns — invisible on skin, yet large enough (nano-free) not to penetrate the dermis; described as ultra-mild, breathable, nonocclusive.
- **Linear dimethicone vs elastomer**: dimethicone reduces drag and forms a breathable barrier but is glossy and does not absorb sebum, so is not a mattifier; it dissolves in solvents whereas elastomers swell and keep semi-spherical shape.
- **Oil control**: sebum causes shine; T-zone (forehead, nose, chin) most affected; absorbents (talc, minerals, polymers) pick up oil. Silicone absorbents are colorless, odorless, oxidatively stable even heated, and don't promote UV free-radical formation.
- **Supply forms**: loose powders, swollen gels in cosmetic solvents, water dispersions, and pre-compounded anhydrous/aqueous "formula bases" that remove homogenization steps and emulsifier guesswork.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Choose solvent by skin type and desired volatility: cyclomethicones, dimethicones, alkanes, or compatible emollients.
- Volatile solvents leave an elastomer sponge on dry-down that continues absorbing sebum all day.
- Higher-end creams/lotions may contain a significant percentage of elastomer to reinforce brand-performance perception.
- Use pre-made formula bases to avoid later homogenization and to simplify emulsifier selection for stabilizing silicones.
- Elastomer + silicone powder can be emulsified into a smooth serum that still accepts oil- or water-soluble actives without losing texture.
- Mattifying primers are applied before makeup and many can be reapplied over makeup during the day.
- Watch for pilling during rub-in and powdery dry-out as signs of a suboptimal elastomer grade.

## Mental Models
- An elastomer is a sponge, not a solute: what goes in (solvent, sebum, active) can come out.
- Sensory elegance creates a consumer feedback loop that reinforces perceived efficacy and brand loyalty.
- Network density is the master variable: it trades absorption capacity against texture refinement.

## Anti-patterns
- **Expecting linear dimethicone to mattify**: it does not absorb sebum and looks glossy.
- **Mismatched cross-link density/chain length**: causes pilling on rubbing and visible powdery residue.
- **Over-cross-linked grades for oil control**: too rigid to swell and absorb.
