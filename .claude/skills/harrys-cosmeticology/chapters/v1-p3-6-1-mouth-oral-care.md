# Part 3.6.1: Substrate: The Mouth and Oral Care
**Authors**: Roger Ellwood (Colgate-Palmolive, Director of Clinical Research Europe) and Scott Harper (8th ed. author)

## Core Idea
Oral care products often deliver cosmetic and drug (OTC) benefits at once, so the formulator has to understand oral biochemistry and microbiology. Most consumer concerns come from dental deposits (pellicle → plaque biofilm → calculus) and from how plaque ecology and saliva interact with tooth mineral and gums. Effective products work on plaque mass and composition, mineral balance (fluoride, remineralization), nerve or tubule pathways (sensitivity), or chromogens and odorants (stain, breath).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Consumer need → underlying problem → functional solution matrix (Table 1)**:
  - When to use: deciding product positioning and whether a claim is cosmetic or drug.
  - How: cosmetic indications are bright smile (stain, deposits → stain removal/prevention, plaque/calculus reduction), fresh breath (malodor → antimicrobials, masking/neutralization), clean mouth, fewer uncomfortable dental visits, and dry mouth (lubricating rinses/gels, artificial saliva). Drug indications are strong teeth (decay → fluorides, remineralization, saliva stimulation, plaque acid reduction), healthy gums (gingivitis → plaque/gingivitis reduction; periodontitis is **not** an OTC indication), sensitive teeth (desensitization), and oral comfort (ulcers → topical anesthetics, antimicrobials).
- **Plaque as an ecological biofilm (succession model)**: Gram-positive aerobic early colonizers attach to the pellicle by specific binding. Over time plaque matures toward Gram-negative anaerobes at the gumline and between teeth (gum disease), while saliva-poor sites (pits, fissures, tight contacts) favor acid-tolerant acidogens (caries).
  - When to use: designing antiplaque or antigingivitis strategies.
  - How: aim at composition and pathogenicity as well as mass; hygiene and diet shift the ecology.
- **Caries control triad**: (1) reduce how often fermentable carbohydrate is eaten and clear debris faster; (2) reduce bacterial acid activity (fluoride, chlorhexidine, xylitol, sealants, flossing); (3) make the tooth less acid-susceptible (fluoride → fluorapatite, remineralization).
- **Hydrodynamic theory of dentin hypersensitivity**: open tubules carry fluid-pressure changes to pulpal nerves. There are two levers: occlude the tubules, or depolarize the nerves.
- **Four malodor strategies**: remove/wash away; mask (flavors); chemically bind/neutralize (metal ions, baking soda); prevent formation (antimicrobials).

## Key Concepts
- **Dentition**: 20 primary teeth (erupt 6 months–4 years) and 32 adult teeth (incisors from ~6 years; third molars ~20–21 years).
- **Enamel**: ~98% partially carbonated hydroxyapatite [3Ca3(PO4)2·Ca(OH)2], the hardest tissue in the body, made of crystallite rods perpendicular to the surface. Ion exchange: F⁻ and CO3²⁻ replace OH⁻; Zn²⁺, Sr²⁺, Mg²⁺ replace Ca²⁺. Fluoride substitution protects against acid.
- **Critical pH ≈ 5.5**: below it, tooth mineral dissolves. It is not absolute; it depends on acid amount, exposure time, Ca/PO4 in the fluid and other ions.
- **Dentin**: ~70% hydroxyapatite, the rest collagen. Perforated by fluid-filled tubules. More acid-soluble and decay-prone than enamel, so decay speeds up once it passes the enamel and exposed roots are vulnerable.
- **Gingiva**: healthy sulcus is ≤3 mm deep, firm and pink, ending just above the cemento-enamel junction (CEJ).
- **Saliva**: lubricates, clears, buffers (bicarbonate) and supplies Ca/PO4. Resting pH 6.5–7.2; stimulated up to ~8.0. Flow drops at night, so clean teeth before bed.
- **Stephan curve**: after sugar, plaque pH falls from ~6.8 to ~5 within minutes and recovers over 20–60 min. Restricted saliva prolongs the time below critical pH. Bacteria can drive pH to 5.5–<4.5.
- **GCF (gingival crevicular fluid)**: plasma-like exudate from the sulcus that feeds subgingival bacteria and carries host defenses.
- **Pellicle**: 1–3 µm film of proline-rich phosphoproteins and glycoproteins that forms within minutes of cleaning. It has no bacteria but contains bacterial enzymes (glucosyltransferase). It protects against erosion as a diffusion barrier.
- **Calculus (tartar)**: ~80% inorganic. Phases progress brushite (CaHPO4·2H2O) → octacalcium phosphate → whitlockite → apatite, getting harder over time. Contains ~400 ppm F. The other 20% is organic matrix. Forms by seeding, then crystal growth and maturation. Only a professional can remove it once formed.
- **Caries progression**: white-spot lesion (intact surface over demineralized subsurface, reversible) → cone-shaped lesion toward the dentino-enamel junction (still reversible) → cavitation (irreversible, needs restoration).
- **Cariogenic bacteria**: *Streptococcus mutans* and *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, which are acid-producing and acid-tolerant.
- **Gingivitis**: reversible inflammation linked to Gram-negative anaerobes (*Fusobacterium nucleatum*, *Prevotella intermedia*) producing butyric/propionic acid, H2S, methyl mercaptan and endotoxins.
- **Periodontitis**: irreversible bone loss and deep pockets. Linked to *Porphyromonas gingivalis* and *Bacteroides forsythus*. Episodic. Diagnosed and treated only by professionals.
- **Dental erosion**: acid dissolution not caused by plaque fermentation (dietary or intrinsic acid). Erosive potential is set by pH, titratable acidity/buffer capacity and calcium chelation. Swishing makes it worse, and abrasion/attrition remove the softened layer.
- **Hypersensitivity prevalence**: about 1 in 7 people.
- **Stain**: intrinsic (developmental, thin enamel, loss of vitality) vs extrinsic (chromogens from tea, coffee, tobacco; metal ions such as tin/iron; colorless precursors that later turn colored).
- **Xerostomia**: over 200 drugs reduce saliva flow (antidepressants, antihypertensives, antihistamines). Severe cases (Sjögren's, head/neck irradiation) cause rampant caries, candidiasis and loss of taste.
- **Aphthous ulcers**: 2–10 mm shallow craters with grey centers and raised borders, on the tongue and buccal mucosa. Last 5–10 days. Probably autoimmune. Triggers include minor injury, foods, and SLS in toothpaste.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Decide early whether each claim is cosmetic (whitening, breath, tartar) or drug (anticaries, antigingivitis, desensitizing, anesthetic). Anticaries fluoride products must follow the US OTC monograph.
- Do not make OTC claims for periodontitis. Antigingivitis products can be positioned as supportive after professional treatment.
- Tartar-control actives can only prevent or slow calculus. Removal is mechanical and professional, so claims should say "prevents/reduces buildup".
- Aggressive remineralization may speed up calculus formation. Check for this side effect.
- Smoker or whitening pastes: control abrasivity. For bleaches, check national rules (e.g., Canada prohibits peroxide oral products).
- Breath products: flavor masking also increases wash-out. The formulation challenge is to deliver more "reodorant" flavor and keep it in the mouth longer.
- Consider leaving SLS out of products for people prone to canker sores, since it is a suspected trigger.
- Clinical endpoints: plaque indices (whole tooth vs gum-margin vs interproximal, matched to the claim). Caries: DMFT/DMFS (uppercase = permanent, lowercase = primary teeth), historically many thousands of subjects over 2–3 years. Newer early-lesion methods: ICDAS, QLF (Inspektor), electrical impedance (CarieScan), red fluorescence (Diagnodent).
- Advice to include: cut snacking frequency, chew sugarless gum to stimulate saliva, use sealants and floss for saliva-poor sites, clean before bed.

## Mental Models
- **Demineralization vs remineralization balance**: decay only happens when acid magnitude × duration × frequency exceeds what saliva can repair. Products tip the balance (fluoride, Ca, saliva).
- **Pathogenicity ≠ mass**: plaque is everywhere. Disease depends on which organisms dominate, so ecology-shifting interventions matter.
- **Site-specific niches**: gumline/interproximal sites lead to anaerobes and gingivitis; fissures and tight contacts lead to acidogens and caries; the tongue holds a reservoir for malodor and recolonization.
- **Two-lever problems**: sensitivity (block tubules vs calm nerves); malodor (remove/mask vs neutralize/prevent).

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating plaque itself as the disease**: it is present in health too. Aim at the pathogenic shift.
- **Treating critical pH 5.5 as absolute**: it varies with Ca/PO4, exposure time and other ions.
- **Over-abrasive whitening pastes**: risk exceeding safe abrasivity.
- **Unregulated bleaching**: can harm teeth or soft tissue if misused; regulatory exposure.
- **Systemic herbal "breath" capsules**: no demonstrated benefit.
- **Cauterizing canker sores (silver nitrate, sulfuric acid)**: regarded as unsafe.
- **Snacking right before bed**: saliva protection is lowest during sleep.
