# Part 4.2.3.1: Silicones in Personal Care Products: Polydimethyl Siloxanes, Organosilicone Polymers, & Copolymers
**Authors**: Anthony J. O'Lenick, Jr. (Siltech LLC); Thomas O'Lenick, PhD (SurfaTech Corporation); Meyer R. Rosen (Interactive Consulting Inc.)

## Core Idea
"Silicone" is not one material but a family — silanes, linear and cyclic PDMS, organo-functional copolymers, resins and crosspolymers — whose value rests on two properties: insolubility in both water and oil, and very low surface tension (strong surface activity when hydrophilic/lipophilic groups are attached). Because silicones are perceived as not green, the authors argue they should be the "spice, not the meat and potatoes": used at minimal, efficient concentrations where no cheaper material delivers the effect, with efficiency verified in the actual formula. The term "silicone copolyol" is a misnomer for thousands of distinct PEG/PPG dimethicones.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Group opposites solubility scheme**: hydrophilic/hydrophobic, oleophilic/oleophobic, siliphilic/siliphobic — a hydrophobic material can be oil-loving or silicone-loving; HLB alone is insufficient once silicone is present.
  - When to use: predicting compatibility and interface behavior of silicone additives.
- **M/D/T/Q nomenclature**: M = monofunctional chain terminator; D = difunctional backbone; T and Q = cross-linkers. MDₙM denotes a fluid (e.g., MD₅₀M = 50 D units end-capped by M, made from MM + D4/D5). Reactive sites (M*, D*, T*; no Q*) placed as comb (pendant), terminal, or star.
  - How: construction (number/location of M, D, T, Q) + functionalization (what reacts at the * sites) define function.
- **Molecular weight → function ladder for dimethicone copolyols**: ~500 Da wetting; 2,500 emulsification; 10,000 conditioning; 50,000 waterproofing.
- **RF50 metric**: concentration of silicone surfactant needed to bring a surfactant solution to 25 dynes/cm — measures efficiency in mixed-surfactant systems where no CMC break exists.
- **Microemulsion rule for copolyol a:b ratio**: Ω = 0.17x + 1 (Ω = minimum D* units, x = number of D units) — choose the polymer that forms a microemulsion (thick, blue in water) for maximum interface delivery.
- **Water tolerance test**: dissolve 2.0 g copolyol in 100.0 g isopropanol, titrate DI water, record grams to first haze.
- **Rapid emulsifier kit screen**: 47% water / 47% oil / 5% emulsifier / 1% salt, both phases at 50 °C, mix 120 s, read stability at 3 and 24 h for O/W vs W/O.
- **Green Star Rating (GSR)** (US Patent Application 20090259409): Green Star Value × % actives summed over ingredients; steps — empirical formula, identify green portion, % weight green, green star value, optimize. Replacing a 20% ingredient GSR 1 → 7 adds (7−1)×0.20 = 1.2.

## Key Concepts
- **Surface tension (dynes/cm)**: water 72.6, squalane 46.2, mineral oil 33.1, dimethicone 20 cP 26.6, hexamethyldisiloxane 22.7, ethanol 22.2, D4 20.6. Low PDMS surface tension stems from flexible Si-O backbone, methyl groups preventing tight packing, and only dispersion forces.
- **Desirable silicone properties**: skinfeel, spread, film formation, dry feel; plus emulsification, foaming in aqueous and non-aqueous media, pigment dispersion.
- **Volatile silicones (cyclomethicone D4, D5, D6)**: heat of vaporization 31 cal/g vs water 539 and ethanol 210; dry feel from both volatility and surface activity in oils. D4 abandoned for safety concerns; D5 (and D6) replaced it but feel less dry; authors expect D5 to fade under consumer pressure.
- **Silicone fluids**: MDₙM homopolymers, 1–1,000,000 cSt; Newtonian (used as viscosity standards); viscosity rises with DP. Bimodal blends behave differently from monomodal fluids of same viscosity. Fluids ≤200 cSt soluble in anhydrous ethanol; ≥350 cSt insoluble.
- **Viscosity grades**: low 5–<50 cSt (5, 10, 20; D5 replacement candidates); standard 50–1,000 cSt; high 10,000–60,000 cSt; gums 100,000–1,000,000 cSt (all CAS 63148-62-9). Gums entangle above the molecular weight of entanglement.
- **Gum vs elastomer vs resin**: gum — linear, oily/sticky, no film, plasticizer; elastomer — lightly cross-linked, rubbery film, waterproofing (reversible elongation up to 1,000%); resin — heavily cross-linked, non-rubbery film that peels/crumbles. Cross-link density sets the boundary.
- **MQ resins**: M + Q only; cage structure forms when M:Q ≤ ~2:1; high M:Q = liquid; drying, plasticized with fluids, hydrocarbon soluble, effective at very low levels. **MDQ resins**: lower cross-link density, plastic or rubbery depending on Q:D.
- **Silicone crosspolymers**: silicone cross-linked with silicone (e.g., dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymer); more flexible than cubic SiO₂-like MQ networks.
- **Dimethicone copolyol (PEG/PPG dimethicone)**: pendant PEG/PPG on silicone backbone; higher a:b (silicone:water-soluble) → less water soluble. Lower MW wets faster (near-instant at 1.0%); wetting slows, with steeper slope above MW ~2,000. Lower MW also more eye-irritating.
- **PPG vs PEG**: one mole PO raises water tolerance as much as four moles EO; PPG-containing copolyol releases antiperspirant actives more efficiently.
- **Alkyl dimethicone**: silicone + oil-soluble groups; lowers oil surface tension (soybean oil ~32 → ~22 dynes/cm), making oils feel drier/silicone-like. Alkyl ≤C16 → liquid; ≥C18 → solid, raises melting point. More silicone → softer translucent wax; more alkyl → brittle opaque solid. ≥50% solid alkyl → translucent occlusive coating.
- **Critical gelation concentration (CGC)**: level at which alkyl silicone stops an oil from flowing; 10–20 wt% in 50 cSt silicone; 10% behenyl dimethicone solidified olive oil; solvent-specific.
- **Multi-domain alkyl silicones**: two alkyl groups (solid ≥C16 and liquid C8–C16) on one backbone → small confined solid/liquid domains: melting 34–38 °C, translucent, flows under shear vs blend at 56 °C, opaque, no flow.
- **Alkyl dimethicone copolyol (alkyl PEG/PPG dimethicone, e.g., lauryl PEG-8 dimethicone)**: hydrophobe + hydrophile on silicone → tunable O/W or W/O emulsifiers.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Use silicone only where it provides a benefit other polymer classes cannot, at the lowest effective dose; measure surface tension in the full formula — "there is simply no other alternative."
- In SLES systems prefer lower-MW dimethicone copolyols (bulky copolyols hydrogen-bond with SLES ethoxylates and reach the surface poorly).
- Match copolyol solubility to the formula, not to water: too soluble → washes down the drain; too insoluble → haze/instability; microemulsion-formers are optimal.
- D5 replacement: low-viscosity linear dimethicones, or a small amount of alkyl/ethyl methicone in triglycerides or dry esters (soybean oil, caprylic/capric triglyceride) — cheaper and greener; must stay nonflammable, non-defatting, cost effective.
- AP stick reference process: Phase B to 70 °C, add A, 75 °C; Phase C at 85 °C; combine under homogenization at 80 °C; add powders, cool; fragrance at 70 °C; pour at 65 °C; heat-treat surfaces.
- Fix lipstick syneresis: melt the unpigmented base — haze and a melt range ≥4 °C indicate incompatibility; correct alkyl silicones narrow the range to 1–2 °C and clarify; multi-domain types also alter cushion and color payoff.
- Determine CGC per solvent before formulating alkyl-silicone gels.
- Use the GSR to quantify trade-offs between green content and performance.

## Mental Models
- Silicone as spice: a small, efficient dose that drives perceptible performance, not a bulk vehicle.
- Effectiveness = getting to the interface: balance solubility (clarity) against insolubility (surface migration).
- Structure (M/D/T/Q placement) + functionalization (pendant groups) + MW predict function better than the INCI family name.
- Greenness is a measurable continuum, not a binary.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using "silicone copolyol" as if it were one material**: thousands of structures behave differently.
- **Extrapolating supplier pure-water data to formulas**: SLS/SLES interactions change efficiency dramatically.
- **Too-soluble copolyol**: expensive silicone stays in water and goes down the drain.
- **High-viscosity dimethicone in ethanol systems**: ≥350 cSt is insoluble in anhydrous ethanol.
- **D5 swap with flammable, defatting or expensive solvent**: unacceptable replacement.
- **Low-MW copolyol in eye-area products without checking**: higher eye irritation.
