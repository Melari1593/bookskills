# Part 2.3.1: Achieving Global Market Access – Focus on Russia
**Authors**: Ramzia Lefebvre (Intertek France, Government & Trade Services)

## Core Idea
Russia and the Customs Union (CU: Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan) form a fast-growing cosmetics market (demand +5%/yr or more; forecasts above 15%/yr to 2015, helped by lower duties after WTO accession). Since July 1, 2012, Technical Regulation TR TC 009/2011 replaced Russia's dual sanitary + GOST R system with a single conformity document: State Registration for 13 higher-risk categories, or a TR Declaration of Conformity for everything else. The rules are modelled on EU law but keep Russian specifics (third-party testing, animal testing, category-specific numeric limits, EAC mark), and implementation was still settling.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two-route conformity assessment (TR TC 009/2011)**:
  - When to use: any cosmetic/perfume entering the CU.
  - How: if product is in one of 13 high-risk categories → State Registration (manufacturer's name, CU-wide, unlimited validity, entered in unified CU register before import). Otherwise → CU TR Declaration of Conformity (in name of a CU-registered importer/buyer/distributor, tied to one contract and its duration). Both need testing at a CU-accredited lab before entry and Russian legalized translations.
- **Product grouping rule**: one document can cover several products if same manufacturer, same technical requirements, similar composition, hygienic specification and use; may differ only in insignificant ways (form, volume, colour or aroma from colourants/flavours).
- **Nine-pillar safety requirements**: composition (Appendices 1–5 regulated-ingredient lists), physico-chemical (App. 6), microbiological (App. 7; Art. 5 item 5), toxic elements (Art. 5 item 5), toxicological (App. 7), clinical (App. 9–10), production (Art. 5 item 7), consumer packaging (item 8), marking (item 9).

## Key Concepts
- **Customs Union**: created January 1, 2010; internal customs borders removed July 1, 2010; single economic area from January 1, 2012 (common tariffs, customs code, technical regulation and IP principles).
- **Decree no. 299 (28.05.2010)**: Unified Sanitary-epidemiological requirements of the CU; introduced CU State Registration.
- **TR TC 009/2011**: "Safety of perfumery and cosmetic products", approved 23.09.2011 by CU Commission Decree no. 799, in force July 1, 2012.
- **Pre-2012 system**: two stages — CU State Registration (fragrances: CU Sanitary Control) plus GOST R Declaration or Certificate of Conformity, with GOST R mark. Old documents valid until July 1, 2014.
- **CU definition of cosmetics**: substance/preparation applied to external body parts (epidermis, hair, nails, lips, external genitals), teeth and oral mucosa to clean, change appearance, perfume, correct odours, protect, keep in good condition and/or care.
- **13 State Registration categories**: self-tanning; skin whitening; tattoo cosmetics; personal hygiene products; skin protection against industrial hazards; children's cosmetics; hair colourants/bleaches/highlights; perms and straighteners; products using nanomaterials; hair removal; peelings; oral hygiene with >0.15% fluorine (>0.05% liquids); tooth bleaching with H2O2 (or releasing, e.g., carbamide/zinc peroxide) at 0.1–6.0% H2O2.
- **EAC mark**: EurAsian Conformity mark (CU Committee Decision N 711, 15.07.2011) replaces GOST R mark; placed after certification and before marketing; indelible; minimum size 5 mm.
- **Authorities**: RosPotrebnadzor (consumer rights, under Ministry of Health; RF decree no. 989, 27 Sept 2012) supervises application; Rossaccreditatsia (Federal Accreditation Service) controls proper application.
- **WTO accession**: accepted December 16, 2011 after 18 years; ratified August 2012; commits Russia to SPS and TBT agreements.
- **Category granularity**: 33 categories for pH, 3 micro groups, 21 toxicology categories, 23 clinical categories (including oral hygiene).

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Check if any product falls into the 13 high-risk categories; plan State Registration (portable across importers) vs Declaration (re-done per importer contract).
- Re-certify when product name, formulation or manufacturer changes.
- Test toxic elements when natural herbal or mineral raw materials exceed 1% of the formula: As ≤5.0 mg/kg, Hg ≤1.0 mg/kg, Pb ≤5.0 mg/kg.
- Formulate within category pH windows (e.g., face/body cream pH 5.0–9.0; liquid mascara/eyeliner pH 5.5–8.5).
- Expect zero-score requirements for skin irritation, mucosal action, clinical irritation and sensitization; in vitro general toxicity must be "none".
- Labeling (official language: Russian for Russia and Belarus; Russian + Kazakh for Kazakhstan): name; intended use if unclear; children's-product indication; manufacturer name and legal address incl. country; country of manufacture if different; claims-accepting representative/importer; nominal amount (except <5 g/<5 ml and samples; soap = nominal mass per bar); colour/tone for makeup; fluoride mass fraction (% or mg/kg or ppm) for oral care; manufacture date + shelf life, or "best before"/"use before" (month, year); storage if non-standard; warnings from Appendices 2–5; batch code; application method if misuse possible; ingredient list; ethanol % by volume. Hand-on-open-book symbol when info is in an accompanying leaflet.
- Keep animal testing data available: CU maintains animal testing, unlike the EU.
- Monitor amendments (Declaration issuance rules, State Registration forms, definition of "peeling", supporting test/labeling standards).

## Mental Models
- State Registration is a manufacturer asset; a Declaration is an importer asset. Choose distribution strategy accordingly.
- "Aligned with EU" ≠ "same as EU": lists are nearly identical, but numeric acceptance criteria and third-party testing add work.
- New regimes bring transition confusion; budget time for shifting interpretations.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming the new TR simplified everything**: experience (e.g., GOST R declaration in 2010) shows changes don't always simplify; it was premature to call it simpler.
- **Using one Declaration for multiple importers**: it is limited to one client and contract.
- **Shipping before assessment**: conformity must be done before entering CU territory; EAC mark only after certification.
- **Relying on EU dossiers alone**: CU requires local accredited testing and legalized Russian translations.

## Reference Tables
Example CU requirements (TR TC 009/2011):

| Parameter | Facial/body cream | Liquid mascara & eyeliner |
|---|---|---|
| pH | 5.0–9.0 | 5.5–8.5 |
| Total aerobic mesophilic + facultative anaerobic count | ≤10^3 CFU/g(ml) | ≤10^2 CFU/g(ml) |
| C. albicans, E. coli, S. aureus, P. aeruginosa | Absent in 0.1 g/ml | Absent in 0.5 g/ml |
| Skin irritation / mucosal action | 0 points | 0 points |
| In vitro general toxicity | None | None |
| Clinical irritation / sensitization | 0 points | 0 points |
| Toxic elements (if >1% natural herbal/mineral raw materials) | As 5.0, Hg 1.0, Pb 5.0 mg/kg | same |

Differences vs EU: mandatory third-party confirmation by CU-accredited labs/certification bodies; animal testing maintained; expiry and manufacture dates required; EAC mark; fluoride content declaration for oral care; numeric limits per product category (pH, micro, etc.).
