# Part 4.1.3: Hyaluronan (Hyaluronic Acid) – A Natural Moisturizer for Skin Care
**Authors**: Daniela Smejkalova, Gloria Huerta-Angeles, Tereza Ehlova (Contipro Pharma, Czech Republic)

## Core Idea
Hyaluronan (HA) is a linear polyanionic polysaccharide whose fixed carboxyl charges give it an enormous water-binding capacity. Its cosmetic behavior depends on molecular weight: large HA acts physically (film, viscosity, reduced TEWL), small fragments act biologically (penetration, signaling for new HA synthesis, fibroblast/angiogenic effects). Chemical derivatives (hydrophobized, cross-linked) extend residence time, robustness and delivery potential. The MW should be chosen for the intended use.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **MW-to-function selection rule**: the larger the MW, the more physical-chemical properties dominate; the smaller, the more biological properties dominate.
  - When to use: choosing HA grade(s) for a formula.
  - How: ~1 MDa for viscosity, film, lower TEWL; ~250 kDa for deeper hydration, texture and wrinkle reduction; ≤50 kDa as signal molecules for new HA synthesis and for hydrophobization/delivery.
- **Two chemical modification strategies**: (1) attach pendant groups to tune hydrophilicity (usually lower it to blend with hydrophobic cosmetic materials); (2) cross-link to make insoluble derivatives/hydrogels (almost all commercial HA products).
  - How: modify the carboxyl (receptor/hyaluronidase recognition site) via peptide-coupling activators, or hydroxyls (ethers via bis-epoxides, carbamoyl bonds, oxidation for lipid conjugation); prefer water as solvent.
- **DSC water-state analysis**: classify water as non-freezable bound, freezable bound and free; bound water = hydration number.
- **Hydrogel characterization**: swelling ratio (water capacity), compression tests (smoothness), rheology (non-Newtonian), shear modulus G (resistance to deformation).
- **MW analytics toolbox**: ESI-MS for short oligomers (≤40-mer); MALDI-TOF for polydisperse mixtures (not quantitative); SEC-MALS-RI for full MW distribution (two SEC columns in series to separate fragments).

## Key Concepts
- **Structure**: alternating β-1,4-linked units of β-1,3-linked glucuronic acid and N-acetyl-D-glucosamine (GlcNAc).
- **Water binding**: osmotic pressure from fixed negative carboxyl charges; HA in skin holds up to 1000× its weight in water.
- **Solution behavior**: crowded random coil physiologically; lower pH protonates carboxyls and raises viscosity; higher ionic strength shields charges, contracts the polyanion and lowers viscosity; viscosity depends on temperature, MW, concentration (overlap parameter).
- **Filtration**: 15 kDa–1.7 MDa solutions through 0.22 µm nylon (properly diluted) unchanged in MW, polydispersity, viscosity (unpublished data).
- **Weakness of native HA**: low enzymatic/radical resistance, rapid turnover, short half-life.
- **Fragment nomenclature**: by monosaccharide count (tetramer = 2 GlcA + 2 GlcNAc; 20-mer ≈ 4 kDa).
- **Cleavage routes**: enzymatic (selective; hyaluronate lyase gives unsaturated C4–C5 bond at non-reducing end; bovine testicular hyaluronidase (BTH) gives saturated oligos; both even-numbered with GlcNAc at reducing end) vs random non-enzymatic (radical, thermal, ultrasonic, acid/alkaline hydrolysis). Alkali removes reducing-end GlcNAc → odd-numbered oligos.
- **Degradation by-products**: alkaline → (E)-3-dehydroxy-3-en-D-GlcNAc/ManNAc stereoisomers; acid → furan-like and cyclopentanone derivatives (unwanted, e.g., carcinogenic potential); remove by ultrafiltration.
- **Fragment biology**: 6–20-saccharide oligos are pro-angiogenic, mitogenic for endothelial cells; fragments support fibroblast proliferation; <5 kDa oligos reduce wrinkles—stimulate epidermal HA synthesis and reduce pro-inflammatory interleukins in dermis; uses also in hair-loss compositions, fertility, cancer sensitization.
- **Skin HA distribution**: skin holds 50% of body HA; epidermal HA mostly extracellular in upper spinous/granular layers, intracellular in basal layer; dermis holds most and equilibrates with lymph/vasculature. With aging, epidermal HA disappears while dermal total stays constant.
- **Oral HA**: no skin benefit; skin cells cannot extract HA from blood.
- **Penetration**: >500 kDa does not penetrate, forms anti-moisture-loss film; <500 kDa claimed to permeate; 14C-HA 357 kDa (porcine ear, Franz cells, 37°C, 48 h) taken up by epidermis but hardly into dermis.
- **Humidity independence**: unlike glycerin, propylene glycol, sorbitol, PEG, HA retains water at low and high RH; classic humectants under xerosis can pull water from inner skin.
- **Hydration numbers (650 kDa)**: 7.6 non-freezable bound waters per disaccharide; up to 43 including freezable bound; 1 g HA holds 0.8–2 g water; binding independent of MW 30–750 kDa; 1.4 MDa ~0.12 g/g more.
- **Hydrogel rule**: low degree of substitution → low cross-link density → high swelling; high substitution → compact network, lower uptake, higher G.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- HA is water-soluble: add to the water phase.
- Use 0.01–0.2% in cosmetics; benefits claimed: fewer lines/wrinkles, less fragility, firmness, texture, smaller pores, brightness, better barrier.
- Negative charge → incompatible with cationics or proteins at certain concentrations.
- Products: serums, moisturizers, creams, shampoos, conditioners, bath oils; in hair, HA restructures keratin fibers, improves elasticity and color wash resistance.
- Combine high-MW HA with penetration enhancers to improve anti-aging penetration.
- Blend MWs: small fragments give fast early hydration; high MW gives sustained hydration over time (study: small best at 1 month, 1.66 MDa best at 3 months).
- Modify in water, not organic solvent: converting to acid form for DMSO/DMF/formamide drastically degrades HA. Expect some chain degradation in every modification.
- Coupling reagents for carboxyl: EDC, CDI, CDT, NHS, p-nitrophenol, p-nitrophenyl trifluoroacetate, ethyl chloroformate.
- After any derivatization, re-test physical, chemical and biological properties (including biocompatibility, biodegradability) and degradation.
- Purify non-enzymatic fragments by ultrafiltration to remove toxic by-products.
- In ESI-MS, tune cone voltage: too high creates charged-species artifacts; too low gives too few ions and odd-numbered artifacts.
- Unmodified HA enhanced skin partitioning of diclofenac and ibuprofen vs water, pectin and CMC; HA micelles/liposomes can >2× epidermal delivery of small hydrophobic drugs.
- Recommend "refilling" skin with topical HA from adolescence.
- Filtration through 0.22 µm nylon is acceptable for HA solutions when properly diluted (no MW/viscosity change); biological impact not yet established.
- For hydrogels, verify swelling ratio, compression (smoothness), rheology and shear modulus after cross-linking; expect higher substitution → stiffer, less swelling gel.
- Hydrophobized HA (amphiphilic) can self-assemble into micro/nano vehicles (liposome-, microemulsion-, micelle-like) to stabilize poorly soluble lipophilic actives and nutrients.

## Mental Models
- Size decides mode: big HA = physical humectant film; small HA = biological signal.
- Hydration is time-dependent: fast penetration wins early, barrier film wins long-term.
- Every modification trades native properties for durability; preserve chain length because viscosity drives lubrication/space-filling.
- Charge governs everything: pH, salt and cationics change conformation and compatibility.

## Anti-patterns
- **Relying on oral HA for skin**: no benefit.
- **Expecting >500 kDa HA to penetrate**: it stays on the surface.
- **Organic-solvent modification via acid form**: severe degradation.
- **Skipping by-product removal after acid/alkaline cleavage**: unwanted bioactive impurities.
- **Mixing HA with cationics/proteins carelessly**: incompatibility.
- **Assuming MALDI-TOF is quantitative**: desorption varies with fragment size.
