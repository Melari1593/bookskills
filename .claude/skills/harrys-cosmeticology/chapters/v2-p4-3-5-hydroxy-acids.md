# Part 4.3.5: AHAs and Beyond — Anti-Aging Ingredients and Their Benefits for All Layers of the Skin
**Authors**: Ronni L. Weinkauf (chapter editor), Peter Konish, Stacy S. Hawkins, Uma Santhanam (NeoStrata, Unilever R&D, Avon)

## Core Idea
Alpha-hydroxy acids (AHAs, e.g., glycolic acid) are multimechanistic anti-aging actives acting on every skin layer: stratum corneum desquamation, epidermal proliferation, dermal matrix (collagen, GAGs) synthesis and melanin reduction, proven in vehicle-controlled trials at 4–10%. Second-generation polyhydroxy acids (PHAs) and third-generation bionic acids (BAs) give comparable benefits with better tolerance plus barrier, antioxidant and MMP-inhibiting effects; N-acetylamino sugars and N-acetylamino acids extend the family to neutral pH. Efficacy depends on free-acid availability (pH vs pKa), so low-pH formulation must balance efficacy, irritation and emulsion stability.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Hydroxy-acid generations**: 1st AHAs (glycolic, lactic, citric, mandelic) → 2nd PHAs/aldonic acids (gluconolactone) → 3rd bionic/aldobionic acids (lactobionic, maltobionic) → N-acetylamino sugars (NAG) → N-acetylamino acids (N-acetyltyrosinamide).
  - When to use: choosing an active by skin sensitivity and pH constraints.
  - How: move to later generations for sensitive, rosacea, eczema, atopic or shaving-prone male skin, or to combine with low-pH-sensitive actives.
- **pH/pKa free-acid rule**: only the undissociated free acid is immediately bioavailable. At pH = pKa, ~50% is free acid; above pKa the free fraction quickly drops below 50%; below pKa it rises sharply.
  - How: 10% glycolic acid (pKa 3.8) at pH 3.8 = 5% free acid + 5% salt. Typical AHA products sit at pH 3.5–4.5.
- **Amphoteric complex**: complex the AHA with arginine or ornithine at a specific molar ratio → temporary complex gives slow acid release, adjusts pH, reduces stinging without losing efficacy.
- **Clinical models**: photo-damaged forearm (bilateral paired, 12 weeks, 0.5–1.0 g twice daily, n~20/cell, women 45–70, crepe-like grade 4–6 on 0–9 scale; directed difference 0–4) and split-face (women 30–70, up to 12 weeks, fewer subjects needed than monadic 6-month studies). Photo-numeric 0–9 scales (0 none, 1–3 mild, 4–6 moderate, 7–9 severe, ½-point steps); physician global 0–3.
- **Stability monitoring triad**: microscope (particle size and distribution), viscosity at each time point (continuing drop = instability, especially at elevated temperature), pH drift (rise = less efficacy; fall = irritation/instability) → buffer with citric acid/sodium citrate.

## Key Concepts
- **AHA structure**: carboxylic acid with OH on the alpha carbon; "fruit acids."
- **Glycolic acid** (2-hydroxyethanoic): smallest AHA, ~76 Da, pKa 3.8 (~1 unit below acetic acid), from sugar cane.
- **Lactic acid** (2-hydroxypropanoic): 3-carbon, from milk, endogenous (pyruvate via lactate dehydrogenase); chiral (D/L).
- **Citric acid**: polycarboxylic; both an AHA and a BHA; Krebs cycle; antioxidant.
- **Mandelic acid** (phenylglycolic): aromatic, lipophilic AHA — affinity for sebum; oily/acne-prone skin.
- **History**: Van Scott & Yu, 1970s — AHAs normalized hyperkeratotic conditions (ichthyosis); 25% glycolic reversed photo-aging markers on forearms; milk (lactic) acids reputedly used in Cleopatra's era.
- **SC mechanism**: AHAs weaken corneocyte cohesion by degrading corneosomes (desmosomes) in the stratum disjunctum only (stratum compactum unaffected); 4% glycolic pH 3.8 left lamellar bodies and lipid lamellae normal and TEWL unchanged. Hypotheses: ionic-bond disruption, inhibition of sulfate/phosphotransferases, acidification activating proteases (kallikreins, esp. KLK-7; cathepsin D-like protease with 50% at pH 0.9); 70% glycolic in mice disrupts calcium gradient (chelation). Lactic acid stimulates ceramide synthesis.
- **Epidermis**: increased viable epidermal thickness (12% ammonium lactate; 5% or 12% lactic twice daily 3 months; glycolic 3 weeks); glycolic and L-lactic are mitogenic to keratinocytes.
- **Dermis**: ↑ GAGs, collagen, dermal thickness. 12% lactic → epidermal + dermal firmness/thickness; 5% lactic → only surface/epidermal changes. 4% glycolic pH 3.8, 3 weeks semi-occluded: 4/10 ↑ VET and GAGs, 7/10 ↑ total collagen, 5–7/10 ↑ pro-collagen I. 20% glycolic 3 months → ↑ collagen gene expression and HA; 20% citric → ↑ VET and GAGs; 25% glycolic/lactic/citric ~6 months → 25% thicker skin vs placebo.
- **In-vitro mechanisms**: dose-dependent pro-collagen I in fibroblasts (glycolic > malic); keratinocyte IL-1α → fibroblast MMP-1/MMP-3 and IL-6 (remodeling); lactic 1.5–3% ↑ VEGF; AHAs stimulate Krebs cycle and O₂ uptake (more ATP); 8% lactic lowers transcutaneous O₂ in 1.5 h; transient intracellular acidification modulates ERK/JNK/p38; TRPV1 (and TRPV3) activation drives proliferation and also likely stinging.
- **Non-inflammatory remodeling**: collagen gains occur without dermal inflammatory infiltrate — direct fibroblast action, not a chemical-burn wound response.
- **Melanocytes**: AHAs reduce melanin dose-dependently (likely tyrosinase inhibition) plus faster turnover disperses pigment → melasma, lentigines, PIH.
- **PHAs**: multiple OH groups, can form lactones (acid/lactone equilibrium depends on pH); hygroscopic; antioxidant (prevent air oxidation of anthralin, hydroquinone, banana peel); chelate metals → inhibit MMPs. Gluconolactone (from corn sugar; pentose phosphate pathway).
- **Bionic acids**: monosaccharide + aldonic acid (~358 Da). Lactobionic = gluconic acid + galactose (milk sugar); maltobionic = gluconic acid + glucose (corn). Form clear water-rich gel on evaporation (maltobionic 29% water, lactobionic 14%); strong chelators, MMP inhibitors; maltobionic reduces lipid peroxidation.
- **SC pH**: higher in impaired/inflamed skin; lactobionic acid and gluconolactone lower SC pH → better barrier via β-glucocerebrosidase and acid sphingomyelinase.
- **N-acetylglucosamine (NAG)**: neutral amino sugar; component of GAGs and HA (HA = ~50,000 disaccharide units of NAG + D-glucuronic acid); chitin monomer; stimulates GAGs; inhibits tyrosinase glycosylation (needed to activate tyrosinase); effective at neutral pH.
- **N-acetylamino acids**: AHAs and amino acids are structural analogs (glycolic ↔ glycine). N-acetylcysteine (antioxidant, pro-drug of cysteine → glutathione); N-acetylproline / N-acetylhydroxyproline → collagen amino acids; N-acetylhydroxyproline improves barrier; N-acetyltyrosinamide ↑ HA, collagen I, elastin in fibroblasts.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Know the acid pKa and formulate pH 3.5–4.5 (≈ pKa for glycolic 3.8) for efficacy; complex with arginine/ornithine to reduce sting.
- Low pH excludes many traditional ingredients; neutralization salts can exceed electrolyte tolerance.
- Thickeners for low pH: smectite clays (house-of-cards; hydrate fully in water before adding acids/electrolytes; salts raise viscosity/yield to a limit; thixotropic); pre-neutralized polyacrylic "Hydro Swellable Droplet" polymers (instant inversion, broad pH, moderate electrolyte tolerance — combine with others in high salt); acid-swellable associative thickeners (protonated acrylic amines swell with acid); xanthan, cellulose ethers, starches, guar as auxiliary stabilizers.
- Emollients: avoid small/medium-chain linear esters (hydrolysis at low pH and heat → color shift, malodor, also in fragrance); use long-chain branched esters, silicones, sesame/olive/jojoba oils (watch color shift), hydrogenated polydecene, isohexadecane, isododecane.
- Emulsifiers: nonionic low + high HLB pair with long-chain fatty alcohol for high-temperature stability; run an HLB study; check droplet size under microscope before stability. Cationic emulsifiers work across pH, are electrolyte tolerant and substantive on dry skin.
- W/Si and W/O: salt (NaCl or MgSO₄) usually added at 0.5–1%, but if AHA neutralization already generates much salt, more salt can destabilize.
- NAG and similar neutral actives allow combination with low-pH-sensitive ingredients (e.g., retinol) in one product.
- Regimen design: 16-week study (69 women, 45–65) — AM gluconolactone/maltobionic cleanser, NAG eye cream, SPF20 day cream with NAG/retinol; PM night cream glycolic/gluconolactone/maltobionic — beat vehicle on all measures. Two-step deep-wrinkle regimen (70 women, 16 wk): Step 1 N-acetyltyrosinamide + N-acetylhydroxyproline + low glycolic; Step 2 NAG + triethyl citrate + palmitoyl oligo/tetrapeptides → nasolabial 10.3% vs 0.9%, glabellar 7.7% vs 1.3%, under-eye 20.3% vs 5.8%, crow's feet 17.8% vs 7.9%.
- Claim-support tools: ballistometry (Diastron; damping coefficient α; FFT filtering + genetic algorithm), 3D Cyberware 3030/HIREZ scanning, dansyl chloride turnover assay, pinch recoil, digital calipers, ultrasound, OCT, silicone replicas, consumer before/after photo assessment.

## Mental Models
- **Bioavailable fraction, not label %**: efficacy tracks free acid = f(concentration, pH − pKa).
- **Efficacy–irritation trade-off is tunable**: slow release (amphoteric complex) or larger molecules (PHA/BA) keep benefit while cutting sting.
- **Treat all layers**: surface exfoliation, epidermal proliferation and dermal matrix synthesis add up; one mechanism alone undersells AHAs.
- **Multi-active regimens**: combining AHA, PHA, BA, NAG and retinol across AM/PM products gives synergy and tolerance.

## Anti-patterns
- **Over-neutralizing to cut sting**: raises pH above pKa and loses free acid.
- **Adding acids before clays hydrate**: smectite won't build full viscosity/yield.
- **Short linear esters or ester-rich fragrances at low pH**: hydrolysis, color shift, malodor.
- **Adding extra salt to W/O or W/Si AHA emulsions**: neutralization salt may already destabilize.
- **Assuming AHA collagen gains are just peel-induced wounding**: evidence shows direct fibroblast stimulation without inflammation.
