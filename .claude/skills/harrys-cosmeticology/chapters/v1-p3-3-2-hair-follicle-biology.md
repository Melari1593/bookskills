# Part 3.3.2: An Overview of Hair Follicle Anatomy and Biology
**Authors**: Paul Mouser, Ph.D. (Ashland Specialty Ingredients)

## Core Idea
The hair shaft is dead keratinocytes, but the follicle that makes it is a dynamic, regenerating mini-organ. Its cycle (anagen, catagen, telogen, plus exogen and kenogen) is driven by dermal papilla–epithelium signaling, and disturbance of this cycle underlies most hair-growth disorders, including androgenetic alopecia. Follicles also age (graying, thinning, slower growth) partly via oxidative stress, so cosmetic "biofunctionals" that target the follicle are a new route beyond fiber treatments.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Permanent vs cycling follicle anatomy**: upper permanent part (infundibulum = canal opening to skin surface down to sebaceous duct; isthmus = sebaceous duct to arrector pili insertion, contains the bulge) vs lower cycling part (suprabulbar region + bulb), remodeled every cycle.
  - When to use: deciding where an active must act (stem cells in bulge vs matrix/papilla in bulb).
- **Hair cycle with five phases**: anagen (growth/differentiation), catagen (regression/apoptosis), telogen (rest), exogen (active, controlled shedding of club fiber), kenogen (empty follicle before new anagen).
  - How: cycling is driven by continuous dermal papilla–epithelial molecular interactions; clock mechanism unknown (circadian clock a candidate).
- **Therapeutic strategy triad for hair loss**: inhibit catagen to prolong anagen; induce anagen in telogen follicles; inhibit exogen.
  - When to use: framing claims and screening targets for anti-hair-loss actives.
- **Dermal papilla as "command center"/"conductor of the orchestra"**: dictates bulb size, shaft diameter, length and anagen duration; source of growth factors.

## Key Concepts
- **Follicle types**: lanugo (very fine, shed in utero or first weeks of life); vellus (very short, nonpigmented, nonmedullated, body-wide); terminal (large, usually pigmented and medullated, mainly scalp).
- **Numbers**: ~5 million follicles in humans, 80,000-150,000 on the scalp; scalp hair grows 0.3-0.4 mm/day; no new follicles after birth (size can change, mainly via androgens). Normal shedding 100-150 hairs/day.
- **Androgen paradox**: androgens convert vellus to terminal in pubic, axillary and beard regions, but in frontal scalp of predisposed people trigger miniaturization (terminal -> vellus).
- **Bulge**: stem-cell reservoir in lower isthmus of the ORS, below the sebaceous gland at arrector pili insertion; all follicle epithelial layers derive from bulge cells. Human bulge not morphologically distinct; defined by unique markers. Also holds melanocyte reserve.
- **Bulb (matrix zone)**: one of the fastest-dividing tissues; matrix keratinocytes + melanocytes; matrix cells differentiate into hair shaft, IRS and companion layer.
- **Dermal papilla growth factors**: keratinocyte growth factor, bone morphogenetic protein, hepatocyte growth factor, IGF, stem cell factor. Surgical removal of papilla + lower dermal sheath stops hair growth. IGF-1 and bFGF made by papilla, receptors on matrix cells.
- **Balding papilla**: higher androgen receptor and type II 5-alpha reductase (testosterone -> DHT); DHT induces papilla to secrete TGF-b1 and DKK-1, which inhibit keratinocyte growth.
- **Hair shaft**: trichocytes (terminally differentiated keratinocytes); cuticle (shingle-like, pointing outward/upward), cortex (hair keratins + keratin-associated proteins + melanin), medulla (large loose cells with air spaces).
- **IRS**: three layers — IRS cuticle (anchors shaft), Huxley's layer, Henle's layer; keratins and trichohyalin act as intracellular cement to mold and guide the shaft.
- **ORS**: continuous with epidermal basal layer; forms directly from bulge (not from matrix); stem cells migrate down the ORS to the matrix.
- **Arrector pili muscle**: adrenergic control; contracts in cold or emotional stress (hair stands up); follicles grow at an oblique angle.
- **Anagen duration**: scalp 2-6 years (long hair); eyebrow/eyelash 1-3 months (short hair).
- **Catagen**: ~2 weeks; apoptosis of lower follicle, loses sheaths, club fiber forms, papilla condenses and moves up beneath bulge, melanogenesis stops. Triggered by fall in IGF-1, bFGF, VEGF and rise in TGF-b1, IL-1a, TNF-a.
- **Telogen**: ~3 months average; follicle ~half previous size; stem cells quiescent until activated by Wnt and TGF-b signals from the papilla.
- **Exogen**: active, highly controlled (proteolytic) shedding; previously thought passive.
- **Kenogen**: empty follicle; seen in healthy skin but more frequent and longer in androgenetic alopecia.
- **Anagen angiogenesis**: perifollicular vascularization rises, with VEGF mRNA from ORS keratinocytes.
- **Androgenetic alopecia cycle changes**: fewer follicles in anagen, shorter anagen, more and longer telogen, exogen shifts from early anagen to telogen -> thinning coverage. Follicles cycle until death or miniaturization to vellus.
- **Follicle aging**: loss of pigmentation, lower follicle density, more telogen hairs, smaller shaft diameter, slower growth.
- **Pigmentation**: eumelanin (brown-black) and pheomelanin (red-blond) made in melanosomes by melanocytes; tyrosinase is the rate-limiting enzyme; melanosomes move to dendrite tips and transfer to cortex/medulla keratinocytes.
- **Graying (canities) mechanisms proposed**: pigmentary machinery failure, melanocyte apoptosis, anagen defects, loss/failed differentiation of melanocyte stem cells, migration defects.
- **Oxidative stress on follicle**: repeated UVB on cultured follicles reduced shaft elongation and caused premature catagen; low UVB doses caused oxidative DNA damage; H2O2 on scalp grafts caused vacuoles and edema mostly in ORS. ROS reach millimolar levels in gray/white follicles; catalase expression lower in unpigmented and older follicles.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Anti-hair-loss actives should aim to prolong anagen, re-induce anagen in telogen follicles, or inhibit exogen; anti-androgen pathway relevance: AR, 5-alpha reductase type II, DHT-induced TGF-b1 and DKK-1.
- Anagen-supporting signals to protect or mimic: IGF-1, bFGF, VEGF; catagen promoters to counter: TGF-b1, IL-1a, TNF-a.
- Follicle-protecting antioxidant/anti-ROS ingredients are justified by data showing the follicle is a direct target of UV and oxidative stress.
- Test models named: isolated hair follicle organ culture (ex vivo), human scalp skin grafts, in vitro cell culture.
- Premature graying definition for claims/consumer framing: onset before 20 years (Caucasians), 25 (Asians), 30 (Africans).

## Mental Models
- "Healthy hair starts at the root": the shaft is the output of a factory; shaft properties (diameter, length, color) are set by papilla signaling during anagen.
- Hair loss is a timing problem: it is mostly a shift in phase proportions and durations, not just fibers falling out.
- Signal balance: anagen maintenance factors vs apoptosis-promoting cytokines decide when a follicle regresses.
- Oxidative load accumulates with age and defenses (catalase) fall, so graying is partly an oxidative-damage phenotype.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating only the fiber**: traditional formulas improve appearance but do not address follicle-level causes of thinning, graying or slow growth.
- **Assuming shedding is passive**: exogen is an active, regulated process and therefore a target.
- **Assuming new follicles can form**: follicle count is fixed after birth; strategies must reactivate or prevent miniaturization of existing follicles.

## Reference Tables
| Phase | Duration | Key events |
|---|---|---|
| Anagen | 2-6 y scalp; 1-3 mo brows/lashes | Matrix proliferation, shaft + IRS production, angiogenesis (VEGF) |
| Catagen | ~2 weeks | Apoptosis, club fiber, papilla moves up, melanogenesis stops |
| Telogen | ~3 months | Rest; stem cells quiescent until Wnt/TGF-b activation |
| Exogen | around new anagen onset | Active, protease-mediated shedding of club fiber |
| Kenogen | variable | Empty follicle; increased in androgenetic alopecia |

| Component | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Infundibulum | Skin surface to sebaceous duct | Canal opening (permanent) |
| Isthmus / bulge | Sebaceous duct to arrector pili | Stem cell + melanocyte reservoir (permanent) |
| Bulb / matrix | Base | Shaft factory, melanocytes (cycling) |
| Dermal papilla | Inside bulb | Signaling center, growth factors |
| IRS (cuticle, Huxley, Henle) | Around shaft | Anchors, molds, guides shaft |
| ORS | Outermost, continuous with epidermis | Derived from bulge; VEGF source |

| Signal | Source / target | Effect on cycle |
|---|---|---|
| IGF-1, bFGF | Dermal papilla -> matrix receptors | Maintain anagen; decline triggers catagen |
| VEGF | ORS keratinocytes | Anagen angiogenesis; decline triggers catagen |
| KGF, HGF, BMP, SCF | Dermal papilla | Hair growth and melanogenesis |
| TGF-b1, IL-1a, TNF-a | — | Promote apoptosis / catagen |
| Wnt, TGF-b | Dermal papilla -> bulge stem cells | End-of-telogen activation, new anagen |
| DHT (via AR, 5-alpha reductase II) | Balding papilla | Induces TGF-b1, DKK-1; inhibits keratinocytes, miniaturization |
