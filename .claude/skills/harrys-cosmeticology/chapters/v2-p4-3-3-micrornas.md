# Part 4.3.3: MicroRNAs in Skin Physiology
**Authors**: Jean-Marie Botto, Valère Busuttil, Florian Labarrade, Catherine Serre, Laurine Bergeron, Christophe Capallere, Nouha Domloge (Ashland Specialties France, Global Skin Research Center)

## Core Idea
MicroRNAs (miRs) are 20–25-nucleotide noncoding RNAs that silence genes after transcription by binding the 3'UTR of target mRNAs, causing degradation or translational repression. They orchestrate epidermal differentiation, pigmentation, dermal matrix/senescence, adipogenesis and hair-follicle development; miR-203 is the keratinocyte-specific master switch of epidermal differentiation. For cosmetic R&D, miR expression profiles — especially in 3D reconstructed skin — are a new readout for evaluating anti-aging ingredients in vitro.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **miR biogenesis pathway**: gene → pri-miR (Pol II or Pol III; several kb, capped, polyadenylated, hairpin, can be polycistronic) → nuclear cleavage by the "microprocessor" (Drosha + DGCR8) → pre-miR → export by Exportin-5 (XPO5) + Ran-GTP (also XPO1) → cytoplasmic cleavage by DICER1 + TRBP → 20–25-nt duplex → one strand loaded into RISC (DICER1, TRBP, AGO2) → target mRNA.
  - When to use: understanding knockouts (Dicer deletion) and where an ingredient might act.
  - How: intronic miRs come from introns of protein-coding transcripts via Pol II promoters and enter as pre-miR.
- **Two silencing modes by complementarity**: full match → AGO2 cleaves/degrades mRNA; partial match → translational block. One miR can hit many mRNAs and one mRNA can be targeted by many miRs.
- **miR nomenclature**: sequential numbers (miR-29, miR-30); "miR" = mature, "mir" = precursor/gene; species prefix (hsa human, mmu mouse, dme Drosophila); letter suffix = paralogs differing at 1–2 positions (miR-29a/b); number suffix = distinct loci making identical mature miR (mir-7-1/-2/-3); miR-155* = minor opposite-arm product; -5p/-3p = arm of origin.
- **Senescence miR classes**: inflamma-miRs, senescence-associated (SA-miRs), onco-miRs; they converge on the p53–p21 and p16–pRB pathways.
- **In vitro aging models**: intrinsic aging ≈ replicative senescence (many subcultures); extrinsic ≈ UV-induced senescence; H₂O₂-induced premature senescence.
- **Tissue-engineered skin workflow**: primary cells from surgery → monolayer amplification → frozen cell bank (liquid nitrogen) → 3D reconstruction (epidermis, optionally with melanocytes; "full thickness" with fibroblasts in a collagen scaffold producing DEJ proteins). Anti-miRs or pre-miRs can be applied to study specific miR function, accounting for donor age, gender, ethnicity and phototype.

## Key Concepts
- **Genome context**: Human Genome Project (2003): ~98% of DNA is non-protein-coding; ~43% of noncoding DNA is transcribed (introns, ncRNAs: siRNA, miRNA, piRNA, snoRNA, lncRNA).
- **RNA interference (RNAi)**: inhibition of gene expression by small double-stranded RNA; named by Fire & Mello (1998, C. elegans); Nobel Prize 2006. Earlier: co-suppression in plants (Napoli 1990), "quelling" in Neurospora (1992).
- **siRNA vs miRNA**: siRNA from exogenous long RNA (viruses, transposons, plasmids, or synthetic); miRNA transcribed from the cell's own genome.
- **Milestones**: lin-4 (1993, Ambros, C. elegans); let-7, first human miR (2000, Ruvkun); MirBASE: 1872 human precursors / 2578 mature miRs; 24,521 entries (June 2013). Human repertoire may reach ~2000 genes, targeting 30–60% of transcripts.
- **Skin evidence**: miRs first described in skin in 2005; Dicer knockdown in mouse embryonic skin progenitors disturbs epidermal morphogenesis; epidermal Dicer deletion → hypoproliferative hair follicles without hair shaft; conditional Dicer ablation in adult mice → premature senescence.
- **Skin miRNome**: 31 most abundant miRs in healthy skin (>100-fold over background; miR-574 highest at ~450-fold; let-7 family among the most abundant); 34 miRs listed in epidermis; 31 in hair follicle.
- **miR-203**: >100-fold higher in skin than other organs; keratinocyte-specific; gradient low in basal → high in suprabasal layers; drives the switch from stem cells (KSCs) to transit-amplifying cells by repressing stemness. Targets: ΔNp63 (main), SOCS3 (controversial), c-JUN, ABL1, ZNF281; minor EDNRA, EYA4. Up-regulated in psoriasis; down in melanoma and basal cell carcinoma (acts as tumor suppressor). Anti-miR-203 lowers involucrin after calcium.
- **Keratinocyte stem cells (KSCs/SSCEs)**: <1% of basal cells, live in a niche; also in hair follicles and sebaceous glands.
- **Other epidermal miRs**: calcium differentiation (377 miRs screened): 13 up/1 down at day 3; 55 up/8 down at day 7. Nine besides miR-203 linked to differentiation in vitro and in vivo: up — miR-23b, -26a, -27b, -95, -200a, -210, -224, -328; down — miR-376a. miR-574-3p/miR-720 ↔ p63/iASPP feedback loop; miR-24 (suprabasal, actin adhesion dynamics); miR-200/miR-205 target ZEB1/ZEB2 (E-cadherin repressors) → epithelial stability; miR-193b/365a cluster anti-proliferative.
- **Pigmentation miRs**: miR-434 → tyrosinase; miR-25 → MITF (alpaca); miR-145 ↑ → depigmentation via Sox9, Mitf, Tyr, Trp1, Myo5a, Rab27a, Fscn1; miR-125b inversely related to pigment (regulated by promoter methylation); miR-203 loss promotes melanogenesis (kif5b, CREB1/MITF/Rab27a); miR-675 secreted by keratinocytes in exosomes regulates melanocytes via MITF; miR-196a-2 targets tyrosinase (rs11614913 C allele → lower vitiligo risk).
- **Dermal fibroblast/senescence miRs**: miR-29 family up in senescence; miR-29b represses collagen I; miR-152 and miR-181a up (target integrin α5, collagen XVI); miR-17/19b/20a/106a down in replicative aging; miR-34a up (targets SIRT1, E2F3); miR-22 up; miR-24 down (target p16 up); miR-191 up in senescent keratinocytes (targets SATB1, CDK6); miR-23a lowers lamin B1 in senescent fibroblasts/keratinocytes and aged skin; miR-146a in UVA fibroblasts; miR-34c in UV senescence.
- **Fibrosis**: scleroderma — let-7a ↓ (excess collagen I), miR-92a ↑ (targets MMP1), miR-150 ↓ (integrin β3, collagen I ↑), miR-7 ↓; keloids — miR-21, -382, -4269 ↑ and miR-200c, -203, -205 ↓; miR-196a involved.
- **Adipocyte miRs**: miR-155/221/222 must fall for human MSC adipogenesis (act on PPARγ, C/EBPβ); miR-22 inhibits (via HDAC6, favors bone); miR-30 promotes (targets RUNX2); miR-138, miR-27b, miR-130 inhibit (PPARγ); miR-26b needed; miR-143 is only a fine-tuner; miR-193b-365 drives brown fat; miR-519d linked to obesity.
- **Hair**: anagen (growth), catagen (regression), telogen (rest); miR-221, -125b, -106b, -410 up in androgenetic alopecia.
- **Exosomes**: carry miRs between cells (e.g., senescence-associated exosomes, p53-regulated) → long-range signaling, inflammation, candidate circulating biomarkers.
- **Centenarian signature**: up-regulated miR-21, -130a, -494, -1975, -1979 and SCARNA17 (U91) in mononuclear cells.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Use miR profiling (microarray, miR RNA-seq, qPCR, qPCR arrays) as an in-vitro efficacy readout for anti-aging ingredients; express change as relative quantification (RQ) vs untreated control.
- Prefer 3D reconstructed epidermis/full-thickness skin over monolayers: reproducible, chemically defined medium, allows keratinocyte–melanocyte and dermis–epidermis crosstalk.
- Candidate markers by claim: differentiation/barrier → miR-203 and differentiation set; pigmentation → miR-145, miR-125b, miR-675, miR-203; dermal aging/collagen → miR-29b, miR-34a, let-7a, miR-23a (lamin B1); senescence → SA-miRs (e.g., miR-20a, -29c, -34a/b/c, -217, -369, -371–373, -499, let-7f).
- Model intrinsic aging via replicative senescence, extrinsic via UV/H₂O₂, and check SA-β-galactosidase alongside miRs.
- Account for donor context (age, gender, ethnicity, phototype) when building reconstructed models.

## Mental Models
- **miRs as dimmer switches, not on/off**: they fine-tune many targets at once; balance (homeostasis) matters — miR-203 both too high (psoriasis) and too low (cancer) is unhealthy.
- **Network thinking**: one miR → many mRNAs and one mRNA ← many miRs; expect pleiotropic effects from any modulation.
- **Aging as convergent pathways**: diverse miRs converge on p53–p21 and p16–pRB, so these are the nodes to watch.
- **Cell-to-cell messaging via exosomes**: one cell type's miRs can change another's phenotype (keratinocyte → melanocyte pigmentation).

## Anti-patterns
- **Reading a bioinformatic target prediction as proof**: miR-203/SOCS3 shows predicted targets can fail validation in differentiating keratinocytes.
- **Generalizing mouse data to human skin**: most hair and adipocyte miR data come from mice/animal models; human data are limited.
- **Treating a single miR as a master regulator without knockdown evidence**: miR-143 knockdown showed it is only a fine-tuner.
