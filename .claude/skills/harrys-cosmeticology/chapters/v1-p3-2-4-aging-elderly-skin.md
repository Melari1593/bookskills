# Part 3.2.4: Compromised Skin in the Elderly
**Authors**: R. Randall Wickett, Martha Tate

## Core Idea
Skin aging is intrinsic (time, all body sites) and extrinsic (mainly UV photo-aging, also cigarette smoke, including second-hand). Surprisingly, baseline SC barrier function is preserved or even slightly improved after 60, but barrier recovery, turnover and wound healing slow, and elderly skin becomes dry and itchy; the dermis changes far more than the epidermis. Photo-aging is driven by DNA damage and ROS→MAP kinase→AP-1→MMP signaling, producing solar elastosis. Daily sunscreen and tretinoin have the strongest evidence; retinol/esters, niacinamide, hydroxy acids and antioxidants are cosmeceutical options whose claims must remain cosmetic.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Intrinsic vs extrinsic aging**: intrinsic = both collagen and elastin decrease; photo-aging = collagen decreases while (dystrophic) elastin increases (elastosis).
  - When to use: interpreting histology/claims; choosing preventive vs corrective actives.
- **Bricks-and-mortar model (Elias; updated by Harding)**: corneocytes in lipid mortar plus desmosomes and NMF; moderately hydrophobic molecules diffuse around corneocytes through lipids.
- **Photo-aging signaling cascade**: UV → ROS → cytokines → MAP kinase cascade → AP-1 → (a) collagenase/MMP transcription degrading matrix and (b) reduced fibroblast sensitivity to TGF-β → less collagen.
  - When to use: rationale for antioxidants, retinoids, sunscreens.
- **Cutometer viscoelastic parameters** (suction, 20–500 mbar, probes 2/4/6/8 mm): see Reference Tables.
  - When to use: substantiating firmness/elasticity claims; Ur/Uf correlates best with age, so increases may substantiate anti-aging elasticity effects.
  - How: small deformations localize to SC; large deformations reflect deeper layers.
- **Barrier recovery testing**: disrupt by tape stripping or acetone to TEWL 20–30 g/m²·h, track recovery over days.
- **SC turnover by dansyl chloride**: stain SC, monitor fluorescence disappearance under UV.
- **Nambour photo-aging grading**: silicone replicas of back of hands graded 1 (none) to 6 (deep horizontal lines, loss of vertical lines).

## Key Concepts
- **Demographics**: ~57 million Americans ≥60 in 2010, ~25% increase since 2000. Wrinkles and color inhomogeneity both drive perceived age.
- **Collagen**: Gly every third residue → triple helix ~300 nm × 1.5 nm; made as pro-collagen, ends cleaved extracellularly. Type III in papillary dermis, type I dominates reticular.
- **Elastic fibers**: oxytalan (10–12 nm, perpendicular to DEJ), elaunin (parallel to surface) in papillary dermis; elastin cross-linked by desmosine (from allysine via lysyl oxidase); fibrillin microfibrils.
- **GAGs/proteoglycans**: HA very high MW; chondroitin/keratan sulfate on core proteins; link proteins form hygroscopic complexes.
- **NMF**: lactate, amino acids from filaggrin, PCA (from glutamine). Filaggrin released from keratin by PAD (arginine→citrulline), digested in mid-SC.
- **Corneocyte**: ~25 µm side, area 550–1300 µm² (900–1100 most sites; ~500 µm² forehead), thickness 0.5–1.0 µm. Larger corneocytes correlate with slower benzoic acid penetration.
- **SC thickness**: 12–16 layers on most sites; ~9 forehead/eyelids; 25 dorsal hand; ≥50 palms/soles.
- **SC lipids**: glucosylceramides (→ceramides), cholesterol, cholesterol esters, long-chain fatty acids; spontaneously organize into multilayers.
- **Aging SC**: corneocyte area +8–14% (young vs >55); ceramides, fatty acids, cholesterol decline with age (21–60 y), notably ceramide 1 (EOS) linoleate, but ceramide ratio to other lipids stays constant.
- **TEWL with age**: mostly unchanged or lower in elderly (e.g., 4.43 ± 0.16 g/m²·h >80 y vs 6.41 ± 0.93 <30 y); only décolleté shows significant increase (5.62 → 7.86 g/m²·h, ages 18–29 to 60–80).
- **Penetration with age**: testosterone/estradiol unchanged; hydrocortisone, benzoic acid, acetylsalicylic acid, caffeine slower in >65 y.
- **Irritation with age**: reactivity to SLS, soap, DMSO, ethyl nicotinate, lactic acid, octanoic acid lower in older subjects; 10-day cumulative test (11 products, 18–45 vs 65–80) showed no significant difference, directionally lower in older.
- **Barrier repair**: 50% recovery at 24 h in <30 y vs 15% in >80 y; 90% recovery in ~4 days young vs 7 days old.
- **Turnover**: volar forearm SC 19.8 ± 1.4 days (<35 y) → 28.1 ± 2.7 days (>60 y); caused by lower epidermal proliferation (layer count constant). Blisters heal slower at 65–70.
- **Elderly dry skin**: itchy, patterned lines on legs; multifactorial — lower PCA (esp. deeper SC), slower renewal, less lactate (reduced sweating), possibly less sebum-derived glycerol (sebum drops at menopause; little decline in men before 80). Lactate and potassium were the only NMF components correlating with SC stiffness/hydration.
- **Dermis aging**: DEJ flattens → less shear resistance, easier ulceration. Skin thickens for 2 decades, roughly stable until ~60, then forearm thinning; face (forehead, cheeks, eye corners) thickens (sun effect). Elaunin network lost, oxytalan nearly gone by 9th decade in protected skin.
- **Dermatoglyphic pattern**: young = many shallow lines; old = fewer, deeper, oriented lines (Langer's lines).
- **Mechanics**: Uf vs age r = –0.78 (shoulder); Ur/Uf strongest age correlate (R² = 0.656, 120 women 20–65); lower Ur/Uf tracks cheek sagging; African American women had higher Ur than Caucasian (melanin photoprotection).
- **Telangiectasia**: from dermal thinning or sun; UVB at 2 MED upregulates VEGF and downregulates TSP-1; stronger sun association in men; strongly linked to current smoking.
- **Photo-aging DNA damage**: pyrimidine dimers, ROS; nucleotide excision repair declines with age; telomeres UV-sensitive.
- **Photo-aging histology**: collagen I and III reduced except Grenz zone; elastosis with lysozyme/amyloid P condensates protecting fibers from elastase; inflammatory cell infiltration.
- **Wrinkles**: crow's-feet number peaks ~35 y, mean depth rises 8× from 35 to 65. Tour de France riders (avg 25 y): exposed furrow depth 42 vs 29.5 on protected skin, pattern resembling >71-year-olds.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Measure barrier by TEWL on dry, non-sweating skin; static TEWL will not show aging deficits — test recovery after tape/acetone challenge instead.
- Elderly skin may tolerate irritants better, but repairs more slowly — design for barrier support and recovery.
- Moisturizers can reduce soap-induced patterned dryness on elderly legs in some subjects but not all.
- Use Cutometer Ur/Uf (and Ur, Uf) for elasticity claims; note dynamic indentation (no guard ring) can show increased extensibility with age — method matters.
- Use silicone replicas and image analysis for wrinkles/roughness endpoints.
- Antioxidant efficacy (e.g., 3% vitamin C) depends on an optimized vehicle.
- "Cosmeceutical" (term first published by Raymond Reed, J Soc Cosmet Chem, January 1962; popularized by Kligman) has no legal meaning under the FD&C Act — products are drugs, cosmetics or both; restrict claims to those appropriate for cosmetics.

## Mental Models
- **Static barrier is robust; dynamic repair is what ages**.
- **Bigger bricks, less mortar**: larger corneocytes may offset lipid loss and slow permeation.
- **Dermis ages more than epidermis** — visible aging is mostly a matrix problem.
- **Photo-aging is a signaling problem**: interrupting ROS→AP-1→MMP is the lever for antioxidants and retinoids.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming elderly skin has higher TEWL/weaker barrier**: data mostly show the opposite (except décolleté).
- **Casual sunscreen use only for long exposure**: evidence requires regular daily application.
- **Calling products "cosmeceuticals" to imply drug claims**: no legal status under FD&C Act.
- **Expecting visible anti-aging from green tea or oral β-carotene**: human trials failed to show it.

## Reference Tables
**Cutometer parameters**
| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ue | Immediate elastic deformation |
| Uv | Viscoelastic creep after elastic deformation |
| Uf | Total extensibility |
| Ur | Immediate elastic recovery |
| Ua | Total recovery at end of stress-off |
| R | Unrecovered deformation |
| Ua/Uf | Overall elasticity incl. creep |
| Ur/Ue | Pure elasticity |
| Uv/Ue | Viscoelastic ratio (rises with age) |
| Ur/Uf | Elastic recovery / total deformation (falls with age; best age marker) |

**Intrinsic vs photo-aging**
| Feature | Intrinsic | Photo-aging |
|---|---|---|
| Collagen | Decreased synthesis | Types I & III reduced (except Grenz zone) |
| Elastin | Decreases (elaunin/oxytalan loss) | Increases but dystrophic (elastosis) |
| Epidermis | Turnover slows | May be slightly thickened |
| Lines | Fewer, deeper, oriented | Deep furrows, crow's feet, age spots, telangiectasia |
