# Part 2.3.2: Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA) – Cosmetics and Perfumery Products: Market Access and Regulations
**Authors**: Aurélie Bafoil (Intertek Government and Trade Services)

## Core Idea
Saudi Arabia, a GCC member, is a strategic and fast-growing market (especially for luxury products) where social customs strongly shape what is acceptable. Cosmetics are regulated by the SFDA under the Gulf standard GSO 1943/2009 (EU 76/768-based) plus extra SFDA classification restrictions, and every shipment needs a pre-shipment Certificate of Conformity from an approved inspection body. Older, unsanctioned SASO product standards still get used in local customs testing, so risk analysis must cover them too.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Layered compliance model**: GSO 1943/2009 tables + SFDA Guidance for Products Classification (2012) restrictions + product-specific SASO standards (not mandatory but used locally).
  - When to use: dossier and label review for KSA.
  - How: verify composition against GSO Tables 1–5 and SFDA extra restrictions; check specific SASO parameters (e.g., cream fat content) to avoid failing customs tests.
- **Product classification (SFDA 2012 Guidance)**: drug, herbal, health, or cosmetic product based on definition, function, composition and claims.
  - How: self-classify via the guidance; for borderline cases request an official SFDA Classification Letter. High-risk (drug, herbal, certain health products) → SFDA registration; low-risk health products and cosmetics → SFDA listing.
- **Per-shipment Certificate of Conformity (CoC)**: required by MoCI Decree 6386 and SFDA.
  - How: pre-classification by inspection agent → documentary review + product testing + inspection against sanctioned standards → physical pre-shipment inspection (integrity vs shipping documents, label check) → CoC valid for one shipment, bearing invoice references; presented to SFDA agent and customs at port. Optional registration with the inspection body avoids testing every shipment.

## Key Concepts
- **SFDA**: created 2003; Cosmetic Department 2008; regulates safety of imported and local cosmetics, standards and cosmetovigilance. SASO transferred food/drug/cosmetic standards to SFDA in 2011, but applicable standards are still SASO-issued/adopted. SFDA draft Cosmetic Safety Standard (Dec 2012) intended to replace all SASO cosmetic standards.
- **GSO 1943/2009 (= SASO 1953/2009)**: Table 1 prohibited; Table 2 restricted; Table 3 colourants; Table 4 preservatives; Table 5 UV filters.
- **Definition**: identical to the EU definition (external parts, teeth, oral mucosa; cleaning, perfuming, changing appearance, correcting odours, protecting, keeping in good condition).
- **Illustrative cosmetic list exceptions**: face masks except peeling products; external intimate hygiene products only without antimicrobial ingredients; skin whiteners except hydroquinone-containing ones; alcohol-containing mouthwashes banned (SFDA 2011, alcohol-free alternatives exist).
- **Country-of-origin rule**: mandatory on product and immediate pack (MoCI Royal Decree M/5, 2010); "Made in EU" not accepted; avoid dual-origin wording ("Brand of Paris" + made in China; "designed in France, manufactured in China").
- **Perfumery (SASO 585/2000, ethanol-based)**: ethanol must be denatured; products with <8% essential oil/fragrance compounds limited to ≤250 ml, non-removable pumps, Arabic warning "for external use only".
- **Ampoules/vials**: allowed as cosmetics if labeled in Arabic and English: "For external use only", "Avoid contact with eyes", "Open with caution".
- **Inspection bodies**: SFDA subcontracted conformity evaluation to approved inspection bodies since 2011; they certify cosmetics only.

Products falling under these restrictions are generally classified as low-risk health products subject to SFDA listing.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Mandatory label items (GSO 1943/2009): name/trademark; function (Arabic if not obvious); manufacturer name/address; production date or batch; durability (PAO or best-before); weight/volume; country of origin; INCI list; use/storage instructions (Arabic if safety-relevant); warnings in Arabic. Some Arabic is always required.
- AHA leave-on ≥3% cautions (Arabic and English): use only as directed; avoid eye contact; discontinue and consult a physician if irritation persists; cover treated areas with sunscreen before sun exposure; limit frequency and duration of contact.
- Avoid medical claims ("treatment") and claims like "cell regeneration" or "stimulation of collagen production".
- Avoid pictures/illustrations inconsistent with prevailing social customs and values.
- Expiry date: GSO only requires best-before or PAO, but some SASO standards require an expiry date; this contradiction can block clearance, so consider adding it.
- Example SASO 1512:2010 (= GSO 1152:2010, skin cream): pH 5–9.5; total fatty substance ≥15% by mass; water ≤85% by mass; shelf life ≤3 years.
- At port, random sampling may occur even with a CoC; shipments without CoC are sampled automatically. Run a risk analysis against specific SASO standards even though not officially sanctioned.
- SASO standards have GSO equivalents that may apply across other GCC countries.

## Mental Models
- In KSA, cultural acceptability is a compliance criterion alongside safety.
- Compliance is per shipment, not per product: build CoC lead time into logistics.
- Unofficial standards can still fail you; test to the strictest locally used spec.

## Anti-patterns
- **Labeling "Made in EU" or dual-origin taglines**: rejected; exact country required.
- **Drug-like claims on cosmetics**: not accepted.
- **Ignoring SASO product standards**: a safe cream can fail on non-safety parameters such as fatty matter.
- **Alcohol mouthwash or hydroquinone whitener**: not allowed as cosmetics.
