# Part 3.3.4: Mechanisms of Changes in Hair Shape
**Authors**: Manuel Gamez-Garcia, Ph.D. (Ashland Specialty Ingredients)

## Core Idea
Hair is a cross-linked semi-crystalline biopolymer that behaves like a shape memory polymer (SMP): it has a permanent shape, can store temporary shapes, and reverts on a stimulus (water, heat). Every shape change — water-set, hot iron, perm, relaxer, styling polymer, or damage-driven frizz — follows three steps: (1) soften (break physical/chemical bonds, rearrange phases), (2) impose a new shape mechanically, (3) lock it by re-forming bonds or vitrifying. Temporary changes must leave the permanent memory intact; permanent changes require irreversibly erasing part of the memory, and the tighter the curl, the more memory sites must be modified.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three-step shape change model**: soften -> impose -> fix.
  - When to use: analyzing or designing any setting, styling, perm or straightening process, or diagnosing reversion.
  - How: identify which bonds/phases are softened, how shape is imposed (static vs in motion), and what locks it (H-bonds, vitrification, disulfide re-formation, new cross-links like lanthionine).
- **Hair memory sites (three)**: (a) crystalline IF phase = permanent memory; stores temporary shape when it unfolds or denatures above Tm; (b) disulfide cross-linked amorphous matrix = stores temporary shapes when softened at Tg or via disulfide interchange; stabilizes crystalline phase; (c) hydrophilic "gel structure" on globular amorphous proteins = moisture-sensitive temporary memory (water-setting).
- **SMP requirements (Liu et al.)**: sharp Tg and/or high Tm to fix/trigger; superelasticity above transition (low loss modulus, no residual strain); complete, rapid fixing without creep. SMPs = primary cross-linked network (permanent shape) + secondary switching groups (temporary shape).
- **Rule of shape change vs memory sites**: the tighter the curl, the more memory elements must be modified to straighten (and vice versa for curling straight hair).
  - Memory deletion ladder: weak curls -> few disulfides broken; medium -> more disulfides; tighter -> plus crystalline denaturation; very tight -> plus amide (peptide) bond breakage. Stabilization required for the new shape rises in the same order.
- **Static vs dynamic shape change**: static (rollers, perm, relaxer; fiber–fiber interaction unimportant) vs dynamic (blow-drying, combing, towel; final shape = average shape set by interplay of inter-fiber friction, drying rate, H-bond formation in gel, permanent shape strength).
- **Friction vs bending balance**: inter-fiber friction > bending force -> fibers act as welded semi-solid ropes, assembly holds deformed shape; low friction (silicones, good conditioners) -> slippage, assembly recovers permanent shape while moving.
- **Body = flexural rigidity of the bundle**: depends on fiber density, intrinsic protein density, inter-fiber friction, and temporary shapes; formulators can only change friction and temporary shape.

## Key Concepts
- **Apparent permanent shape**: everyday shape = temporary shape in equilibrium with the permanent one, set by own weight, fiber–fiber interactions, length, RH, hair type (a quasi-permanent water-set). Real permanent shape appears after ~10 min water immersion of a single fiber: most wavy Caucasian, Mulatto and African fibers coil into near-perfect circles; Asian fibers stay straight. Wavy pattern arises from weight as fibers hang; tight African coils do not open under own weight.
- **Humidity effects**: high RH breaks gel H-bonds -> wavy hair gets curlier, fine straight hair gets limper.
- **Permanent memory origin**: imprinted at keratinization in the follicle; ortho:para cortical cell ratio; primary/secondary/tertiary structures stabilized by disulfide and isopeptide bonds, H-bonds, salt bridges, dispersion forces.
- **Water-setting**: water breaks gel H-bonds (covalent bonds intact) -> shape on rollers/fingers -> fast drying re-forms H-bonds and locks shape. Slow drying lets elastic recovery win. Reversed instantly by re-wetting. Gel behaves pseudoplastic with yield stress.
- **Water-set limits**: >80 °C begins disulfide breakage; >5% deformation induces alpha->beta transitions that may be partly irreversible. Gel is a weak memory: cannot straighten very curly hair or tightly curl straight hair. Water-sets relax back even at very low moisture (may take months) because the gel relaxes to the crystalline modulus.
- **Bed hair**: long-duration deformation at room temp and moisture -> bond interchange and stress relaxation in gel phase locks a shape.
- **Hot irons**: 18th-century origin; popular early 1900s for African hair. Two types: cylindrical (curling) and flat (straightening). Process: wash/condition, dry to ~80% moisture content, thin sections, 160-230 °C, 2-3 passes root to tip at ~1 inch/s, firm but gentle pressure.
- **Heat-transfer limits**: hair is a poor conductor; air pockets and rough cuticle cause gradients. Fibers 100 µm below plate are 20-25 °C cooler; middle of a 2 mm tress ~25 °C cooler; across a ~1 mm swatch on a 200 °C plate the far side stays ~60 °C cooler at equilibrium. Faster heating raises apparent Tm. Hence 170-210 °C markedly improves straightening efficiency.
- **Hot-iron mechanism (not H-bonds)**: water evaporates above ~100 °C; at 120 °C for 15 s hair is fully dehydrated, yet 100-130 °C (even 150 °C) gives little or no straightening because the gel hardens before shape is imposed. Efficient change at 150/165-230 °C via partial melting/denaturation of crystalline phase (Tm ~156 °C) and softening of disulfide matrix (Tg ~144 °C), then cooling vitrifies amorphous phase and reorients non-denatured crystallites. Lasts longer than water-set but is lost on wetting (water plasticizes amorphous phase, crystallites reorient).
- **Temporary -> permanent shift**: from 160 to 220 °C irreversible denaturation rises; more vitrified denatured protein and disulfide scission make straightening more permanent but cause damage.
- **Straightening needs motion**: static flat iron at 210 °C does not straighten curly hair; plates must stretch/open the curl by friction. Curling straight hair on a rod iron at 210 °C works statically.
- **Hot-iron damage**: protein oxidation, cuticle lifting/bulging/tear-off (excessive friction leaves cuticle fragments on plates), internal pores/voids 3-10 µm (after 5 passes at 190 °C, 0.2 in/s; pores at ~10 µm depth), tryptophan degradation, disulfide scission, gradual loss of permanent shape and strength; fiber cross-section flattening and imprints at 210-220 °C (reversible on wetting).
- **Permanent waving**: 7-9% ammonium or sodium thioglycolate at pH ~9.5 (thiolate unzips disulfides, partially destabilizes crystallites) -> wind on rollers -> 3% hydrogen peroxide reoxidizes. Changes only part of tertiary structure of amorphous phase; crystalline and primary/secondary structures retain memory -> slow reversion over months. A small % of disulfides remains permanently broken. Urea with thioglycolate may enhance H-bond destabilization/efficiency. Anfinsen ribonuclease analogy: scrambled disulfide proteins can revert to native form.
- **Alkaline relaxers**: most damaging permanent treatment; pH 11.5-14.0 (depending on curl tightness), 1.5-3% base: NaOH (lye), LiOH, Li/Na blends, guanidine hydroxide formed in situ from calcium hydroxide + guanidine carbonate (mix). Active is always OH-. Base relaxers need petrolatum scalp layer; no-base include it. 15-20 min application; rinse; citric acid neutralizing shampoo (sulfur odor, turbid with protein fragments); condition to reduce tangling. Over-exposure becomes depilatory.
- **Relaxer chemistry**: breaks disulfides, strong/weak H-bonds, salt linkages, some amide bonds; forms lanthionine, cysteic acid, sulfur, protein fragments (cystine + OH- at pH 13.5-14.0). Denaturation enthalpy reduced 30-50% (NaOH and no-lye). Efficient memory deletion but weak lock-in -> weak fibers.
- **Three relaxer straightening theories**: lanthionine cross-link locks shape; supercontraction imposes straightness; protein denaturation is key. Author: combination — disulfide breakage + denaturation delete memory, supercontraction during cream rubbing aids straight shape, lanthionine stabilizes it. Straightening while curl is uncoiled/extended, and neutralizing while held straight, increases efficiency.
- **Japanese (sodium bisulfite) and Thio relaxers**: disulfide-selective; not recommended for very tight African hair (gradual reversion). Formaldehyde (Brazilian) cross-links proteins for near-permanent straightening but is a potential carcinogen, banned in cosmetic use (US).
- **Volume**: bundle occupying more space than its nominal volume, fibers aligned in synchrony at rest and in motion. Created by synchronized waves during drying (dynamic water-set) or by increasing inter-fiber distance (friction, polymer films). Towel-dried hair holds ~80% internal moisture.
- **Drying rate and volume**: ingredients that retard evaporation slow H-bond lock-in so waves are lost; faster-drying formulas trap shape better.
- **Back-combing (teasing)**: lifts cuticles to interlock fibers — effective but very damaging.
- **Static fly-away**: hair is an insulator below rubber in the triboelectric series; combing transfers electrons to hair (negative charge); fibers repel -> volume, but uncontrollable, lost via moisture, suppressed by moisturizers; some deposited polymers increase charge buildup; causes frizz.
- **Frizz**: fibers out of synchrony with the bundle (curvature radius mm to 1-2 cm), appears after drying. Causes: memory damage (disulfide network loss, crystalline denaturation -> partially shapeless fibers), cuticle damage (high inter-fiber friction prevents recovery), static fly-away. Typical in over-bleached, over-dried, hot-ironed, poorly conditioned hair.
- **Styling polymers**: act externally by seam and spot welding between fibers; do not interact with H-bonds, disulfides or crystalline structure — but vehicle water/solvents can plasticize the gel. Key parameters: solvent evaporation rate, hair plasticization by solvent, polymer Tg, polymer/hair adhesive strength.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Water-set: dry fast (blow-dryer) before removing rollers; keep <80 °C and deformations <5% to protect memory.
- Hot iron: 170-210 °C for efficient straightening; 100-150 °C dehydrates without setting. Pre-styling polymers reduce hot spots/damage, but polymers and oils that raise friction too much cause stalling and cuticle tearing; too little friction prevents curl opening.
- Relaxers: limit to 15-20 min; neutralize immediately after rinse; keep hair extended during processing and neutralization; condition right away.
- Perms: accept periodic retreatment; to improve, address residual crystalline/primary-secondary memory, not just disulfide yield.
- Volume products should modify inter-fiber friction, drying rate, H-bond formation, or permanent shape; favor faster-drying vehicles.
- Body boosters: raise inter-fiber friction, spot welding, strong wave patterns, high drying rates.
- Frizz: temporary fixes (water-set, iron) recur after washing; for surface-caused frizz, smooth cuticle / lower friction; combined causes need combined treatments; control static.
- Aerosols (ethanol or ethanol/water): low hair plasticization, fast evaporation -> rapid weld hardening, good for capturing shape in motion; need appropriate Tg and sufficient cationic charge on the polymer. Produce irregular spot welds.
- Gels/mousses: water-based, partly plasticize hair; pseudoplastic with high yield stress (low viscosity at high shear during application, high at zero shear) suppresses recovery; gels give thicker, more uniform seams -> best for extreme styles, forming polymer/hair composites.
- Polymer Tg: well above room temp -> hard, brittle seams, flaking; near room temp -> flexible hold; below room temp or moisture-plasticized -> flows like chewing gum, style lost. Choose moisture-resistant high-Tg polymers for humidity hold.
- Adhesion at polymer/hair interface must resist recovery stresses and fiber de-swelling contraction.
- VOC limits push toward water-soluble polymers that turn hydrophobic once cast as films.
- Polymers alone cannot straighten very curly hair or tightly curl straight hair; combine with irons or chemistry.

## Mental Models
- Erase-then-write: permanent change = delete old memory + imprint and lock new shape; efficiency depends on both, not just bond breaking.
- Memory strength scales with curl tightness: match process severity to curl type, or expect reversion.
- Time/temperature/plasticizer superposition: anything that lowers Tg (water) unlocks heat- or polymer-set shapes.
- Assembly behavior is a friction vs bending contest: surface treatments decide whether hair follows its own memory or the stylist's.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ironing at 100-150 °C expecting a set**: dehydrates and hardens gel before shape is imposed; little straightening.
- **Pressing too hard / high-friction pre-treatments with irons**: stalls the iron, lifts and tears cuticle.
- **Thio or bisulfite on very tight African curls**: insufficient memory deletion -> reversion.
- **Leaving relaxer on too long**: depilatory; delayed neutralization keeps OH- reacting.
- **Slow-drying volumizers**: H-bonds form too slowly to beat recovery forces.
- **Polymer Tg too high or too low**: flaking vs flow/loss of hold.
- **Back-combing for volume**: works by cuticle damage.
