# Part 1.2: Creating the Right Fragrance for Your Personal Care Product
**Authors**: Jill B. Costa, Ph.D. (Bell Flavors and Fragrances)

## Core Idea
Fragrance covers base malodor, makes a product appealing and, most importantly, reinforces its benefits; its in-product performance is critical to commercial success. A fragrance smells different in each base because perception depends on volatility, oil/water partitioning (log Kow) and odor detection thresholds, and on the product-use cycle. The formulator gets the best result by briefing the fragrance house fully (product type, use cycle, key evaluation point, regulatory/label needs), buying the right fragrance type, buying it concentrated, and troubleshooting against an unfragranced control.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Fragrance-house workflow**: Salesperson gathers brief -> Sales Management prioritizes (project financials + relationship) -> Evaluation checks the library -> Perfumer(s) create if needed -> Applications dose into bases -> Evaluator selects -> submission -> chemist feedback -> purchase -> formula removed from library, QC standard set.
  - When to use: planning a fragrance request and timeline.
  - How: supply product type, odor character, benchmarks/olfactive targets, brand and target market, fragrance price point, dosage, price per unit, regulatory needs, expected annual volume, and ideally an unfragranced base.
- **Fragrance pyramid (top / middle / base notes)**: notes (accords) are odor characters, each built from 2-20 materials.
  - When to use: reading a fragrance breakdown; houses provide the pyramid on request.
- **Three hurdles to perception**: (1) volatilize into air (boiling point/vapor pressure), (2) escape the product matrix (solubility, Raoult's Law, log Kow), (3) exceed the odor detection threshold at the nose.
  - When to use: diagnosing why a fragrance changes character in a base or disappears in use.
- **Product-use cycle / key evaluation point**: each use event is a possible consumer evaluation point; usually one is most important.
  - When to use: briefing the house and deciding fragrance complexity and budget.
  - How: identify the key point (e.g., bloom for rinse-off; application for facial lotion/hair gel; hours later for body lotion) and ensure top performance there.
- **Fragrance-type selection** (traditional, allergen-label-free, WS, WD, INCI blends, natural, essential-oil, GRAS): choose based on label wording, clarity, natural claims, region and mouth contact.
- **Troubleshooting protocol**: always run the unfragranced base under identical conditions first; if the fragrance is implicated, document conditions, dosage, packaging, time to onset; give the house the unfragranced base and time for several rework cycles.

## Key Concepts
- **Evaluator vs Perfumer**: Evaluator (critic/caddy) translates the request into an olfactive structure, tracks trends, critiques; Perfumer (chef/pro golfer) chooses materials. Evaluators specialize (Air Care, Household, Personal Care).
- **Perfumer training**: 5-10 year apprenticeship (Compounder -> Assistant -> Junior -> Perfumer); memorizes up to 3,000 raw materials.
- **Creation process**: start with a ~85-90% complete formula, then 5-15 trials to finish the last 10-15%; several perfumers may compete.
- **Library**: unchosen, approved fragrances; tens of thousands of formulas, hence no catalogs.
- **QC on purchase**: flash point, refractive index, color range, specific gravity known from trial samples; odor matched by **triangle test** (two of one, one of the other; find the odd sample; statistical analysis).
- **RIFM**: safety/environmental studies; maintains master list of permitted materials. **IFRA**: limits each material per product category via the QRA document; 11 product categories (lip care to shampoos, wipes, candles). Know your IFRA category when requesting paperwork.
- **Natural materials**: essential oils (steam distillation; citrus by peel pressing), concretes (hydrophobic solvent extraction; oil + waxes), absolutes (ethanol extraction of concrete, or supercritical CO2), natural isolates (crystallization, e.g., menthol from peppermint, or fractional distillation; no chemical change).
- **Synthetics**: from crude sulfate turpentine, petroleum or plant molecules; well characterized, plentiful, cost-stable, not seasonal, widest odor range; form the framework of most fragrances.
- **Botanics**: plant materials used for marketing/performance rather than odor; labeled per PCPC (formerly CTFA).
- **Top notes**: most volatile, immediate (citrus, green, marine, ozone, light fruity). **Middle notes ("Coeur")**: most characteristic (florals, sweet, gourmand, deeper fruity, light woody). **Base notes**: least volatile, longest lasting, most expensive; prevent a "cheap"/"thin" smell (musks, woods, amber, heavy florals).
- **Character vs characteristics**: character = odor components (term of art); characteristics = measurable physical properties.
- **log Kow**: octanol-water partition coefficient; each 1 unit of log Kow = 1,000x more material prefers the oily phase (as stated). Most fragrance materials are hydrophobic.
- **Odor detection threshold**: minimum air concentration to be perceived; intensity then rises to a plateau. Food/ripe fruit and fecal/decay characters have very low thresholds; flowers and woods ("survival-neutral") higher.
- **Dilution in use**: on dilution (e.g., shampoo with water) materials fall below threshold and drop out of the profile.
- **Anosmia** (specific inability, often genetic, e.g., certain musks, sandalwood); **saturation** (reversible receptor overload by a strong material); **adaptation** (brain stops noticing a constant odor).
- **Trigeminal response**: ammonia also triggers a separate nerve pathway, so it cannot be fully covered by fragrance.

## Ingredients & Actives
| Ingredient (INCI/common) | Function / mechanism | Use level / data given in the text | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essential oils | Complexity, "naturalness" | ~1-20% of a standard fragrance | Cost swings with yield, politics, season, weather; citrus often cheaper (food by-product) |
| Concretes / absolutes | "Touch" ingredients, richness | 0.001-0.100% of the fragrance | Very expensive; mainly fine fragrance, high-end creams |
| Carriers (total) | Physical stability, processing aid, fluidizer vs crystallization | ~5-20% of a traditional fragrance | Choice depends on product type |
| Dipropylene glycol | Carrier | — | Most water-soluble common carrier; poorly soluble in bath oils/some silicones (forms second phase); preferred for WS fragrances |
| Isopropyl myristate | Hydrophobic carrier | — | Homogeneous in bath oils/silicones; may be unsuitable for sulfate-free shampoo |
| Benzyl benzoate, triethyl citrate, Polysorbate 20 | Common carriers | — | Listed with IPM and DPG as most common today |
| Diethyl phthalate | Universal carrier | — | Very good for all product types but many companies no longer allow it |
| Polysorbates | Solubilizer in WS fragrances | 70-80% of a WS blend | Only 20-30% of WS is odor-active |
| Hydrogenated soybean oil, medium-chain vegetable triglycerides | Cost-reducing carriers for natural INCI blends | — | Highly hydrophobic; can cause phase/viscosity issues |
| Chelating agent + slightly acidic pH | Mitigate color shift from vanilla/gourmand notes | — | Helps; may not fully solve |

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Evaluate in 1-2 oz wide-mouthed bottles with ~50% headspace for standardized comparisons; houses screen 2-3x the final number of submissions.
- Match fragrance intensity to positioning: high-end facial creme usually much less intense than mass-market body creme.
- Judge cost by contribution per kg of finished product, not price per kg of fragrance. Worked example: $40/kg at 0.5% = $0.20/kg product; diluted to $20/kg with $7.50/kg carrier leaves only ~38% odor-active, needing 1.3% dosage = $0.26/kg (+$0.06/kg) plus extra carrier that may affect physical properties.
- DIY presolubilization: start at 2-3 parts surfactant : 1 part fragrance oil; test at final dosage; go up to 4-5 : 1 if hazy or phase-separating; ask the house for a DPG carrier to reduce surfactant need. All-natural fragrances are most hydrophobic and need the most surfactant.
- WD (water-dispersible): typically 50/50 surfactant/oil (as low as 30/70); hazy but temporarily stable; used for low-dose dosing, e.g., facial wipe at 0.010% fragrance dosed as 0.100% of a water dilution.
- Color: vanilla/spun-sugar gourmand -> yellow or light pink; grapey, fruity, heavy jasmine/tuberose -> pink (often only fix is reducing the note).
- Phase instability: try a carrier change first; it barely affects character.
- Stability signals: color/odor in rapid aging or UV/fluorescent light; phase in freeze-thaw; pearlizer separation on heating not reversed on cooling.
- Odor "turning" in stability usually means reaction with base or actives; reformulate or switch fragrance.
- Highly volatile malodors (ammonia in hair color, SO2 from bisulfite) can be only partly masked; bisulfite levels are usually low enough for fragrance to help.
- EU: if any of the 26 dermal allergens (SCCNFP consensus) exceed set levels in a finished product, they must be named on the label; request "EU Allergen Label Free" up front if none should appear.
- Request INCI blends up front (special creation, usually under confidentiality agreement); tell the house how many ingredients/letters the label can carry.

## Mental Models
- **Fragrance and base are "in a dance"**: never assume the fragrance is the culprit until the unfragranced base is cleared.
- **Same smell, different recipe**: a "fresh green rosy musky" fragrance for a facial creme and for a shampoo may smell alike but use very different materials; fragrances must be designed per product type.
- **Concentration buys performance**: buying dilute fragrance to cut price per kg raises cost in use.
- **More evaluation points = more complexity = higher price**: cheaper fragrances compromise on some evaluation points.

## Anti-patterns
- **Asking for a diluted fragrance to lower price/kg**: costs more in use and adds carrier that may destabilize the product.
- **Blaming fragrance without an unfragranced control**: the base itself may discolor or split.
- **Requesting just "a fragrance"**: you get a traditional fragrance; INCI, allergen-free, WS or GRAS types must be specified at the start.
- **Buying WS fragrance when you can presolubilize**: pays for 70-80% surfactant; DIY at the needed level may be cheaper.
- **Expecting fragrance to cover ammonia**: lower vapor pressure and the trigeminal response make it nearly impossible.
- **Allergen-label-free INCI blend for a base with malodor**: these are mostly volatile fruity top notes with little base, poor masking.

## Reference Tables
| Fragrance type | What it is | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional / standard | Natural + synthetic + 5-20% carrier; labeled "fragrance" | Best default: cost-stable, versatile, robust, any character/price |
| EU allergen label free | Avoids the 26 EU allergens | Limited palette; no natural essential oils |
| Water-soluble (WS) | Fragrance pre-solubilized in 70-80% surfactant | Clear gels; only 20-30% odor-active |
| Water-dispersible (WD) | 30-50% surfactant | Hazy, semi-stable; for very low dosing |
| Natural INCI blend | All-natural, each material INCI-listed, no word "fragrance" | Very restrictive, costly, cost volatile |
| Allergen-label-free INCI blend | Only INCI chemicals (from essential oils/natural sources), no oils/absolutes | Most restrictive; long chemical names on label; mostly fruity top notes |
| Traditional INCI blend | INCI-named naturals + synthetics | Pleasant but limited; cheaper than natural INCI |
| "Unscented" INCI blend | 2-3 neutral materials | Softens base odor (eye/face creams) without scent; not full coverage |
| Natural fragrance (traditional natural blend) | Oils, absolutes, concretes, isolates, fermentation products; labeled "natural fragrance" | 3-10x cost of traditional; no fine-fragrance knockoffs |
| Essential-oil natural blend | Whole essential oils only | Most expensive, most limited (citrus, herbal, lavender, some woody, few florals) |
| GRAS | Every material has FEMA-GRAS number/flavor status; meets skin limits | For lip products, items entering mouth, children's toys; fruity/food, short-lived, simple |

| Use-cycle stage | Importance |
|---|---|
| Point of purchase (open pack) | Fragrance house focuses heavily here |
| Dispensing on hands | Considered, hard to standardize |
| Bloom with water (rinse-off) | Often the key point for rinse-off |
| Rinsed wet skin/hair | Minor for cosmetics (key for laundry) |
| Dry skin/hair | Minor unless claims made |
| Application (leave-on) | Key for facial lotion, hair gel |
| Hours after (leave-on) | Key for body lotion; irrelevant for hair gel if not unpleasant |
