# Part 4.3.7: Antioxidants in Cosmetics for Anti-Aging
**Authors**: Ratan K. Chaudhuri (Sytheon Ltd.)

## Core Idea
UV-generated reactive oxygen and nitrogen species damage proteins, lipids, carbohydrates and DNA directly, and indirectly via oxidase enzymes (NADPH oxidase), free iron/copper and calcium; aged skin has a weakened antioxidant network. A true broad-spectrum antioxidant strategy must quench radicals and nonradicals, chelate transition metals, inhibit oxidase enzymes, stimulate endogenous defenses — and, for anti-aging, both protect ECM/DEJ from MMPs and stimulate their synthesis. Antioxidants can turn pro-oxidant with transition metals, so combinations and photostable, chelating actives used with photostable broad-spectrum sunscreens are the recommended approach.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two damage routes**: (1) direct oxidation of critical biomolecules + altered gene expression and inflammation; (2) indirect — oxidase enzymes induced, then radicals generated.
- **ROS generation chain**: endogenous photosensitizers (porphyrins, riboflavin, ubiquinone, NADH/NADPH, trans-urocanic acid) + UVA → triplet states → singlet oxygen and superoxide → SOD → H₂O₂ → with Fe²⁺ (Fenton) → hydroxyl radical → lipid peroxidation. Many organic sunscreens also act as triplet sensitizers making singlet oxygen.
- **Broad-spectrum antioxidant criteria**: quench radicals and nonradicals; chelate free Fe, Cu and Ca; inhibit NADPH oxidase and other oxidases; up-regulate antioxidant defense.
  - When to use: screening an antioxidant or blend for skin protection claims.
- **Two-front anti-aging test**: (a) protect ECM and DEJ from UV-induced MMPs; (b) stimulate ECM/DEJ protein synthesis.
- **Four-part antioxidant defense (Halliwell & Gutteridge)**: low-MW scavengers; enzymes (SOD, catalase, GPx); metal-sequestering proteins (transferrins, metallothionein); protective proteins (heat shock proteins).
- **Antioxidant definition**: any substance that, at low concentration vs an oxidizable substrate, significantly delays or prevents its oxidation.

## Key Concepts
- **RNS**: longer-lived and more specific than ROS; NO + superoxide → peroxynitrite → cytochrome c release → apoptosome (caspase-9, Apaf-1) → caspase-3/7 → apoptosis.
- **NADPH oxidase (Nox)**: a "professional" ROS producer (others make ROS as by-products); major source of UVA-induced ROS in keratinocytes and fibroblasts; Nox1 activated by rising intracellular Ca²⁺ and by ceramide; drives PGE2; glucose also stimulates it. Other oxidases: COX, lipoxygenase, P450, NOS, xanthine oxidase, mitochondrial complex I.
- **Iron, copper, calcium**: UVB/UVA cause iron-catalyzed damage; iron elevated in sun-exposed skin (superoxide attacks ferritin → free iron); iron release activates NF-κB in UVA-irradiated fibroblasts. Sources of Fe/Cu on skin: UV, sweat, water. UVA ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ → Nox1 → more ROS → more Ca²⁺ (loop).
- **Pro-oxidant antioxidants**: vitamin C reduces Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺, which reacts with H₂O₂ → OH•; also vitamin E (high concentrations accelerate lipid autoxidation; tocopheroxyl radicals under UV), glutathione, proanthocyanidins, plant phenolics, carotenoids. Not all bad: ascorbate's pro-oxidant action hydroxylates proline → hydroxyproline for collagen.
- **Skin antioxidant levels**: lipophilic (vitamin E, ubiquinones, carotenoids) and hydrophilic (vitamin C, GSH, urate). Molar: vitamin C is 15× glutathione, 200× vitamin E, 1,000× ubiquinones. Epidermis > dermis (6× for C and GSH, 2× for E and ubiquinones). Vitamin E dominates in stratum corneum.
- **Aging changes**: epidermal SOD and GPx unchanged, catalase and glutathione reductase tend to rise; dermal catalase significantly falls; α-tocopherol, ascorbate, GSH fall.
- **MMPs**: 24 identified; UVA and UVB raise MMP-1 and MMP-3 4–5-fold in fibroblasts while TIMP-1 barely rises; MMP-1 (not MMP-13) is the main collagen-degrading enzyme in photo-aging. Collagen synthesis in sun-damaged skin is similar to protected skin → loss is from degradation.
- **ECM roles**: collagen = tensile strength; elastin = elasticity; glycoproteins = adhesiveness; proteoglycans/hyaluronan = compressibility; integrins = communication.
- **DEJ**: four zones — basal keratinocyte membrane/hemidesmosomes; lamina lucida (laminin 5); lamina densa (collagen IV, perlecan, nidogen); sublamina fibrillar zone (collagen VII anchoring fibrils). Aging flattens rete pegs (fragility, less nutrient exchange), reduces collagen VII (wrinkle marker), pro-collagen 1/3, fibrillin 1; collagen IV falls after age 35 while basement membrane thickens.

## Formulation & Practical Guidance
- Apply antioxidants topically to target skin directly; benefit is clear before UV exposure, less so after.
- Combine antioxidants: C + E far exceeds either alone; adding melatonin improves further; 15% L-ascorbic + 1% α-tocopherol + 0.5% ferulic acid protected in vivo.
- Pair antioxidants with photostable broad-spectrum sunscreens: SPF-30 alone cut free-radical damage 39%; adding 0.5% vitamin E + 0.1% emblica raised it to 73%.
- Vitamin E with emblica gave synergistic protection of skin lipids (tape-stripped lipid model); vitamin E protects dose-dependently.
- Screen in realistic models: in tape-stripped skin lipids, N-acetyl cysteine, rosemary oleoresin, Rosa gallica extract, Thermus thermophillus ferment and a green-plant photosynthetic complex *increased* lipid hydroperoxides (pro-oxidant).
- Include chelators or choose chelating antioxidants to neutralize Fe/Cu catalysis; formulate SAP/MAP above pH 7 with a chelating agent; use ascorbyl glucoside for acidic systems.
- Stabilize retinol against photo-oxidation and singlet oxygen with bakuchiol; stabilize avobenzone with antioxidant-photostabilizers.
- Antioxidants must be in the right place, at the right concentration, at the right time; real-world benefit is limited by how adequately products are applied.

## Mental Models
- **Network, not a single hero**: antioxidants regenerate and back each other up; single-antioxidant products ignore this interplay.
- **Metal context flips the sign**: any reducing antioxidant can become pro-oxidant with free Fe/Cu — chelation is part of antioxidant design.
- **Source control over mop-up**: inhibiting Nox and chelating metals stops ROS generation upstream instead of only scavenging downstream.
- **Prevention + repair**: anti-aging antioxidants must both block MMP-driven ECM loss and stimulate new ECM/DEJ synthesis.

## Anti-patterns
- **Advocating antioxidants without considering pro-oxidant activity**: vitamin C, E, GSH, carotenoids can generate radicals with metals or UV.
- **Relying on tocopheryl esters for fast protection**: conversion to tocopherol in skin is slow and minor.
- **Formulating L-ascorbic acid in plain aqueous systems**: unstable even at neutral pH.
- **Assuming any "antioxidant" botanical protects**: several increased lipid peroxidation in skin-lipid tests.
- **Using sunscreen alone**: leaves most free-radical damage unaddressed.
