# Patterns & Techniques

## Jun-Chen-Zuo-Shi Formula Architecture
**When to use**: Evaluating or understanding any Chinese herbal formula's structure — why it has multiple herbs instead of one "active ingredient."
**How**: Every well-balanced formula has (1) Jun/Emperor — main herb(s), highest ratio, targets the disease directly; (2) Chen/Minister — adjuvant, boosts main herb or targets secondary symptoms; (3) Zuo/Assistant — reduces the main herb's side effects; (4) Shi/Courier — guides the formula to target Organs / harmonizes the whole.
**Trade-offs**: Multi-herb complexity increases bioavailability and safety but makes formulas harder to "simplify" — isolating one herb (see Ma Huang → Ephedra) breaks the built-in safety design.

## Pattern Differentiation (core diagnostic method)
**When to use**: Before selecting any herbal formula for any condition in this book.
**How**: Match the full physical + emotional symptom picture (not just the Western diagnostic label) to a named TCM pattern (e.g., Liver Qi Stagnation, Deficient Vexation, Kidney Yang Deficiency). One Western diagnosis can map to multiple TCM patterns, and one TCM pattern/formula can treat multiple Western diagnoses simultaneously ("one formula, many patterns").
**Trade-offs**: Requires attending closely to physical symptoms (temperature of extremities, digestion, pulse) most Western mental-health frameworks ignore — more diagnostic effort, but more targeted treatment.

## Five Element Music Therapy Protocol
**When to use**: Adjunct for insomnia, depression, anxiety, or SAD.
**How**: Match musical key to target Organ (Gong/Earth-Spleen, Shang/Metal-Lung, Jue/Wood-Liver, Zhi/Fire-Heart, Yu/Water-Kidney); play 30 minutes, 1–2x/day, for at least 1–4 weeks, in a quiet comfortable setting; steady rhythms preferred over dramatic dynamic shifts for anxiety.
**Trade-offs**: Low risk, low cost, but slower-acting than herbal formulas; best combined with, not substituted for, other treatment.

## Acupressure Self-Treatment
**When to use**: Daily or as-needed adjunct to herbal treatment; safe for self-administration without practitioner supervision.
**How**: Locate the point (see per-chapter worked examples), apply firm but not painful pressure (finger, acupressure stick, or pencil eraser) for 1–3 minutes, breathing slowly.
**Trade-offs**: Gentler and slower than herbal formulas or professional acupuncture; best for daily maintenance and acute in-the-moment relief rather than as a sole treatment for severe conditions.

## Footbath Protocol (insomnia)
**When to use**: Nightly sleep-hygiene ritual, especially for heat/stress-driven insomnia.
**How**: Fill a basin with 104–110°F (40–43°C) water covering the ankles; optional mugwort or ginger herbal sachet; soak 15–30 minutes (24 min is the researched optimum); dry feet immediately; hydrate with warm water after.
**Trade-offs**: Contraindicated for diabetics (reduced peripheral sensation risk). Preferred over full-body baths, which TCM considers overly heating/draining.

## Qigong's Three Adjustments
**When to use**: Selecting or designing a qigong practice for a specific mental health goal.
**How**: Target one or more of — Adjusting the Heart-Mind (調心, mental/emotional focus), Adjusting the Breathing (調息), Adjusting the Physical Structure (調形, posture/movement). The three interact — tension held in posture and habitual breathing both affect mental state.
**Trade-offs**: Requires supervision for those with severe mental illness or family history of it; stop immediately if symptoms worsen.

## Ginseng Constitutional Matching
**When to use**: Selecting a single-herb tonic for brain fog, fatigue, or general depletion.
**How**: Asian (Korean) ginseng = warming — for poor circulation, coldness, digestive issues. American ginseng = cooling — for heat-y, thirsty, irritable, flushed constitutions. White ginseng = simply dried; red ginseng = further steamed (different, and understudied-as-distinct, brain effects).
**Trade-offs**: Mismatching warming/cooling type to constitution can worsen symptoms. Stop if it causes insomnia or nervousness — sign of poor constitutional fit.

## Formula-Taking Protocol (general)
**When to use**: Starting any new herbal formula in this book.
**How**: Take with warm water only (never juice or coffee — alters pharmacology); space ≥1 hour from Western medications; take at full dosage for 4–6 weeks; expect noticeable improvement within 1–2 weeks; if none, the formula likely doesn't match your pattern — reassess. After improvement, taper to as-needed or discontinue.
**Trade-offs**: Slower onset than sedatives/stimulants, but designed for sustainable resolution rather than symptom masking, with better long-term safety profiles in the cited trials.

## Environmental Qi Engineering (Feng Shui, depression-focused)
**When to use**: Structuring a bedroom/living space to support mood, especially for depression.
**How**: Well-lit, sunlit rooms; warm colors (avoid dark/cool tones if prone to depression); square-shaped rooms (avoid triangular/irregular); never place beds/sofas/desks under a roof beam or under a chandelier on a floor above; add live plants; spacious living areas; warming incense (mugwort, cinnamon, Chen Pi).
**Trade-offs**: Preliminary evidence only (small RCTs, HRV/mood-state studies) — a low-cost adjunct, not a primary treatment.

## Daoyin Exercise for Knotted Qi
**When to use**: Worry/overthinking-driven qi stagnation with depression and arm/back pain.
**How**: Practice at dawn (not after a big meal or during strong emotion, per the 7th-century *Zhubing Yuanhou Lun*). Sit, lengthen the lower back, raise the left arm palm-up, press the right hand down behind the back, inhale through the nose for 7 breaths, pressing the right hand down between breaths.
**Trade-offs**: Gentle, low-risk; best as a daily maintenance practice rather than acute intervention.
