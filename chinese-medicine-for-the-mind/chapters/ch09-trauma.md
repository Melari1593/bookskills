# Chapter 9: Trauma

## Core Idea
TCM understands trauma as "scattered" qi and disrupted Heart circulation ("Heart Shock" / "Stuck Qi in the Chest") rather than a purely psychological event — treatment restores Heart-centered Blood/qi circulation via herbs, acupressure, and the Eight Extraordinary Channels, alongside (not instead of) professional care.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Trauma's defining characteristics** (a TCM-informed checklist that closely mirrors modern trauma theory): overwhelms the nervous system; violates boundaries; disrupts relationality; severs connections; admits feelings of powerlessness; damages sense of meaning; inhibits sense of self; disrupts contact with self; instigates collapse/separation of yin and yang.
- **Heart Closed / Xiong Bi (Stuck Qi in the Chest)**: Dr. John Shen's term (via Dr. Leon Hammer's lineage) for the state where sadness/fear render the Upper Jiao (Chest, Heart, Lung) impassable — the central pathological mechanism of trauma in this framework. Zhang Zhongjing's parallel classical term is Xiong Bi.
  - When to use: as the diagnostic target whenever trauma presents with chest tightness, oppression, or disrupted heart rhythm.
- **Jingkuang vs. Jingxian**: Two opposite trauma-fright outcomes from the *Shanghanlun* — jingkuang (倞狂) is manic/frantic "madness" from collapse of yang in the Heart; jingxian (倞癇) is its opposite — withdrawal and silence. Both are "fright rising to illness," just opposite polarities.
- **Eight Extraordinary Channels model** (from the 1st-century *Nan Jing*) — a channel-based framework specifically suited to trauma's lifelong, developmental nature:
  | Channel & Master Point | Function | Symptoms/Patterns |
  |---|---|---|
  | Du (SI 3) | Sea of Yang Qi, governs yang | Spine stiffness, seizures, spasm, mental agitation, autonomy |
  | Ren (LU 7) | Sea of Yin Qi, governs yin | Internal/abdominal pain, bonding, connection to resources |
  | Chong (SP 4) | Sea of Blood/Channels | Counterflow, circulation inhibition, deep spirit communication |
  | Dai (GB 41) | Binds channels, governs ascent/descent | Cold/pain in low back/abdomen, leg weakness, suppressed feeling |
  | Yinwei (PC 6) | Coordinates yin channels (interior) | Heart pain, annoyance, loss of will/self-control, resourcefulness |
  | Yangwei (TB 5) | Coordinates yang channels (exterior) | Heat/cold suffering, difficulty from external/internal reactions |
  | Yinqiao (KI 6) | Regulates yin movement, sleep/waking | Inner tension, hard-to-identify pain, inner narratives, somnolence |
  | Yangqiao (UB 62) | Regulates yang movement, sleep/waking | Outer tension, generalized pain, insomnia |
  - **Chong + Yinwei (SP 4 + PC 6)** is highlighted as the most powerful pairing in clinical experience — Chong Mai represents "the interface between what has been and what is possible," paired with Yinwei/PC 6's classical Heart Pain function.

## Key Concepts
- **Sheng Mai San (Generate the Pulse Powder)**: Three-herb formula (Ren Shen/ginseng or American ginseng, Mai Men Dong, Wu Wei Zi) that regulates pulse rate/rhythm — take until the pulse normalizes; unique to the Shen-Hammer lineage's use for trauma.
- **Yunnan Baiyao dosing protocol**: Night before starting, take the included extra-strength red pill; Day 1: 1 capsule 2x/day; Day 2: 1 capsule 3x/day; Day 3: 1 capsule 4x/day; then max 2 capsules 4x/day until a total course of 64 capsules is complete. Always take with food.
- **Shen-Hammer pulse lineage**: Dr. Leon Hammer (MD, psychiatrist, and Chinese medicine practitioner) and his teacher Dr. John Shen's diagnostic tradition, identifying pulse qualities (e.g., "flat," "inflated") that reveal when qi/Blood in the chest is stuck — described as a "block to treatment" that must be addressed before other interventions can work.
- **Jing (精, essence)**: What severe, unaddressed trauma is said to damage, undermining basic life function over time.

## Mental Models
- Trauma manifests trans-systemically (head, musculature, nervous system, digestive system, urogenital system) — match the herbal formula to the *specific* symptom cluster (e.g., menstrual pain → Dang Gui Shao Yao San; insomnia/nightmares → Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang) rather than treating "trauma" as one target.
- Joy is framed as the classical antidote to fear-based trauma (fear is overcome by joy, per the Suwen's emotion-opposition model, also used in Ch 6 for anxiety) — deliberately generating joy-producing activity is a legitimate adjunct, not a dismissal of the trauma's seriousness.
- Circulation is Chinese medicine's throughline for trauma: promoting Heart-centered Blood/qi circulation is described as "a conduit for the healing force," reframing physical circulatory health as inseparable from psychological/spiritual healing.

## Anti-patterns
- **Self-treating trauma without professional support**: The chapter explicitly and repeatedly encourages seeking professional help (acupuncture for trauma is often available at low-cost/sliding-scale rates via nonprofits) — herbal/acupressure tools are adjuncts, not replacements.
- **Using Yunnan Baiyao while on blood thinners**: Avoid or seek professional guidance first.
- **Treating trauma as purely psychological**: Physical presentations (sweating, palpitations, racing breath, numbness) can indicate underlying emotional trauma even without an identifiable precipitating event — don't dismiss physical symptoms as unrelated.

## Worked Example
**Acupressure for trauma** (a few minutes each, or as long as comfortable):
1. **PC 6 Nei Guan (Inner Gate)**: a few finger-widths above the wrist crease, between the two prominent tendons on the inner wrist — for chest oppression, disrupted heart rhythm, or nausea; the primary point for Stuck Qi in the Chest.
2. **TB 5 Wai Guan (Outer Gate)**: directly opposite Nei Guan on the outer wrist — can be pressed simultaneously with PC 6 to foster inner/outer ease; eases agitation, irritability, overwhelm.
3. **LU 1 Zhong Fu (Central Residence)**: below the collarbone end, in the first intercostal space — crossing hands to contact both sides promotes chest openness and fuller breath.

A simple grounding technique requiring no point knowledge: cup both hands on the shoulders, or apply gentle pressure to the abdomen with both hands, breathing into the area while acknowledging "this is my own body, my own experience" — establishing safety and internal stillness.

## Key Takeaways
1. Treat trauma's core mechanism as Heart-centered qi/Blood stagnation ("Heart Shock"/Stuck Qi in the Chest) — most formulas and acupressure points target restoring this circulation.
2. Match formula to the dominant symptom system: pulse/palpitation issues → Sheng Mai San; chest/heart pain + nightmares → Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang or Yunnan Baiyao; menstrual/abdominal → Dang Gui Shao Yao San; grief/weeping → Gan Mai Da Zao Tang.
3. The Chong + Yinwei (SP 4 + PC 6) Eight Extraordinary Channel pairing is highlighted as especially powerful for trauma work.
4. Always pair self-care tools with professional support — trauma treatment in this book is explicitly adjunctive, not a replacement for licensed care.
5. Avoid Yunnan Baiyao on blood thinners; follow its specific escalating dosage protocol exactly.
