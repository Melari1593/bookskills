# Chapter 7: ADHD and Lack of Focus

## Core Idea
TCM treats ADHD/inattention through the same "supplementing deficiency, reducing excess" logic used elsewhere, framing inattention (excess yin/Water bubbling up to extinguish Heart Fire) and hyperactivity/impulsivity (excess yang/heat carrying the spirit away) as opposite poles of one spirit-not-at-rest dynamic — treatable without stimulants or their side effects.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Inattentive vs. hyperactive/impulsive as yin/yang poles**:
  - **Inattentive (yin-excess pattern)**: "Water bubbling up" extinguishing Heart Fire — palpitations, dizziness, instability, spaciness. Root text: Running Piglet's excess-yin-in-the-upper-burner model.
  - **Hyperactive/impulsive (yang-excess pattern)**: heat rises and "carries away the spirit" — restlessness, fidgeting, impulsivity, agitation.
  - **Phlegm Misting the Orifices of the Heart**: a later-developed explanatory model for the sensory/clarity end of the spectrum — a continuum from mild inattention through confusion to (in extreme, unrelated conditions) hallucination; ADHD sits at the mild end.
  - When to use: identify which pole (yin/inattentive vs. yang/hyperactive-impulsive) dominates before selecting supplementing vs. reducing formulas.
- **"Supplementing deficiency, reducing excess"**: The general ADHD treatment rule — if a quality is in excess, remove it (sedating/settling herbs); if deficient, add to it (tonifying herbs). Applied specifically at the Heart.
- **Neurodiversity framing**: The book explicitly frames ADHD as a difference to be *harmonized*, not a disease to be eradicated — many high achievers reframe ADHD traits (high energy, creativity, empathy, hyperfocus) as assets, consistent with TCM's balance-restoration (not elimination) philosophy.

## Key Concepts
- **Kidney as root of Prenatal Qi**: Because the Kidney governs marrow/bone/teeth/brain formation, all brain-based disorders (including ADHD) are considered Kidney-related in TCM.
- **Onset age theory**: The *Huangdi Neijing* places Kidney qi abundance — and thus the theoretical onset window for attention issues — at age 7 (females) / 8 (males), coinciding with tooth replacement and hair growth (brain-development markers).
- **Historical origin of stimulants**: Adderall and other amphetamines originated from early attempts to synthesize Ma Huang (Ephedra sinica) — the same TCM herb discussed in Ch 3's Ephedra cautionary tale — though Ma Huang itself is not the recommended ADHD approach in this book; non-stimulant nourishing/calming herbs are preferred.
- **Six-Ingredient Rehmannia Pill (Liu Wei Di Huang Wan)**: An 11th-century pediatric formula (Qian Yi) derived from Kidney Qi Pill, developed specifically for children considered to have "abundant yang" (i.e., not needing warming herbs) — still one of the most recognized ADHD formulas today.

## Mental Models
- Read ADHD symptoms through the same "spirit not at rest" lens used for insomnia (Ch 4) and anxiety (Ch 6) — heat carries the spirit away (hyperactivity) or Water destabilizes the Heart's fire (inattention).
- Consider the downstream cascade: unsupported ADHD-pattern sensitivity → chronic anxiety (e.g., public speaking, structured projects) → repeated failure → eventual depression from inner-resource exhaustion. Early pattern-based intervention is framed as preventing this cascade, not just treating the presenting symptom.
- Diet-driven phlegm/Dampness (loose stools, bloating) can cloud perception and eventually affect the Heart — treat gut/digestive symptoms as potentially causal for attention symptoms, not incidental.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming stimulant medication is the only option**: Long-term stimulant use carries side effects (appetite loss, fatigue, anxiety, insomnia, crash/depressed mood, jaw clenching, hair loss, skin problems, and gray matter abnormalities) that TCM's nourishing/calming approach avoids.
- **Using Fu Ling Gui Zhi Gan Cao Da Zao Tang with digestive issues**: Contraindicated.
- **Treating all inattention the same way**: Yin-excess (spacey, watery, dizzy) and yang-excess (heat, agitation, impulsivity) patterns require opposite therapeutic direction (settling vs. clearing heat vs. tonifying).

## Worked Example
**Acupressure for ADHD**: Press **Yintang** (Third Eye Point, between the eyebrows — also used for insomnia in Ch 4) to relax the forebrain and calm an overactive mind, enhancing executive function. Then work the **Small Intestine channel** from **SI 3 (Houxi)** — the small bulge formed when making a loose fist — toward **SI 5** (adjacent to the Heart's source point, Shen Men), massaging along the outer edge of the hand/5th metacarpal toward the elbow. Rationale: the Small Intestine "separates the clear from the turbid" both digestively and mentally (thoughts from feelings, ideas from urges), and SI 3 is the master point of the Governing Du Vessel, which tracks the spine/brain axis.

A simple non-point-specific reset: place hands just below the navel and follow the breath's rise and fall for a few minutes to draw awareness into the abdomen and settle the nervous system — useful as a quick in-the-moment intervention (e.g., via a smartphone reminder) even without formal acupressure.

## Key Takeaways
1. Match the pole — inattentive/yin-excess vs. hyperactive-impulsive/yang-excess — before selecting a formula; they call for different (sometimes opposite) herbal strategies.
2. Huang Lian Wen Dan Tang and Gui Zhi Gan Cao Long Gu Mu Li Tang are the most broadly useful formulas for classic vexation/agitation-type ADHD symptoms.
3. Bu Nao Wan suits the "spacey, fatigued, cold" inattentive presentation specifically, distinct from the agitated/impulsive presentation.
4. ADHD-pattern sensitivity left unsupported can cascade into anxiety and later depression — treat proactively, not only reactively.
5. Yintang and the SI 3→SI 5 hand massage are simple daily-use acupressure tools for calming focus issues.
