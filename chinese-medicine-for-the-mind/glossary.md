# Glossary

**Agitation (Zao, 躁)** — Physical restlessness, usually heat-derived (fever, overwork, chronic inflammation); contrast with vexation's more mental quality. (Ch 6)

**An Shen (安神, calming the spirit)** — The core therapeutic principle underlying nearly all anxiety and panic treatment in TCM. (Ch 6)

**Blood Deficiency** — A pattern of inhibited circulation/nourishment: cold hands/feet, pallor, poor memory, dry skin/hair, anxiety, or depression, depending on presentation. (Ch 4, 5, 6, 8)

**Chi (痴, senselessness)** — Classical term for memory/clarity impairment; in severe form sometimes translated as "dementia." (Ch 8)

**Daoyin** — A precursor to qigong: therapeutic stretching/pulling exercises prescribed per-disorder, first systematized in the 7th-century *Zhubing Yuanhou Lun*. (Ch 5)

**Deficient Vexation** — The most common insomnia pattern: an internal, longer-term inability to settle the mind from overthinking, worry, or overwork; involves Liver Blood Deficiency. (Ch 4)

**Eight Extraordinary Channels** — A set of eight acupuncture channels (Du, Ren, Chong, Dai, Yinwei, Yangwei, Yinqiao, Yangqiao) from the 1st-century *Nan Jing*, particularly relevant to trauma's lifelong/developmental effects. (Ch 9)

**Fan (煩, vexation)** — "Fire next to the head" — an overwhelming heat-y mental irritability/restlessness. (Ch 6)

**Feng shui (風水, "wind and water")** — Environmental qi theory; preliminary studies support its effects on mood, heart rate variability, and sleep quality. (Ch 5)

**Five Element Music Therapy** — Pentatonic-scale music (Gong/Shang/Jue/Zhi/Yu) matched to the five Organ systems, first defined in the Zhou-dynasty *Book of Rites*; clinically studied for insomnia, depression, and anxiety. (Ch 3, 4, 6)

**Five Phases (五行) / Five Emotions (五志)** — Water, Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal — the foundational model mapping emotions, directions, and Organ systems to each other. (Ch 2)

**Heart (Xin, 心) / Heart-Mind** — The seat of awareness and mind in TCM; all emotion is ultimately processed through it. (Ch 1, 2)

**Heart Closed / Xiong Bi (Stuck Qi in the Chest)** — Dr. John Shen's term for the state where sadness/fear render the Upper Jiao (Chest, Heart, Lung) impassable — the central mechanism of trauma. (Ch 9)

**Heart-Kidney interaction/noninteraction** — The primary insomnia model since the 15th century: Heart yang (sun/heaven) must properly interact with Kidney yin (earth/water); breakdown causes severe, chronic insomnia. (Ch 4)

**Huangdi Neijing (黃帝內經, Inner Classic of the Yellow Emperor)** — The foundational Chinese medical text (compiled 1st century BCE), containing the Suwen and Lingshu Jing sections; the earliest systematic discussion of mental illness in the Chinese tradition. (Ch 1, throughout)

**Internal Dampness** — Poor absorption/elimination causing excess fluid retention and compromised digestion; the unifying TCM mechanism behind brain fog and related conditions. (Ch 8)

**Jing (精, essence)** — A foundational life-resource that severe, unaddressed trauma is said to damage. (Ch 9)

**Jingkuang / Jingxian** — Two opposite trauma-fright outcomes from the *Shanghanlun*: jingkuang is manic "madness" from Heart yang collapse; jingxian is its opposite — withdrawal and silence. (Ch 9)

**Jun-Chen-Zuo-Shi (君-臣-佐-使)** — "Emperor-Minister-Assistant-Courier": the structural template for every well-designed Chinese herbal formula (main herb, adjuvant, side-effect-reducer, Organ-guiding herb). (Ch 3)

**Kidney Yang Deficiency** — A pattern closest to major depression: wanting to sleep all the time, deep hopelessness, fatigue. (Ch 5)

**Knotted qi** — A Liver-related term for qi that becomes physically "knotted," presenting as neck/upper-back/rib tension alongside low mood. (Ch 5)

**Kuang (狂, "madness")** — An early diagnostic category clustering symptoms now split into anxiety, bipolar disorder, and depression (and in severe cases, psychosis). (Ch 1, 8)

**Liver Qi Stagnation** — The most common TCM pattern implicated in depression/anxiety; also causes irregular bowels and menstrual irregularity; governed by stress/tension. (Ch 2, 4, 5, 6)

**Organ systems (zang, 藏/臟)** — Metaphorical functional systems (Heart, Lung, Spleen, Kidney, Liver) in TCM, not literal anatomical organs. (Ch 1, throughout)

**Patent formula** — A standardized, ready-to-use TCM medicine (pill/tablet/granule); "patent" refers to standardization, not legal ownership. (Ch 3)

**Phlegm Misting the Orifices of the Heart** — An explanatory model for sensory/clarity disruption, spanning a continuum from mild inattention to (in unrelated severe conditions) hallucination; ADHD sits at the mild end. (Ch 7)

**Qi (氣)** — Vital energy/"psychophysical substance" flowing through the Organ systems; can be yin (still) or yang (active). (Ch 1, throughout)

**Qigong (氣功, "qi skill")** — Mind-body exercises (breathing, visualization, self-massage, stretching) for physical and mental health, dating to at least the Warring States Period. Three levers: Adjusting Heart-Mind, Breathing, Physical Structure. (Ch 3)

**Running Piglet (Ben Tun, 奔豚)** — A sudden rushing sensation from abdomen to chest/throat with panic, attributed to fright; resembles sympathetic hyperarousal. (Ch 2, 6, 7)

**Shen (神, spirit)** — The individual component (alongside the Heart) most affected by mental illness; "calming the spirit" is the core anxiety treatment principle. (Ch 1, throughout)

**Shen-Hammer pulse lineage** — Dr. Leon Hammer (MD/psychiatrist/TCM practitioner) and his teacher Dr. John Shen's diagnostic tradition using pulse qualities to reveal stuck qi/Blood; source of the trauma-specific "Heart Shock" pattern and formulas. (Ch 9)

**Spirit Settling herbs (Long Gu + Mu Li)** — "Heavy Spirit Settling" mineral pair: salty Mu Li (oyster shell) descends into Water phase to dissolve obstruction; bland Long Gu (dragon bone) grounds and stabilizes. (Ch 6, 7)

**Stagnation syndrome (鬱症, Yu zheng)** — A broad TCM diagnostic umbrella for congested/blocked qi manifesting as ADHD, anxiety, autism-adjacent presentations, or depression. (Ch 2, 3)

**Tian (天)** — Commonly mistranslated as "heaven"; actually means sky/universe/nature — illustrates translation-driven "mysticism" misconceptions about TCM. (Ch 1)

**Tui Na (推拿)** — Therapeutic massage/bodywork; outperformed benzodiazepines and other TCM therapies for primary insomnia in 2018/2023 meta-analyses. (Ch 4)

**Vexation and agitation** — A TCM term-cluster that can correspond to Western generalized anxiety disorder. (Ch 2, 6, 7)

**Visceral Agitation** — A classical pattern (Gan Mai Da Zao Tang's target) explaining overwhelm, sadness, and grief consistent with both depression and anxiety. (Ch 5, 6)

**Wan (丸)** — Round pill dosage form, colloquially "tea pills"; most popular TCM format. (Ch 3)

**Yang cannot enter yin** — The classical explanation for insomnia: sleep initiates when qi/blood move inward from the body's surface to the Organs; blockage prevents this. (Ch 4)

**Yangsheng (養生, "Nourishing Life")** — Self-cultivation practices (mind-body exercise, breathwork, self-massage, fasting, food therapy) for proactive health and longevity, dating to the Han dynasty. (Ch 3)

**Yao shi tong yuan (药食同源)** — "Food and medicine have the same source" — the core tenet of TCM food therapy. (Ch 3)

**Yu (郁/鬰, stagnation)** — The master concept behind most depression patterns; the character depicts a thorny thicket — an irrepressible force meeting an immovable object. (Ch 5)
