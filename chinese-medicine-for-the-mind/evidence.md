# Evidence check

**Checked**: 2026-10-28 · **Sources**: PubMed (meta-analyses, Cochrane reviews, the specific trials cited) and agency pages (FDA, PMDA, Hong Kong Department of Health, Taiwan Ministry of Health and Welfare, MSKCC) · **Full entries with DOIs, confidence and search logs**: [evidence/verif_1_efficacy_es.md](evidence/verif_1_efficacy_es.md) (items 1.x) and [evidence/verif_2_safety_es.md](evidence/verif_2_safety_es.md) (items 2.x), in Spanish. The claims as extracted from this skill are in [evidence/claims_es.md](evidence/claims_es.md).

This file checks the *empirical* claims the skill makes (trials, safety, chronobiology). It does not judge TCM theory (qi, Organs, Five Phases), which is a framework, not a testable claim. When answering from this skill, apply the notes below whenever one of these claims comes up: state the book's claim and the evidence note together.

Pattern across the efficacy claims: herbal formulas, tui na and five-element music mostly point in a favorable direction, but the trials are small, largely Chinese, often unblinded, and rated low or very low certainty. The problem is the strength of the evidence, not its direction.

## Efficacy and trials

| # | Claim in the skill (where) | Verdict | Evidence note | Key sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Xiao Yao San matched sertraline in a 2001 double-blind RCT, faster onset; "no adverse effects" across 1,837 patients (SKILL.md, ch05) | Inaccurate | The double-blind trial vs. sertraline tested *Jia Wei* Xiao Yao capsules (n=210) and was published in 2019, not 2001; faster onset was seen only for anxiety, and adverse events were *similar* in both arms. The 26-trial, 1,837-patient review says the trials *did not report* adverse effects and rates their quality as generally low. Later reviews also favor it, mainly as an add-on to antidepressants, with low-quality evidence. | Su et al. 2019, J Tradit Chin Med (PMID in verif file); Zhang et al. 2011/2012, eCAM |
| 1.2 | SSRIs are no better than placebo for ~85 % of people (ch05) | Overstated | Stone et al. 2022 pooled all antidepressants (232 FDA trials, 73,388 participants), not only SSRIs: about 15 % get a *substantial* benefit beyond placebo. That does not mean the drug "doesn't work" in the other 85 %; the mean difference was 1.75 HAMD points and grew with baseline severity. | Stone et al. 2022, BMJ |
| 1.3 | Thousands of placebo-controlled trials; large trials are rare only because herbs aren't patentable (ch01, SKILL.md) | Overstated | Of 3,159 TCM trials, only 3 % reported adequate randomization, allocation concealment and blinding; only 6.8 % of Chinese "RCTs" were truly randomized; 99 % of trials published in China were positive (publication bias). The patentability explanation is a hypothesis, not a finding. | He et al. 2011, Trials; Wu et al. 2009, Trials; Vickers et al. 1998, Control Clin Trials |
| 1.4 | Suan Zao Ren Tang + Zhi Zi Chi Tang outperformed lorazepam (2015 RCT) (ch04) | Holds up | The trial exists: 119 people with insomnia *and anxiety*, 4 weeks, better ISI, PSQI and WASO than lorazepam. One small, short trial; blinding not stated in the abstract. | Hu et al. 2015, eCAM |
| 1.5 | Tui na outperformed benzodiazepines (2018 and 2023 meta-analyses) (ch04, glossary) | Holds up, low certainty | Both meta-analyses exist and favor tui na, but all 2018 trials were at high risk of bias for blinding and a 2026 network meta-analysis rates most evidence low or very low. | J Acupunct Tuina Sci 2018; Wang et al. 2023, Front Neurosci; Tong et al. 2026 |
| 1.6 | Five Element music therapy is evidence-supported; 2018 Beijing Normal University study; Shanghai COVID-quarantine study (ch03, ch04, ch06) | Overstated | Several meta-analyses show reductions in anxiety, depression and poor sleep, all rated low or very low certainty with no blinding; one meta-analysis in cancer found no effect on anxiety. The two specific studies were not found. "Promising, low certainty" fits better than "evidence-supported". | Sun et al. 2026, Complement Ther Med; Li et al. 2025, Medicine; Yang et al. 2021 |
| 1.7 | +3 cups of tea/day → 37 % lower depression risk (2015 meta-analysis) (ch05, cheatsheet) | Correct figure, contested | RR 0.63 per 3 cups/day is correct but comes from observational studies (association, not causation). Another 2015 meta-analysis found no significant association for tea (RR 0.70; 95 % CI 0.48–1.01), and a 2023 meta-analysis found none. | Dong et al. 2015, Aust N Z J Psychiatry; Grosso et al. 2015; Torabynasab et al. 2023 |
| 1.8 | ~5 cups of coffee' worth of caffeine triggers panic; L-theanine/GABA tea is a well-evidenced anxiety adjunct (ch06) | Half true | Caffeine part holds: panic attacks in 51 % of panic-disorder patients vs. 0 % on placebo. L-theanine effects on anxiety were inconsistent and non-significant across 31 RCTs; GABA tea not checked. | Klevebrant & Frick 2021, Gen Hosp Psychiatry; Gerolymos et al. 2026, Mol Psychiatry |
| 1.9 | Feng shui: 2021 RCT on bedroom changes improving sleep; sleeping under a beam linked to heart attack (ch05, glossary, patterns) | Source not found | No 2021 RCT found. The closest study, a 2016 survey of 405 adults, found no association between feng shui layout and sleep quality. Nothing links sleeping under a beam to heart attack; treat it as traditional belief. | Hong et al. 2016, Archit Eng Des Manag |
| 1.10 | Ginger's warming effect validated in a placebo-controlled palm-temperature trial (ch03) | Overstated | The trial exists but had 6 cold-sensitive women, the effect lasted ~20 min, and the authors work in a company R&D department. Pilot data, not validation. | Sugimoto et al. 2018, eCAM |

## Safety, interactions and chronobiology

| # | Claim in the skill (where) | Verdict | Evidence note | Key sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Space formulas ≥1 h from Western medications; Xiao Yao San is safe with SSRIs if spaced ≥1 h (SKILL.md, ch05, cheatsheet) | Risk omitted | No study shows that a 1-hour gap prevents interactions; metabolic (CYP) inhibition can last far longer (up to 24 h for grapefruit and CYP3A4). Danzhi Xiaoyao pills lowered venlafaxine exposure in beagles (animal data). Japanese PMDA labels for Kamishoyosan (Jia Wei Xiao Yao San) list mesenteric phlebosclerosis with long-term Gardenia use (usually >5 years). | Zhu et al. 2019; Tsumura 2023 and Ohsugi 2022 PMDA labels |
| 2.2 | Ma Huang is safe within a balanced formula; ephedra harm came from isolation and overdose (ch03, patterns) | No human evidence | FDA banned ephedrine-alkaloid supplements in 2004 after reports of hypertension, stroke, seizures and deaths (10 deaths among 140 reports in one review). No human evidence that a formula removes ephedrine's cardiovascular risk; the FDA rule's exemption for traditional Chinese remedies was a regulatory choice, not proof of safety. | Haller & Benowitz 2000, NEJM; FDA final rule 2004 |
| 2.3 | Acetaminophen: ~56,000 ER visits/year and #1 cause of liver transplant in the US (ch03) | Partly accurate | ~56,000 ER visits/year is correct, and acetaminophen is the leading cause of *acute liver failure* (~50 % of US ALFSG cases). "#1 cause of liver transplant" overall is not confirmed. | Lee 2004; FDA 2011 |
| 2.4 | Heart attacks/arrhythmias peak 11 a.m.–1 p.m., the TCM Heart hours (SKILL.md, ch04) | Inaccurate | Myocardial infarction and sudden cardiac death peak between 6 a.m. and noon, maximum around 9 a.m. This does not match 11 a.m.–1 p.m. and does not corroborate the organ clock. (Circadian disruption and metabolic/neurodegenerative risk is a separate, real research area.) | Muller et al. 1985, NEJM; Cohen et al. 1997 |
| 2.5 | An Shen Ding Zhi Wan, Gui Pi Tang, Jia Wei Xiao Yao San are safe with a low side-effect profile; Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan for anxiety/insomnia (ch04, ch06, cheatsheet) | Risk omitted | The classical Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan contains cinnabar (Zhu Sha, mercury sulfide); many commercial versions replace it, but heavy metals and undeclared drugs have been documented in Asian herbal products, so choose cinnabar-free products with metal testing. No dedicated safety trials support "low side-effect profile". | Taiwan MOHW; MSKCC 2026; Ernst 2002 |
| 2.6 | ADHD formulas for children are "safe long-term" (Huang Lian Wen Dan Tang, Bu Nao Wan, etc.) (ch07) | Overstated | A 2022 meta-analysis (42 studies, 3,484 children) found efficacy similar to conventional drugs with fewer adverse events, but certainty was moderate to very low and no long-term pediatric safety studies were found. Berberine (Huang Lian) displaces bilirubin from albumin in vitro and in rats; avoid in jaundiced newborns. | Lee et al. 2022; Chan 1993; Fok 2001 |
| 2.7 | Adderall originated from attempts to synthesize Ma Huang; long-term stimulants cause gray-matter abnormalities (ch07) | Inaccurate | Amphetamine was synthesized by Edeleanu in 1887; Alles revisited it in 1929 while seeking an ephedrine substitute, so the link is loose. A meta-analysis associated stimulant treatment with *more normal* basal-ganglia gray matter in ADHD, not damage (longitudinal data still lacking). | Science History Institute 2012; Nakao et al. 2011, Am J Psychiatry |
| 2.8 | Yunnan Baiyao for emotional trauma, 64-capsule protocol; only blood-thinner warning (ch09, cheatsheet) | Risk omitted | Yunnan Baiyao contains Cao Wu (Aconitum root). In 2013 Hong Kong's Department of Health recalled five products for undeclared aconite alkaloids (risk of breathing problems and arrhythmia). A pharmacovigilance study (31,556 users) found mostly mild reactions in 1.17 %. No evidence was found for use in emotional trauma; warn about aconite and pregnancy, not only blood thinners. | Yao et al. 2021; Hong Kong DH 2013; Li et al. 2019 |
| 2.9 | Unsupervised qigong contraindicated in severe mental illness (ch03, ch08, cheatsheet) | Holds up | DSM-IV lists "qi-gong psychotic reaction"; poorly guided practice can precipitate psychosis in vulnerable people. Supervised qigong has been trialed as an adjunct in stable schizophrenia. "Family history" as a specific criterion is not confirmed. | Ng 1999; Shen et al. 2024 |
| 2.10 | Ginseng: 3,400+ studies; reduces neuroinflammation via NLRP3/AIM2; best-evidenced herb for brain fog (ch08) | No human evidence for the mechanism | NLRP3/AIM2 data come from mice and cell cultures, and one AIM2 study was retracted. The only human trial found in long COVID (n=216) showed less fatigue in women only, with no validated brain-fog outcome. American ginseng lowered warfarin's effect (INR) in a controlled trial. "3,400+ studies" not confirmed. | Seo et al. 2025; Yuan et al. 2004, Ann Intern Med |
| 2.11 | Footbath 40–43 °C, "24 minutes is the researched optimum"; avoid in diabetes (ch04, patterns, cheatsheet) | Partly accurate | No source gives 24 min as optimal; meta-analyses favor about 40 °C for ≤20 min (Jiang 2023) or ≤40 °C for ≥10 min (Chang 2025), so 41–43 °C exceeds the best-studied range. The diabetes warning is well founded: 22 of 68 burned diabetic patients in one burn-center series were hurt soaking their feet. | Jiang et al. 2023; Chang et al. 2025; Barsun et al. 2013 |
| 2.12 | Jun-Chen-Zuo-Shi architecture increases bioavailability and safety (SKILL.md, ch03, patterns) | No human evidence | Support comes from network-pharmacology (in silico) and rat studies; a 2020 review says its modern scientific basis "has not been clarified". Combination does not guarantee safety: herbal products with aristolochic acid caused kidney failure and urothelial cancer in Taiwan. | Ren et al. 2019/2020; Luan et al. 2020; Chen et al. 2012; Yang et al. 2014 |

## Not confirmed in this check
