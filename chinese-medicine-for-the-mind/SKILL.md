## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**No mind/body split**: TCM never separates mental from physical symptoms — every diagnosis is a *pattern* combining both (e.g., Liver Qi Stagnation = irritability/depression + irregular bowels + menstrual irregularity). Always read the full physical symptom picture (temperature of extremities, digestion, pulse) alongside the mental complaint before selecting treatment.

**Qi and the Five Organs**: Mental illness stems from qi (vital energy) being depleted, blocked, or misdirected among five metaphorical Organ systems — Heart, Lung, Spleen, Kidney, Liver (not literal anatomy). The Heart is the seat of awareness; all emotion is processed through it.

**Five Phases / Five Emotions correspondence** — the master diagnostic lookup table:
| Emotion | Organ | Direction | Qi Movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anger | Liver | East/Wood | Rising |
| Joy | Heart | South/Fire | Relaxing |
| Sadness | Lung | West/Metal | Dissipating |
| Fear | Kidney | North/Water | Descending |
| Fright | Heart-Kidney | — | Chaos/panic |
| Pensiveness | Spleen | Center/Earth | Lumping/dampening |

**"One formula, many patterns"**: A single herbal formula (e.g., Xiao Yao San family) can treat several seemingly unrelated Western diagnoses if they share a TCM pattern. Conversely, one Western diagnosis can map to multiple different TCM patterns depending on the individual's full presentation. Always diagnose the *pattern*, not the label.

**Depression vs. Anxiety — energy direction**: Depression = exhausted/immobilized qi ("stuck," an irrepressible force meeting an immovable object). Anxiety = a rush of uncontrolled qi. Same stagnation family (Yu, 郁), opposite direction. This single heuristic resolves most differential-diagnosis confusion between the two.

**Jun-Chen-Zuo-Shi formula architecture** ("Emperor-Minister-Assistant-Courier"): every well-designed formula has a main herb (Jun), an adjuvant that boosts its effect (Chen), a side-effect reducer (Zuo), and an Organ-guiding herb (Shi). This is why formulas use 2–10+ herbs instead of one "active ingredient" — isolating a single herb from its formula context (e.g., Ma Huang → Ephedra) breaks the built-in synergy/safety design and has historically caused serious harm.

**Formula-taking protocol**: warm water only (never juice/coffee — alters pharmacology); space ≥1 hour from Western medications; full dose for 4–6 weeks; expect improvement within 1–2 weeks or reassess the pattern match; taper once symptoms resolve.

**TCM Body Clock**: optimal bedtime 9–11 p.m.; be fully asleep by 11 p.m.–1 a.m. (modern chronobiology corroborates: cardiac events peak in the TCM Heart-hours; circadian fragmentation correlates with Alzheimer's/obesity/diabetes risk).

**Evidence base**: TCM has been tested in placebo-controlled trials since the 1960s (China) / 1970s (West). Several flagship formulas (esp. the Xiao Yao San family) have matched or outperformed SSRIs/benzodiazepines in RCTs with faster onset and no adverse effects found in systematic review — don't assume "traditional" means "untested."

**Multi-modal treatment**: Every condition chapter offers herbal formulas *plus* acupressure, Five Element music therapy, food therapy, qigong/daoyin, and (for some) feng shui — treatment is rarely herb-only.
