# Glossary

French culinary and technique-specific terms as they appear in *Fruits & Nuts* (FERRANDI Paris). Alphabetical.

**Bain-marie** — A hot-water bath (heatproof bowl over barely simmering water, or a water bath around a dish in the oven) for gentle, even heating of things that scorch or curdle over direct heat: chocolate, custards, mousses, flans. (Ch. 4, Ch. 11)

**Baba** — A yeasted cake soaked in flavored syrup after baking (e.g. rum baba); here reworked with spiced orange syrup. (Ch. 5)

**Beurre noisette** — Butter cooked until it turns golden-brown and smells nutty ("brown butter"), then strained; brushed onto the Taggiasca Olive Tapenade Swirls before baking. (Ch. 6)

**Biscuits roses de Reims** — Pink champagne biscuits from Reims, used to line the Blackberry Charlotte instead of standard ladyfingers. (Ch. 8)

**Brunoise** — A very fine dice, roughly 2 mm (1/8 in); used throughout for apple, mango, and pear garnishes. (Ch. 6, 7, 8)

**Bûche** — A Yule-log-shaped cake/mold; used for the Grapefruit Yule Log and, unusually, for the Black Forest Gâteau. (Ch. 5, 6)

**Calisson** — A diamond-shaped Provençal candy of ground almonds, candied fruit, and orange blossom water, topped with royal icing. (Ch. 10)

**Candissoire** — A lidded candying pan used for multi-day staged fruit candying (e.g. kumquats). (Ch. 1, Ch. 4)

**Canelling / canelle knife** — Cutting decorative parallel grooves into citrus/firm fruit skin with a channel (canelle) knife before slicing, for a striped presentation. (Ch. 3)

**Charlotte** — A molded chilled dessert lined with sponge fingers or biscuits and filled with a mousse. (Ch. 8)

**Chausson** — A folded, filled puff-pastry turnover (here, greengage plum). (Ch. 6)

**Compote** — Fruit gently stewed with sugar (and often spices) to a soft, chunky preserve; distinct from a smooth jam. (Ch. 4)

**Confit** (as a noun, in this book) — A fruit reduced/cooked down with sugar to a thick, spreadable consistency, used as a component within a larger dessert (e.g. mango confit, guava inserts, prickly pear filling). Not to be confused with the savory meat-preservation method.

**Coulis** — A smooth, strained fresh fruit sauce/puree, usually raw or barely cooked. (Ch. 2)

**Craquelin** — A crisp sugar-butter-flour topping piped or rolled onto choux pastry before baking, for a crackled, crunchy shell. (Ch. 6)

**Crémet d'Anjou** — A Loire Valley specialty: whipped cream folded with stiffly beaten egg white, molded in cheesecloth to drain and set into a light, airy "cheese"-style dessert (no actual cheese involved). (Ch. 10)

**Crémeux** — A rich, pourable-to-spoonable custard-based cream (thicker than a sauce, softer than a mousse), typically egg-yolk- and gelatin-set; used as a filling/insert layer throughout ch. 5-10.

**Dacquoise** — A meringue-based sponge made with ground nuts (usually almond or hazelnut), used as a cake layer or a decorative crusted shell. (Ch. 10)

**Décoction (decoction)** — An infusion made by simmering aromatics in liquid to extract flavor, then straining. (Ch. 5, Langoustine recipe)

**Détrempe** — The basic flour-water-salt-butter dough ("water dough") that forms the base of laminated puff pastry, before the butter block is folded in. (Ch. 5, 6)

**Diplomat cream** — Pastry cream lightened with whipped cream (and often set with gelatin); used as a filling across several composed desserts. (Ch. 5, 7, 8)

**Fraisage** — The technique of smearing shortcrust dough against the work surface with the heel of the hand to finish bringing it together smoothly, without overworking the gluten. (Ch. 6)

**Fraisier** — The classic French strawberry layer cake (genoise, mousseline cream, whole strawberries). (Ch. 8)

**Ganache montée** ("whipped ganache") — A ganache aerated by whipping once chilled, for a lighter mousse-like texture; used in fillings like the Hazelnut Spheres. (Ch. 10)

**Gianduja** — A smooth hazelnut-and-chocolate paste (roasted hazelnuts ground with sugar, blended with melted chocolate), the base for many nut-and-chocolate fillings. (Ch. 4)

**Historier** — Cutting a fruit (melon, citrus, kiwifruit) with an alternating zigzag "V" incision all the way around, so it separates into two decoratively edged halves; French for a "fruit crown." (Ch. 3)

**Joconde sponge** — A thin, flexible almond sponge cake, often used rolled (Yule logs) or layered in entremets. (Ch. 5, 8, 9)

**Julienne** — A fine matchstick cut. (Ch. 3)

**Kasha** — Toasted buckwheat groats; used here as a crunchy exterior coating pressed onto a glazed cake roll, not in its usual savory-grain role. (Ch. 9)

**Marmalade** — A citrus preserve made from whole fruit pieces (peel included), distinguished from jam/jelly by using the fruit's peel and pith along with the flesh and juice. (Ch. 4)

**Mendiant** — A disk of chocolate studded with nuts/dried fruit. (Ch. 10, Cashew Nut Mendiants)

**Mirepoix cut** (in this book's fruit context) — An even large dice, about 1 cm, used for fruit salads. (Ch. 3) — note: distinct from the classic savory mirepoix (carrot/onion/celery) usage of the term elsewhere in French cooking.

**Mousseline cream** — Pastry cream enriched by beating in softened butter at a matching temperature, for a light, spreadable, stable cream (classic fraisier filling). (Ch. 8)

**Opaline** — A thin, brittle, often flavored disc of cooked sugar/fondant, used as a crisp garnish. (Ch. 8)

**Pain aux raisins** ("raisin swirl") — A laminated viennoiserie: croissant dough rolled around a raisin pastry cream, sliced into spirals. (Ch. 10)

**Pâte à glacer** — A "glazing paste" (a fluid, coconut-oil/fat-based chocolate coating) used to give a shiny, easy-to-set finish or to enrich a spread. (Ch. 4)

**Pavlova** — A meringue-shell dessert filled with cream and fruit (not a French term, but used in the book). (Ch. 8)

**Praline** (paste) — Caramelized nuts (almond and/or hazelnut, or other nuts) ground to a smooth paste; a base ingredient for fillings, ganaches, and spreads throughout ch. 4 and ch. 5-10.

**Quatre-épices** — A French "four-spice" blend (typically pepper, clove, nutmeg, ginger or cinnamon); substitutable with allspice or pumpkin pie spice. (Ch. 7, Tarte Tatin)

**Religieuse** — A stacked double choux-pastry pastry (traditionally coffee or chocolate), reinterpreted here with nectarine and lemon verbena. (Ch. 6)

**Sabayon** — A warm, airy whisked egg-yolk-and-sugar foam, classically made with a sweet wine (here, champagne); can be stabilized with gelatin and siphon-aerated for a cold plated version. (Ch. 9)

**Saint-Tropez crumbs** — A crumbled butter-sugar-flour topping associated with the *tarte tropézienne*, used here on a rhubarb tart. (Ch. 8)

**Streusel** — A crumbled topping/component of butter, sugar, flour (or nut flour); used here as a chocolate streusel disk. (Ch. 10)

**Tuile** — A thin, crisp baked wafer, often shaped while warm (draped over a rolling pin, etc.). (Ch. 6, 10)

**Umeboshi** — A salted, pickled Japanese plum; used here as a sharp, salty-sour counterpoint set into jelly cubes within a lychee dessert. (Ch. 9)

**Vacherin** — A layered dessert of meringue, ice cream, and whipped cream (classically also the name of a French cheese, unrelated here). (Ch. 7)
