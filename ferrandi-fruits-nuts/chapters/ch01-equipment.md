# Chapter 1: Equipment

Source: FERRANDI Paris, *Fruits & Nuts*, Techniques > Equipment (book pp. 18-23). This section is a captioned photo spread of the school's standard kit for fruit and nut work — reference list only, no procedures.

## Molds, Pans, and Rings
- **Cake, pastry, and confectionery rings and frames** — for tarts, entremets, and framing pâte de fruits/jellies to a set thickness.
- **Candissoire** — a candying pan with a lid, used for multi-day staged candying (see *Candying Kumquats*, ch. 4).
- **Stainless steel cooling rack** — for draining, drying, and cooling candied or glazed fruit.
- **Piston funnel** — for portioning liquid fillings/purees precisely.
- **Flexible silicone molds** — for chocolates, bonbons, and small entremets.

## Hand Tools — Peeling, Coring, Zesting
- **Ice cream scoop**
- **Chocolate dipping fork** — for dipping candied fruit, bonbons in glaze/chocolate.
- **Grapefruit knife** — curved, serrated blade for segmenting citrus and working pineapple flesh free of the skin (see *Cutting Pineapples Method 2*).
- **Melon baller** — for fruit balls (melon, papaya, kiwi, dragon fruit).
- **Peeler (vegetable peeler)**
- **Zester with a channel knife** — combination tool: fine zest plus a canelle groove for decorative citrus striping.
- **Apple corer**

## Knives and Measuring Tools
- **Refractometer** — measures sugar concentration (°Brix) in syrups, jams, sorbet bases; essential for hitting the precise sugar targets called out throughout the Cooking chapter.
- **Digital instant-read thermometer** — for syrup/candy stage temperatures.
- **Bird's beak (tourné) paring knife** — curved blade for turning and detail peeling work.
- **Paring knife**
- **Microplane grater** — fine zesting.
- **Serrated knife** — for soft/delicate fruit (tomatoes, citrus) without crushing.
- **Chef's knife**
- **Palette knife**
- **Scraper (bench scraper/pastry scraper)**
- **Offset spatula** — for spreading and lifting delicate items (glazed chestnuts, jellies).
- **Flexible spatula**
- **Exoglass (heatproof) spatula** — for hot sugar work and cooking jams/pastes.
- **Whisk**
- **Skimmer** — lifting fruit/foam from hot liquid or syrup.
- **Fine-mesh sieve or strainer** — straining coulis, juice, syrups.
- **China cap (conical sieve/strainer)** — for high-volume straining (jellies, marmalades).

## Electrical Appliances
- **Immersion (stick) blender** — pureeing coulis, sorbet bases, sauces.
- **Stand mixer** with paddle beater, whisk, and dough hook attachments.
- **Food processor** — grinding nuts for praline/gianduja pastes, blending purees.

## How to Use This Chapter
Cross-reference: whenever a technique or recipe entry lists "Equipment," these are the standard items it draws from. The refractometer and instant-read thermometer are the two tools that show up most often in Cooking-chapter techniques (ch. 4) — precise Brix and temperature targets are how the book gets consistent results in jams, jellies, syrups, and candying.
