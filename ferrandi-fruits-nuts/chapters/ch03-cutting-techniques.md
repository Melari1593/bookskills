# Chapter 3: Cutting Techniques

Source: FERRANDI Paris, *Fruits & Nuts*, Techniques > Cutting (book pp. 52-71).
Twelve knife techniques covering everyday cuts and decorative presentation cuts.

## Slicing
**For**: Simple even crosswise slices — apples, pears, oranges, lemons, limes, etc.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife (or a mandoline for firm fruit like apples, pears, quinces).
**Method**: Cut the fruit crosswise into equally sized slices, as thick or thin as the use requires.

## Julienne Cut
**For**: Fine matchstick cuts, e.g. apple for garnish or salad.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife.
**Key parameters**: Slices ~1/16 in (2 mm) thick, cut into matchsticks ~1/16 in (2 mm) wide.
**Method**: Slice the fruit into thin, even slices; stack several slices and cut crosswise into matchsticks of the same width.
**Chef's notes**: Toss the matchsticks in lemon juice if not using immediately, to prevent browning.

## Mirepoix Cut
**For**: Even large dice for fruit salads and similar preparations — mangos, pears, melons, peaches, etc.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife.
**Key parameters**: Slices ~1/2 in (1 cm) thick, diced to ~1/2 in (1 cm) cubes.
**Method**: Peel the fruit, cut into slices of the target thickness, then cut the slices into even cubes.

## Preparing Fruit Balls
**For**: Decorative round scoops for plated desserts, fruit salads, or skewers — melons, papayas, kiwifruits, pitayas (dragon fruit), etc.
**Equipment**: Melon baller.
**Method**: Press the melon baller into the fruit's flesh and twist with the wrist to scoop out a round ball.

## Cutting Mangos
**For**: Getting clean flesh off the flat, fibrous mango pit.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, paring knife.
**Method**:
1. Cut off the base to make the mango stand flat; stand it upright.
2. Cut lengthwise through the flesh along one side of the pit, then repeat on the other side, freeing two "cheeks."
3. Cut the skin off the remaining central piece surrounding the pit.
4. Remove as much flesh as possible from the pit, then cut off the rest in sections with the paring knife, following the pit's curve.
5. Cut the two flesh halves into quarters and peel the quarters.

## Cutting Pineapples (Method 1)
**For**: Neat pineapple spears/slices with minimal waste — requires the fruit to be peeled first (see *Peeling Pineapples*, ch. 2).
**Equipment**: Chef's knife.
**Method**: Cut the peeled pineapple in half lengthwise, then each half into quarters lengthwise. Cut out the hard core (edible but tough). Slice the quarters lengthwise into spears, or crosswise into rounds, as needed.

## Cutting Pineapples (Method 2)
**For**: A faster way to serve pineapple, including in its own hollowed-out shell — trades a little flesh yield for speed.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, grapefruit knife.
**Method**: Cut off about two-thirds of the crown; halve the pineapple lengthwise. With the grapefruit knife, cut into the flesh about 1/2 in (1 cm) from the skin, following the fruit's curve, and work the blade under the flesh to remove it from each half in one piece. Cut each flesh half in two lengthwise and remove the core with the chef's knife. Slice as required; the empty skins can be used as serving shells.
**Trade-off vs Method 1**: quicker, but loses more usable flesh.

## Cutting and Seeding Pomegranates
**For**: Removing pomegranate arils (seeds) intact, without crushing them or staining everything with juice.
**Equipment**: Paring knife.
**Method**: Score and remove a "hat" from the bottom of the fruit. Cut several vertical slits through the skin following the fruit's natural internal sections; remove the central white core attached to the calyx. Pull the fruit apart by hand along the scored sections. Hold each section over a bowl and gently separate the seeds from the membrane with your fingers, letting them fall into the bowl.

## Canelling
**For**: A decorative striped cut, mainly on citrus and other firm untreated fruit.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, channel (canelle) knife.
**Method**: Slice off the top and bottom of the fruit. With the channel knife, remove vertical strips of zest at regular intervals around the fruit (creating a striped pattern). For a decorative presentation, halve the fruit lengthwise, remove seeds, then slice crosswise into thin rounds showing the stripes.

## Cutting Citrus Zest into Thin Strips
**For**: Fine julienned zest for garnish, infusing, or candying — without the bitter white pith.
**Equipment**: Vegetable brush, vegetable peeler, paring knife, chef's knife.
**Key parameters**: Strips cut ~1/16-1/12 in (1-2 mm) wide.
**Method**: Scrub the fruit in warm water with the vegetable brush. Cut off both ends. Peel the zest off in wide strips with the vegetable peeler. Lay each strip flat, zest-side down, and trim away any white pith with the paring knife. Overlap/stack the cleaned strips and slice into thin, long strips with the chef's knife.
**Chef's notes**: Use untreated, preferably organic fruit for zest. Remove all pith — it is very bitter.

## Peeling and Segmenting Citrus Fruits (Supreming)
**For**: Clean, membrane-free citrus segments — for salads, plating, garnishes.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, paring knife.
**Method**: Cut off the top and bottom of the fruit; stand it upright. Cut away the peel (zest, pith, and membrane together) following the fruit's curve to expose the flesh. Trim off any remaining pith with the paring knife. Holding the fruit over a bowl (to catch the juice), cut in along each side of a membrane to release each segment. Squeeze the leftover membrane by hand to extract remaining juice.

## Preparing a Fruit Crown (*Historier*)
**For**: A decorative zigzag-cut fruit, split into two show pieces — melons, citrus, kiwifruit.
**Equipment**: Paring knife.
**Method**: Lightly score a line crosswise around the center of the fruit as a cutting guide. Insert the knife tip on this line at an angle, pushing all the way to the center; remove the blade and make a second cut at the opposite angle to form a "V" notch. Continue this alternating-angle cut all the way around the fruit to build a zigzag seam. Pull the two halves apart. If it's a fruit like melon, scoop the seeds/membrane out of each half with a spoon once separated.
