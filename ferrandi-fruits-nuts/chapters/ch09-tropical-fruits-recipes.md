# Chapter 9: Tropical Fruits — Recipe Index

Source: FERRANDI Paris, *Fruits & Nuts*, Recipes > Tropical Fruits (book pp. 230-259). 15 recipes covering pineapple, papaya, banana, dragon fruit, prickly pear, tamarillo, mangosteen, guava, rambutan, mango, coconut, lychee, cape gooseberry, and pomegranate.

## Frozen Pineapple Soufflés — *Soufflé glacé à l'ananas*
Serves 4.
Components: paper-thin dried pineapple chips (dried 12 hrs at 115°F/45°C), toasted almond crumbs, a pineapple-ginger-vanilla-rum frozen soufflé base (fruit puree + meringue), candied pineapple marmalade (diced pineapple, honey, coconut sugar, vanilla, lime).
Notable: uses the classic frozen-soufflé illusion trick — a paper/acetate collar raised above the dish rim, filled so it "rises" above the ramekin, then the collar is removed to mimic a baked soufflé's puff. Also molds an acetate cylinder into the center before freezing to leave a hollow core.

## Papaya and Ginger Carpaccio with Champagne Sabayon — *Carpaccio de papaye et gingembre, sabayon au champagne*
Serves 4.
Components: dried papaya "tuiles" (puree dried 24 hrs into a thin sheet), a papaya-and-chia confit (chia soaked/swollen 12 hrs), a gelatin-set, siphon-aerated champagne sabayon, pickled green papaya julienne (blanched in a ginger-lime syrup), raw papaya carpaccio (mandolined thin, dressed in olive oil and lime).

## Banana, Milk Chocolate, and Speculaas Gâteau — *Entremets banane, chocolat lait et spéculoos*
Serves 6. A full multi-layer entremet.
Components: a speculaas crumb crust (blond chocolate + speculaas crumbs + feuillantine flakes + toasted pecans), banana sponge, banana crémeux, banana compote, a tempered chocolate triangle decoration, milk chocolate mousse, dried banana chips, neutral glaze.
Notable: freeze at least 4.5 hrs before glazing/serving — a standard requirement for entremets assembled in a ring so layers hold their shape when unmolded.

## Piton Tropical Fruit Volcanoes — *Piton de la Fournaise*
Serves 6. Named for the active volcano on Réunion Island.
Components: an agar-agar passion fruit jelly (set in a dome/mold, scored with a cross on top so the fruit inside shows through — the "volcano"), a fresh tropical fruit salad (mango, pineapple, kiwifruit, papaya, dragon fruit, lychee, diced ~4 mm), raw passion fruit seeds folded in for crunch.
Draws on: *Cutting Mangos* (ch. 3, p. 56), *Peeling Pineapples* (ch. 2, p. 28).

## Lychee Harmony — *L'harmonie au litchi*
Serves 6.
Components: red currant sorbet, lychee crémeux (custard finished with butter, cooked past 180°F/82°C), umeboshi (salted Japanese pickled plum) jelly cubes, a red currant and pink champagne sorbet, ring-shaped shortbread (cut with two round cutters).
Notable: umeboshi is used as a savory-sour counterpoint inside a fruit dessert — an unusual cross-cultural pairing worth remembering for balancing very sweet fruit (lychee) against something sharply acidic/salty.

## Mango with Coconut Rings from the Levant — *Mangue au pays du Levant*
Serves 6.
Components: gelatin-stabilized mango sorbet (matured 12 hrs, cut into two sizes of disks), coconut tapioca rings (coconut milk + tapioca pearls simmered 35 min, folded with whipped cream, frozen in ring shape), a pectin-set mango confit/brunoise, fresh mango, gold leaf.

## Coconut Squares Infused with Lemongrass — *Carré tout coco, infusion citronnelle*
Makes 16. One of the most technically dense recipes in the book.
Components: coconut sorbet squares, whipped coconut ganache, coconut shortbread crumbs, coconut whipped cream squares, coconut sponge, a coconut-and-lemongrass gel, coconut ganache, a white glaze, white chocolate velvet spray.
Notable: nearly every component is frozen separately in its own confectionery frame, then the finished squares are stacked and plated at a 45° angle — a good reference for building a modular multi-texture "study of one ingredient" dessert.

## Dragon Fruit Gâteau — *Gâteau pitaya*
Serves 6.
Components: Swiss meringue, white-tea crémeux (tea-infused custard, butter-finished), joconde sponge, a tea jelly layered with diced fresh dragon fruit, a dragon fruit mousse made from fresh-juiced dragon fruit trimmings.
Chef's notes: pairs well with a white tea from the Colombian Andes.

## Chocolate and Prickly Pear Bonbons — *Bonbons chocolat figue de barbarie*
Makes 56. Classic filled-bonbon technique.
Components: tempered dark-chocolate shells piped into polycarbonate molds, a prickly-pear filling (juice extracted with a juicer — wear thick gloves for the thorns — reduced to 265°F/130°C, then tequila added to arrest cooking and flavor the ganache), sealed with more tempered chocolate.
Draws on: chocolate tempering curve (referenced from ch. 5, p. 140-style method).

## Tea-Poached Tamarillos with Matcha Cream — *Tamarillo poché au thé et crème matcha*
Serves 4.
Components: gelatin-stabilized matcha whipped cream (matcha powder + mascarpone), tamarillos poached 12-15 min in a spiced tea syrup (ginger, kaffir lime, vanilla), peeled while still warm with the stems left on for presentation.

## Sri Lankan Honeymoon with Mangosteen — *Mangoustan, lune de miel au Sri Lanka*
Serves 6. One of the most elaborate composed plates in the book.
Components: mangosteen sorbet, pectin-set mangosteen fruit jellies, mangosteens marinated in vanilla syrup (12 hrs), an agar-agar black tea jelly poured over a ginkgo-leaf stencil for a decorative shape, vanilla crémeux, coconut sorbet, cashew praline paste, black-tea-and-mace shortbread, homemade vanilla powder (whole dried pods ground in a food processor).
Notable: two reusable decorative techniques — a leaf-shaped stencil for a poured jelly, and grinding whole dried vanilla pods into a garnish powder instead of discarding spent pods.

## Mini Rolled Guava Cakes — *Petits gâteaux roulés à la goyave*
Makes 6.
Components: rolled joconde sponge, pectin-set guava inserts, a toasted-hazelnut sweet shortcrust base, tea-infused guava Bavarian cream, a pink-tinted white chocolate coating with toasted buckwheat groats (*kasha*) pressed on for crunch, white chocolate disks, guava glaze.
Notable: toasted buckwheat groats as a crunchy exterior coating is an unusual grain-as-garnish technique, applicable to any glazed roll or bonbon needing textural contrast.

## Rambutan Mochi — *Mochi au ramboutan*
Makes 6. Japanese mochi technique applied to a Southeast Asian fruit.
Components: rambutan sorbet (fruit pureed with syrup, matured 12 hrs, churned, frozen very firm before wrapping), mochi dough (glutinous rice flour + cornstarch + sugar + water, steamed 30 min, butter kneaded in while warm), toasted glutinous rice flour (toasted 10 min at 325°F/160°C) used to dust the work surface so the dough doesn't stick.
Notable: toasting the rice flour used for dusting (rather than using it raw) adds flavor and is standard mochi-making practice worth carrying over to any similar wrapped-dough dessert.

## Cape Gooseberry Salsa Verde with Yellowtail Gravlax — *Salsa verde aux physalis, gravlax de yellowtail*
Serves 4. Savory, Japanese-influenced.
Components: cured yellowtail (salt/sugar/lime zest cure 2 hrs, rinsed, marinated in mirin and soy, torch-seared), a spiced cucumber jus (herb/spice-infused, strained through cheesecloth), a cape gooseberry salsa verde (cape gooseberries, onion, shiso, honey, coconut milk, yuzu, sake, wasabi), a cape-gooseberry tangy vinaigrette (agar-agar/xanthan-stabilized), shiso-wasabi "pearls" (gelatin mixture dropped into chilled oil for spherification).
Draws on: the cold-oil-drop spherification method also used for apple caviar (ch. 7).

## Poached Oysters with Pomegranate Sauce Vierge — *Fines de claire juste pochées, vierge de grenade*
Serves 5. Savory.
Components: a pomegranate sauce vierge (diced apple, peeled seedless grapes, pomegranate seeds, lime juice, pomegranate juice, pumpkin seed oil, olive oil), oysters opened by steam oven or oyster knife, then gently poached in their own liquor at **~149°F/65°C just until the edges curl** — a very low, precise poaching temperature that keeps the oyster from turning rubbery.
Draws on: *Cutting and Seeding Pomegranates* (ch. 3, p. 62).
