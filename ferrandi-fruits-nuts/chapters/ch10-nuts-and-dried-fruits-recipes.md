# Chapter 10: Nuts and Dried Fruits — Recipe Index

Source: FERRANDI Paris, *Fruits & Nuts*, Recipes > Nuts and Dried Fruits (book pp. 262-295). 16 recipes covering hazelnuts, pistachios, almonds, walnuts, pecans, macadamias, Brazil nuts, cashews, peanuts, chestnuts, dates, raisins, prunes, dried figs, dried apricots, and goji berries.

## Hazelnut Spheres with Molten Praline Centers — *Sphère noisette, ganache montée et praliné coulant*
Makes 10.
Components: dry-caramel hazelnut praline "marbles" (infused with cocoa butter 24 hrs), a praline crémeux, hazelnut-buckwheat chocolate streusel disks, a gelatin-stabilized whipped hazelnut ganache.
Notable: assembled as nested half-spheres (three mold sizes) so the finished ball has a deliberately "molten" liquid-praline center once cut — a reusable architecture for any filled-sphere plated dessert.

## Pistachio, Praline, and Chocolate Bonbons — *Bonbons à la pâte pistache et d'amandes, praliné, ganache*
Makes 130. A high-volume production technique.
Components: rolled pistachio-and-almond paste, a pistachio praline layer (blanched almonds + pistachios in dry caramel, ground to paste), chocolate-pistachio ganache, tempered chocolate square toppers, halved pistachio garnish.
Notable: everything is layered as one large slab in a big confectionery frame, then cut into ~130 small squares at the end — much more efficient than shaping bonbons individually.

## Calissons
Makes about 20. The classic Provençal almond candy.
Components: a calisson base (confectioners' sugar, almond flour, orange blossom water cooked to a paste, blended with candied orange, lemon, and melon), rested on rice paper for **72 hours**, topped with a thin royal icing.
Key parameter: the icing must be cut into diamonds within **1-2 minutes** of spreading — it sets and hardens fast, and past that window it will crack instead of cutting cleanly.

## Walnut Tartlets — *Tartelettes aux noix*
Makes 6.
Components: walnut financier base, a gelatin-set coffee mousse, coffee-and-walnut praline (blanched almonds + walnuts in dry caramel), coffee-walnut shortbread, walnut tuiles, coffee glaze.
Draws on: *Blanching Almonds* (ch. 2, p. 30).

## Caramel Pecan Brownies
Components: dry-caramelized pecans (sugar cooked to a sandy crystallized stage, then re-melted to coat the nuts), a dark chocolate-walnut-oil brownie batter, a soft fleur-de-sel caramel piped in diagonal lines over the baked brownie before cutting into bars.
Draws on: the dry-caramel nut method from *Making Almond and Hazelnut Praline Paste* (ch. 4, p. 116).

## Macadamia Nougat — *Nougat aux noix de macadamia*
Makes 32 bars. Classic French nougat technique.
Key parameters: honey cooked to **265°F/130°C**, poured into whipping egg whites (Italian-meringue method); meringue then beaten down to **140°F/60°C** before folding in the nuts.
Method: toasted macadamia halves are folded in **while still warm** (not fully cooled) — critical, or the nuts break apart in the stiff nougat base when overmixed. Set between rice paper sheets in a frame; rests 24 hrs before cutting. Storage: up to 2 months airtight.

## Brazil Nut Bars — *Barre aux noix du Brésil*
Makes 10-12.
Components: Brazil nut shortbread base, a gelatin-stabilized whipped praline ganache, a soft caramel layer, a full dark chocolate coating (bars speared on toothpicks and dipped whole).

## Cashew Nut Mendiants — *Mendiants aux noix de cajou*
Makes 6. The simplest candy technique in this chapter — a good entry point.
Components: caramelized cashews (syrup cooked to 270°F/130°C, then crystallized/re-melted around the nuts), tempered dark chocolate piped into rings and topped with the caramelized nuts, set at least 2 hrs.

## Peanut Chicken — *Cuisse de poulet aux cacahuètes*
Serves 8. Savory.
Components: boneless chicken thighs marinated overnight (sugar, soy sauce, oyster sauce, sesame oil, ginger), roasted, served in a woven banana-leaf "basket" held together with bamboo skewers, garnished with toasted peanuts, toasted sesame seeds, blanched snow peas.
Notable: the banana-leaf-basket presentation (shaped and pinned with skewers) is a reusable low-cost serving vessel technique for any Southeast-Asian-style dish.

## Mont Blanc Pyramids — *Mont Blanc en pyramide*
Makes 3 pyramids, each serving 2. The most architecturally ambitious recipe in the book.
Components: gelatin-set chestnut mousse, pectin-set black currant confit, chestnut shortbread, chestnut sponge, a reduced-sugar meringue, whipped cream, roughly chopped candied chestnuts, a sprayed chocolate velvet finish.
Draws on: *Making Chestnut Spread* (ch. 4, p. 119-120), *Glazing Chestnuts* (ch. 4, p. 89).
Notable: the pyramid mold itself is hand-built by taping food-grade cardboard triangles together — a cheap, reusable DIY-mold technique for any faceted shape.

## Orange Blossom and Date Macarons
Components: Italian-meringue macaron shells (sugar syrup to **243°F/117°C** poured into whipping whites), some shells painted with an activated-charcoal-and-alcohol (rum or kirsch) wash for a decorative pattern, an orange blossom white chocolate filling, a date paste mixed with chopped almonds.
Chef's notes: mash the dates **by hand with a fork**, not in a food processor or electric mixer — machine-processing can turn the paste an unappetizing pale/white color.

## Raisin Swirls — *Pain aux raisins*
Makes 12. Classic French viennoiserie.
Components: a raisin pastry cream (rum-soaked raisins pureed directly into the custard base, not just scattered on top), laminated croissant dough (a single turn followed by a "book"/double turn), rolled and sliced into swirls, proofed in steam, baked, brushed with a simple-syrup glaze.
Chef's notes: extra rum-soaked raisins can be sprinkled over the swirls after baking, before glazing, for more raisin flavor.

## Monkfish Tagine with Prunes and Blood Orange Broth — *Tajine de lotte aux pruneaux, bouillon d'orange sanguine*
Serves 4. Savory.
Components: classically turned (*tourné*) carrots and potatoes, an aromatic broth (onion, garlic, ginger, star anise, cinnamon, Sichuan peppercorn, turmeric, cumin), monkfish gently steamed just 8-10 minutes to avoid overcooking, prunes, served in a tagine dish with sliced almonds, cilantro, and dried chili shreds.
Notable: the fish is removed from the broth immediately after its short cook time specifically to stop carryover cooking — timing discipline matters more than technique complexity here.

## Dried Fig Shortbread Cookies — *Sablés de randonnée à la figue séchée*
Makes 6. Literally "hiking shortbreads" — designed as a portable trail snack/energy cookie.
Components: Breton shortbread baked in rings, an almond-and-pumpkin-seed dry-caramel praline paste, a fig caramel (cream + fig puree + dry caramel), dried fig strips as garnish.

## Dried Apricot Loaf Cake — *Cake aux abricots secs*
Serves 6.
Components: a dried apricot jam (microwave-softened dried apricots blended with apricot puree), a butter cake batter folded with the jam and chopped dried apricots, an almond dacquoise shell lining the loaf pan (sliced almonds pressed into the buttered pan before piping in dacquoise batter, for a patterned exterior crust once unmolded), apricot glaze, candied apricot strips.
Notable: pressing almonds into the buttered pan before batter goes in is a reusable technique for giving any loaf or cake a decorative nut-crusted exterior.

## Crémet d'Anjou and Goji Berry Bowls — *Crémet d'Anjou aux baies de goji*
Serves 4.
Components: goji berries soaked overnight in orange juice (until plump — add more juice if they still look dry), a classic *crémet d'Anjou* (a Loire Valley specialty: whipped cream folded with stiffly whipped egg white, set in cheesecloth-lined molds or small cheese strainers/*faisselles* to drain and firm), toasted sesame seeds, baby cress.
Notable: crémet d'Anjou is a regional French fresh "cheese" made without any actual cheese — the cheesecloth-lined-mold draining technique is the whole trick and is transferable to any airy cream dessert that needs to hold a firm unmolded shape.
