# Chapter 4: Cooking Techniques

Source: FERRANDI Paris, *Fruits & Nuts*, Techniques > Cooking (book pp. 74-123).
Seventeen techniques: heat treatments, preserving (jams/jellies/marmalade/candying), and confectionery pastes (gianduja, praline, chestnut spread).

## Blanching Citrus Fruits
**For**: Softening citrus and drawing out bitterness before candying or making marmalade.
**Equipment**: A round wire rack sized to the pan (to keep fruit submerged), skimmer.
**Key parameters**: Boil 5 minutes.
**Method**: Submerge whole fruit in a pot of water, weight down with the rack so it stays under. Bring to a boil, boil 5 min, then lift out with the skimmer straight into ice water to stop cooking.

## Poaching Pears
**For**: Classic whole poached pears (e.g. Poires Belle Hélène) with a fruit-flavored syrup.
**Equipment**: Vegetable brush, stainless steel mesh (for smoothing the skin), large oven-safe saucepan, immersion blender, fine-mesh sieve.
**Key parameters**: Poach in a 175°F/80°C oven (lowest setting) for 45 minutes, covered with a parchment cartouche (paper lid with a center hole).
**Method**: Peel pears (keep stems), flatten bases, hold in lemon water to prevent browning; scrub gently with the steel mesh for a smooth finish. Make syrup: blend water, raspberries, sugar, and split vanilla bean, strain, bring to a boil, pour over the pears. Cover with a cartouche and poach in a low oven ~45 min. Cool in the syrup, then stand upright and pour syrup over to serve.
**Chef's notes**: Smoothing the skin with steel mesh isn't essential but gives a more even, professional finish.

## Roasting Pineapples
**For**: A whole roasted pineapple basted in a spiced butter mixture.
**Equipment**: Paring knife, roasting pan, foil (to protect the crown).
**Key parameters**: Oven 350°F/180°C, roast 30 minutes, basting every 5 minutes.
**Method**: Peel the pineapple (crown wrapped in foil to protect it), brush with vanilla butter, push vanilla-bean pieces into the flesh, pour over warmed honey-rum-orange juice mixture, and roast, basting with pan juices every 5 minutes.
**Chef's notes**: Frequent basting is what makes the fruit tender and deeply flavored — don't skip it.

## Candying Kumquats
**For**: Fully candying small whole citrus (kumquats) — a slow, staged sugar build-up so the fruit doesn't collapse or turn leathery.
**Equipment**: Toothpick, instant-read thermometer, candissoire (candying pan with lid), skimmer.
**Key parameters**: Total candying time **7 days**. Initial poach at 185°F/85°C for 15 min; sugar syrup concentration is increased daily by adding more sugar and reheating to 185°F/85°C, repeated over several overnight soaks.
**Method**: Pierce both ends of each fruit with a toothpick (helps syrup penetrate). Poach in a sugar+glucose syrup at 185°F/85°C for 15 min. Transfer fruit to the candissoire, cover with syrup, soak overnight at room temperature. Each following day: drain syrup, add more sugar, reheat to 185°F/85°C, pour back over fruit, soak overnight again — repeated in stages across the week until fully candied. Store in the syrup, refrigerated, up to 3 months.
**Chef's notes**: The gradual, staged increase in sugar concentration is what prevents the fruit from shriveling — jumping straight to a high-sugar syrup would pull water out too fast.

## Glazing Chestnuts
**For**: Coating candied chestnuts (*marrons glacés*-style) in a shiny fondant-like glaze.
**Equipment**: Silicone mat, dipping fork.
**Key parameters**: Glaze warmed via bain-marie; oven 400°F/210°C for a 1-minute warm-up and ~40 seconds to set the glaze.
**Method**: Dry candied chestnuts on a rack 12 hours. Warm chestnut syrup + rum over a bain-marie, whisk in confectioners' sugar to a thick-but-fluid glaze (thin with more syrup if needed). Warm chestnuts briefly in the oven, dip each in the glaze with the fork, let excess drip off, then return to a hot oven briefly to set (glaze should granulate slightly on the mat).

## Frying Pineapple Fritters
**For**: Beer-batter-style deep-fried pineapple rounds.
**Equipment**: Round cookie cutters (one for shaping, a smaller one for the core), instant-read thermometer.
**Key parameters**: Fry oil at 347-355°F/175-180°C; fry ~2 minutes per side (4 min/batch total).
**Method**: Cut unpeeled pineapple into 1 cm rounds, use the large cutter + paring knife to trim the skin into even rounds, use the small cutter to punch out the core. Make a vanilla batter (flour, sugar, baking powder, egg, milk added in two additions, rum). Dip rounds in batter, fry until golden on both sides, drain, dust with confectioners' or vanilla sugar, serve immediately.

## Making an Apple Compote
**For**: A basic stewed-fruit compote technique, adaptable to other fruit.
**Equipment**: Vegetable peeler, paring knife.
**Key parameters (example)**: ~1 lb 2 oz (500 g) apples : ~2 cm dice; steamed covered, low heat, **30 minutes**.
**Method**: Peel, core, and dice the apples (~2 cm). Melt butter in a saucepan with vanilla bean/seeds and a cinnamon stick, add apples and sugar, add a little water, cover with a parchment cartouche, and let steam over low heat 30 min, stirring occasionally (add water if the pan dries out). Remove the aromatics before serving.
**Chef's notes**: Toss cut apple in lemon juice first if it will sit before cooking, to prevent browning.

## Making Strawberry Jam
**For**: The classic macerate-then-boil jam method.
**Equipment**: Sterilized jars, skimmer, instant-read thermometer.
**Key parameters**: Macerate berries + sugar + vanilla **6-12 hours** (draws out juice). Cook to **~223°F/106°C**. Storage: 3 months at room temp, or 1 year if jars are pasteurized/sterilized.
**Method**: Hull/halve strawberries, toss with most of the sugar and scraped vanilla seeds/pod, cover and let macerate 6-12 h in a cool place. Transfer with juices to a pan, boil, skim foam continuously. Stir in remaining sugar + pectin, cook to 223°F/106°C. Off heat, stir in tartaric acid or lemon juice (needed for proper set). Fill hot sterilized jars, seal, and invert the jars so residual heat pasteurizes the lid/headspace; turn upright once cold.
**Chef's notes**: Sterilize jars by boiling 20 min or heating 20 min at 300°F/150°C; keep jars warm until filled or they may crack from thermal shock.

## Making Citrus Marmalade
**For**: A properly bitter-balanced whole-fruit citrus marmalade.
**Equipment**: Instant-read thermometer, skimmer, refractometer, immersion blender.
**Key parameters**: Triple/quadruple-blanch the cut fruit in fresh water each time (~10 min boils) to mellow bitterness, then poach in syrup at **176°F/80°C for ~1 hour**. Final cook to ~223°F/106°C, target **63° Brix** on the refractometer.
**Method**: Cut fruit into pieces; blanch 4 times in fresh water (~10 min each) to remove excess bitterness. Poach blanched fruit in a light syrup at 176°F/80°C for 1 hour. Separately boil juice + more sugar; combine with the poached fruit and syrup, boil skimming foam, stir in remaining sugar+pectin, cook to 223°F/106°C until the refractometer reads ~63° Brix. Puree partially with the immersion blender for texture, jar hot, invert to pasteurize.

## Making Quince Jelly
**For**: A clear fruit jelly using the whole fruit's natural pectin (peel/core/seeds).
**Equipment**: Food-safe muslin bag, fine-mesh sieve, digital scale, instant-read thermometer, refractometer.
**Key parameters**: **1:1 ratio by weight** of strained quince juice to sugar. Simmer fruit 45 min-1 hr to extract juice. Final cook to **223-224°F/106-107°C**, target **70° Brix**.
**Method**: Peel/core the quinces, reserving peel, cores, and seeds (pectin-rich) tied in a muslin bag. Simmer fruit + the bag together in water 45 min-1 hr until tender. Strain through the bag/sieve, pressing hard to extract all juice (discard or repurpose the pulp, e.g. into fruit jellies). Weigh the juice and add an equal weight of sugar; boil, skim, cook to 223-224°F/106-107°C until the refractometer reads ~70° Brix. Stir in tartaric acid/lemon juice, jar hot, invert to pasteurize.
**Chef's notes**: The muslin bag isn't strictly necessary but makes squeezing out the juice much easier.

## Making Mango Chutney
**For**: A savory-sweet condiment for pâtés, cheese, or fish.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife.
**Key parameters**: Reduce vinegar by 1/4 first; reduce stock by half; simmer until thick.
**Method**: Boil vinegar down by a quarter. Add sugar, diced onion, and crushed garlic; cook until vinegar has nearly evaporated. Add stock, reduce by half. Add diced mango, ginger, raisins, and pectin NH; simmer, stirring occasionally, until thick. Jar and cool.

## Making Passion Fruit and Apricot Fruit Jellies
**For**: Firm, sugar-dusted French-style fruit pâte de fruits.
**Equipment**: Instant-read thermometer, refractometer, confectionery frame.
**Key parameters**: Heat purees to 113°F/45°C before adding pectin. Cook to **223-225°F/106-107°C**, target **73-74° Brix**. Dry cut pieces 12 hours before coating in sugar.
**Method**: Heat combined fruit purees to 113°F/45°C, whisk in sugar+pectin mixture until dissolved. Boil, add glucose syrup and remaining sugar in stages while boiling, cook to 223-225°F/106-107°C (73-74° Brix). Off heat, whisk in dissolved tartaric acid/lemon juice. Pour into a frame on a silicone mat, cool, unmold, cut to size, dry 12 h on a rack, then coat both sides in granulated sugar.
**Chef's notes**: To freeze, skip the sugar coating — dust with cornstarch and wrap flat instead; after thawing, moisten lightly and coat in sugar as normal.

## Making Gianduja Paste
**For**: A hazelnut-chocolate paste (base for chocolate hazelnut spread, bonbons).
**Equipment**: Food processor, marble slab (optional, for fast cooling), instant-read thermometer.
**Key parameters**: Roast hazelnuts 275°F/140°C for 30 min. Cool the finished paste quickly to **79°F/26°C**.
**Method**: Roast skinned hazelnuts. Melt milk chocolate + cocoa butter over a bain-marie. Grind roasted hazelnuts with confectioners' sugar in the food processor to a smooth paste, then blend in the melted chocolate. Pour onto a marble slab (or a tray) and work with an offset spatula/scraper, folding it back toward the center, to cool it quickly and evenly to 79°F/26°C.

## Making Almond and Hazelnut Praline Paste
**For**: The classic caramelized-nut paste base for pralines, spreads, and fillings.
**Equipment**: Food processor.
**Key parameters**: Roast nuts 275°F/140°C for 30 min. Dry-caramelize sugar+water (boil 2-3 min, add warm nuts, cook until sugar re-melts to a light golden caramel coating).
**Method**: Roast blanched almonds and skinned hazelnuts. Make a syrup (sugar+water), boil until it crystallizes to a sandy texture, add vanilla seeds and the warm nuts, keep stirring/cooking until the sugar re-melts and coats the nuts in a light golden caramel. Turn out onto a mat, sprinkle with fleur de sel, cool. Break into chunks and grind in the food processor in stages (to avoid overheating) until smooth and slightly runny.
**Chef's notes**: Do not over-roast the nuts or over-cook the caramel — if either darkens too much, the finished praline turns bitter.

## Making Chocolate Hazelnut Spread
**For**: A from-scratch spread built on gianduja + praline (richer/more complex than a simple ganache).
**Equipment**: Food processor.
**Method**: Combine premade almond-hazelnut praline paste, gianduja paste, hazelnut butter, pâte à glacer blonde (blond glazing paste), and coconut oil in the food processor; blend smooth. Add powdered milk and cocoa powder, process again until smooth and glossy. Jar and store cool/dry.

## Making Chestnut Spread (*Crème de Marrons*)
**For**: The classic French chestnut spread, from raw peeled chestnuts.
**Equipment**: A small wire rack that fits inside a large saucepan (for steaming), food processor, fine-mesh sieve (optional, for extra smoothness).
**Key parameters**: Steam chestnuts ~30 min until a toothpick pierces easily. Syrup cooked to **234°F/112°C**.
**Method**: Steam peeled chestnuts on a rack over simmering water (covered) ~30 min until tender. Separately make a vanilla sugar syrup, cooked to 234°F/112°C. Process the chestnuts in the food processor while pouring in the hot syrup in a thin stream, processing to a paste; push through a fine sieve for an ultra-smooth finish.
**Chef's notes**: For a darker spread, roughly chop the steamed chestnuts and cook them directly in the sugar syrup ~15 min (letting them absorb it and swell) before processing. If the finished spread is too thick, loosen with a 1:1 sugar:water simple syrup.

## Drying Citrus Slices and Coconut Shavings
**For**: Shelf-stable decorative dried citrus wheels and coconut shavings (garnish, candying-adjacent).
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, vegetable peeler, wire racks.
**Key parameters**: Oven 160°F/70°C (lowest setting), dry **12 hours**.
**Method**: Cut untreated citrus crosswise into very thin slices (1/16-1/8 in / 2-3 mm). Arrange in a single layer on a wire rack. Dry in a 160°F/70°C oven for 12 hours. Cool and store airtight. (Coconut shavings are peeled/shaved thin and dried the same low-and-slow way.)
