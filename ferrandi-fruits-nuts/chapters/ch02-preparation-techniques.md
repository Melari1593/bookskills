# Chapter 2: Preparation Techniques

Source: FERRANDI Paris, *Fruits & Nuts*, Techniques > Preparation (book pp. 28-49).
Eleven foundational techniques for getting raw fruits and nuts ready to cook or cut.

## Peeling Pineapples
**For**: Removing the pineapple "eyes" (dark spots under the skin) while keeping as much flesh as possible — cleaner than just slicing off a thick layer of skin.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, paring knife (kitchen scissors for the crown).
**Method**:
1. Snip the small leaves off the base of the crown with scissors (all leaves can be removed if desired).
2. Cut off the base of the pineapple with the chef's knife.
3. Stand it upright; cut away the skin in strips top to bottom, following the fruit's natural curve.
4. Use the paring knife to cut a V-shaped groove along each diagonal row of eyes: insert the blade above an eye, angle in, follow the diagonal line around the fruit in a spiral, then come back and cut the matching angle from the other side to lift out the wedge.
5. Repeat row by row, working right to left, top to bottom, until every eye is removed.
**Chef's notes**: The eyes run in a natural diagonal spiral — work with that pattern rather than cutting straight down, or you waste flesh.

## Blanching Almonds
**For**: Slipping the skins off nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, pistachios) and off delicate fruits (peaches, plums, tomatoes) without bruising the flesh.
**Equipment**: Skimmer.
**Key parameters**: Boil 2-3 minutes.
**Method**: Drop nuts into boiling water; boil 2-3 min; lift out with a skimmer straight into cold water to stop cooking; drain, spread on paper towel; once cool enough to handle, rub each one between your fingers to pop the skin off.

## Skinning Hazelnuts
**For**: Removing hazelnut skins via dry roasting rather than blanching (better flavor from toasting).
**Equipment**: Silicone baking mat, clean kitchen towel.
**Key parameters**: Oven 350°F/180°C, roast 15 minutes, stir halfway.
**Method**: Spread hazelnuts on the mat, roast 15 min, watching closely near the end (they color and burn fast). When skins begin to crackle, tip them onto the towel, gather it closed, and roll/press vigorously on the counter to create friction that separates skin from nut. Open and rub any stubborn bits off by hand.
**Chef's notes**: Don't walk away in the last few minutes of roasting — hazelnuts go from done to burnt quickly.

## Peeling Peaches
**For**: Clean peeling of peaches (and similar fruit) without a peeler.
**Equipment**: Paring knife, skimmer.
**Key parameters**: Blanch 20 seconds.
**Method**: Score a shallow cross at the base of each peach. Plunge into boiling water 20 seconds, then straight into ice water to stop cooking. Peel away skin in sections, pulling from the base cross toward the stem.

## Peeling Chestnuts
**For**: Loosening both the tough outer shell and the bitter inner pellicle (skin) at once.
**Equipment**: Skimmer/slotted spoon, paring knife, silicone baking mat.
**Key parameters**: Soak 15 min; oven 480°F/250°C; roast 5 min.
**Method**: Soak chestnuts in cold water 15 min to soften the shell; drain. Preheat oven to 480°F/250°C. Slit the shell on the flat side of each nut with the paring knife tip, place on the mat, roast 5 min. While still warm, peel off both the shell and the inner brown membrane with the knife tip.

## Removing Melon Seeds
**For**: Cleanly hollowing a melon before cutting/serving.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, spoon.
**Method**: Trim off top and bottom. Cut the melon crosswise in half. Scrape out all seeds and fibrous threads with a spoon, leaving the flesh intact.
**Chef's notes**: For a decorative presentation, cut the melon using a crown cut (see *Historier*, ch. 3) instead of a plain crosswise cut.

## Opening Coconuts
**For**: Cracking a whole coconut and saving the water.
**Equipment**: Chef's knife, fine-mesh sieve.
**Method**: Hold the coconut over a large bowl. Tap firmly around the "equator" line with the blunt (spine) edge of the knife, 2-3 times, until it cracks open. Insert the knife tip into the crack to open it fully, draining the water into the bowl through the sieve. Pry the meat loose from the shell with a paring knife.

## Juicing Citrus Fruits by Hand
**For**: Getting maximum juice yield without a mechanical juicer.
**Equipment**: Paring knife, fine-mesh sieve, fork, spatula.
**Method**: Wash/dry fruit, then roll it firmly on the counter under your palm first — this breaks the internal membranes and makes juice release easier. Cut crosswise in half. Squeeze each half over a sieve set on a bowl, using a fork to help work the juice out. Finish by pressing down on the pulp caught in the sieve with a spatula to extract the last of the juice.
**Chef's notes**: Prefer organic fruit when using the zest as well as the juice.

## Preparing a Fresh Fruit Coulis
**For**: A quick raw fruit sauce/puree — also a good way to use fruit that's just past peak freshness.
**Equipment**: Fine-mesh sieve, immersion blender.
**Key parameters (example, raspberry)**: 1 lb 2 oz (500 g) fruit : 1¾ oz (50 g) sugar : scant ½ cup (100 ml) water : juice of ½ lemon.
**Method**: Rinse fruit in the sieve under cold water. Combine fruit, sugar, water, and lemon juice in a tall container; puree with the immersion blender. Push the puree through the fine sieve to remove seeds, pressing remaining pulp with the back of a ladle to extract all the juice.

## Preparing a Sorbet
**For**: The base technique for any fruit sorbet — a puree stabilized and sweetened to a precise sugar level, then churned.
**Equipment**: Immersion blender, refractometer, fine-mesh sieve, ice cream maker.
**Key parameters**: Gelatin (or 1 g stabilizer per sheet) bloomed in cold water; syrup heated to a boil; mixture matured 12 hours chilled before churning; **target sugar concentration 27-30° Brix** (measured with a refractometer) — too high and the sorbet won't freeze properly, too low and it turns hard/icy; adjust added sugar to reach this window regardless of the fruit's natural sugar. Storage: up to 2 weeks in the freezer.
**Method**: Bloom gelatin in cold water; squeeze out and dissolve the soaking liquid with sugar in a saucepan, bring to a boil. Off heat, stir in lemon juice and the melted gelatin; cool. Pour the near-cold syrup over the fruit, puree with the immersion blender, strain through the fine sieve. Chill 12 hours to mature the flavor. Check/adjust to 27-30° Brix. Churn in an ice cream maker per manufacturer instructions; freeze in an airtight container.
**Chef's notes**: Always verify sugar concentration by refractometer rather than trusting a fixed recipe ratio — natural sugar varies by fruit and ripeness.

## Preparing Brandied Cherries
**For**: A long-steeped preserved cherry (classic bar/pâtissier garnish).
**Equipment**: Kitchen scissors, clean airtight glass jar.
**Key parameters (example)**: 10½ oz (300 g) tart cherries (preferably Montmorency) : scant ½ cup (130 g) sugar : ½ cup (130 ml) kirsch or other 40% ABV brandy. Steeping time **1-2 months minimum**, turning the jar over once a week. Storage: up to 1 year refrigerated once opened (use a clean spoon each time).
**Method**: Wash/dry cherries; snip off the top half of each stem with scissors (intensifies flavor). Layer cherries and sugar in the jar, pour in the kirsch, seal tightly. Store in a cool, dark place, inverting the jar weekly, for at least 1-2 months before using.
