# Technique Patterns — Quick Reference

Flat index of all 40 techniques from ch. 2-4 (Preparation, Cutting, Cooking). Companion to the fuller chapter entries — use this to scan for the right technique fast, then open the chapter for full steps/parameters.

## Preparation

### Peeling Pineapples
**When**: Need max flesh yield with clean eye removal. **How**: Strip skin top-to-bottom, then cut V-groove spirals following the natural diagonal eye pattern. **Watch-outs**: Cutting straight down instead of following the diagonal wastes flesh.

### Blanching Almonds
**When**: Slipping skins off nuts, or peeling delicate fruit (peach/plum/tomato) without bruising. **How**: Boil 2-3 min, ice bath, rub skins off by hand. **Watch-outs**: Works for hazelnuts/pistachios too.

### Skinning Hazelnuts
**When**: Want roasted flavor while removing skins (vs. blanching). **How**: Roast 350°F/180°C 15 min, friction-rub in a towel. **Watch-outs**: Burns fast in the last few minutes — watch closely.

### Peeling Peaches
**When**: Clean peel without a peeler, delicate fruit. **How**: Score a cross, blanch 20 sec, ice bath, peel from the cross toward the stem.

### Peeling Chestnuts
**When**: Removing both shell and bitter inner pellicle at once. **How**: Soak 15 min, slit shell, roast 480°F/250C 5 min, peel warm. **Watch-outs**: Peel while still warm — cold chestnuts are much harder to peel.

### Removing Melon Seeds
**When**: Basic melon prep. **How**: Halve crosswise, scrape with a spoon. **Trade-off**: For presentation, cut with the Historier crown cut instead.

### Opening Coconuts
**When**: Whole coconut, want to save the water. **How**: Tap the "equator" with the knife spine until cracked, drain through a sieve, pry out meat.

### Juicing Citrus by Hand
**When**: No mechanical juicer, want max yield. **How**: Roll fruit on the counter first, halve, squeeze over a sieve+fork, press pulp with a spatula.

### Preparing a Fresh Fruit Coulis
**When**: Quick raw sauce, or using slightly overripe fruit. **How**: Puree fruit+sugar+water+lemon, strain through a fine sieve pressing with a ladle.

### Preparing a Sorbet
**When**: Any fruit sorbet base. **How**: Gelatin/stabilizer + syrup poured over fruit, blended, strained, matured 12h, churned. **Key number**: target **27-30° Brix** — too high won't freeze right, too low turns hard/icy. **Watch-outs**: Adjust sugar to hit the Brix window regardless of the fruit's natural sugar.

### Preparing Brandied Cherries
**When**: Long-steeped cocktail/pâtissier garnish cherries. **How**: Layer cherries+sugar in a jar, cover with kirsch (40% ABV), steep 1-2 months, inverting weekly. **Trade-off**: Takes real time — plan a month+ ahead.

## Cutting

### Slicing
**When**: Simple even crosswise slices. **How**: Chef's knife or mandoline (firm fruit only).

### Julienne Cut
**When**: Fine matchsticks for garnish/salad. **How**: ~2mm slices stacked, cut into ~2mm sticks. **Watch-outs**: Toss in lemon juice if not using immediately.

### Mirepoix Cut
**When**: Large even dice for fruit salad. **How**: Slice ~1cm thick, dice ~1cm.

### Preparing Fruit Balls
**When**: Decorative round scoops (melon, papaya, kiwi, dragon fruit). **How**: Melon baller, twist to scoop.

### Cutting Mangos
**When**: Clean flesh off the flat pit. **How**: Cut cheeks off both sides of pit, trim skin off center piece, scrape remaining flesh from pit, quarter and peel.

### Cutting Pineapples (Method 1)
**When**: Neat spears/rounds, minimal waste, have time. **How**: Quarter lengthwise, remove core, slice.

### Cutting Pineapples (Method 2)
**When**: Speed matters, or want to serve in the shell. **How**: Grapefruit knife frees flesh in one piece from each half; core out, slice. **Trade-off**: Faster but loses more flesh than Method 1.

### Cutting and Seeding Pomegranates
**When**: Intact arils, no mess. **How**: Score/remove a "hat," score sections, pull apart by hand, separate seeds over a bowl underwater-free (by hand over a bowl).

### Canelling
**When**: Decorative striped citrus/firm fruit. **How**: Channel knife removes zest strips at intervals before slicing.

### Cutting Citrus Zest into Thin Strips
**When**: Fine zest for garnish, infusing, candying. **How**: Peel wide strips, trim pith flat, stack and slice fine. **Watch-outs**: Untreated/organic fruit only; pith is bitter — remove all of it.

### Peeling and Segmenting Citrus Fruits (Supreming)
**When**: Membrane-free segments for salads/plating. **How**: Cut peel+pith+membrane off following the curve, then free segments between membranes over a bowl.

### Preparing a Fruit Crown (Historier)
**When**: Decorative two-piece zigzag-cut fruit (melon, citrus, kiwi). **How**: Alternating-angle "V" cuts all the way around, pull apart.

## Cooking

### Blanching Citrus Fruits
**When**: Prepping citrus for candying/marmalade — mellows bitterness. **How**: Submerge weighted, boil 5 min, ice bath. **Watch-outs**: Repeat in fresh water 3-4x for marmalade-level bitterness removal.

### Poaching Pears
**When**: Whole pears in flavored syrup (Belle Hélène, etc). **How**: Low oven (175°F/80°C) under a parchment cartouche, ~45 min.

### Roasting Pineapples
**When**: Whole caramelized roasted pineapple. **How**: 350°F/180°C, 30 min, basting every 5 min. **Watch-outs**: Frequent basting is what makes it tender — don't skip.

### Candying Kumquats
**When**: Fully candying small whole citrus without shriveling. **How**: Staged daily sugar increases over **7 days**, each poach ~185°F/85°C. **Watch-outs**: Gradual staging prevents water loss/shriveling — don't jump straight to high sugar.

### Glazing Chestnuts
**When**: Shiny fondant coating on candied chestnuts. **How**: Warm glaze via bain-marie, dip, set briefly in a hot oven.

### Frying Pineapple Fritters
**When**: Beer-batter-style fried fruit rounds. **How**: Fry 347-355°F/175-180°C, ~2 min/side.

### Making an Apple Compote
**When**: Basic stewed-fruit technique, any fruit. **How**: Butter+sugar+aromatics, steam covered (cartouche) low heat 30 min.

### Making Strawberry Jam
**When**: Classic macerate-then-boil jam. **How**: Macerate 6-12h, boil skimming, cook to ~223°F/106°C, acid stirred in off heat. **Watch-outs**: Jars must self-pasteurize by inverting hot.

### Making Citrus Marmalade
**When**: Whole-fruit citrus preserve. **How**: Blanch fruit 4x fresh water, poach 176°F/80°C 1h, boil to 63° Brix. **Key number**: 63° Brix target.

### Making Quince Jelly
**When**: Clear jelly using natural pectin from peel/core/seeds. **How**: 1:1 juice:sugar by weight, cook to 223-224°F/106-107°C, target 70° Brix.

### Making Mango Chutney
**When**: Savory-sweet condiment. **How**: Reduce vinegar 1/4, reduce stock by half, simmer with fruit to thick.

### Making Passion Fruit and Apricot Fruit Jellies
**When**: Firm sugar-dusted pâte de fruits. **How**: Cook purees+pectin+glucose to 223-225°F/106-107°C, target 73-74° Brix, dry 12h before sugar coating.

### Making Gianduja Paste
**When**: Hazelnut-chocolate paste base. **How**: Roast nuts 275°F/140°C, grind with sugar, blend in melted chocolate, cool fast to 79°F/26°C on marble.

### Making Almond and Hazelnut Praline Paste
**When**: Caramelized-nut paste for fillings/spreads. **How**: Dry caramel over roasted nuts, cool, grind in stages. **Watch-outs**: Don't over-roast nuts or over-cook caramel — turns bitter.

### Making Chocolate Hazelnut Spread
**When**: From-scratch richer Nutella-style spread. **How**: Blend praline+gianduja+hazelnut butter+pâte à glacer+coconut oil, then cocoa/milk powder.

### Making Chestnut Spread (Crème de Marrons)
**When**: Classic French chestnut spread. **How**: Steam chestnuts ~30 min, process with hot syrup (234°F/112°C) to a paste.

### Drying Citrus Slices and Coconut Shavings
**When**: Shelf-stable decorative dried citrus/coconut. **How**: Very thin slices, oven 160°F/70°C, 12 hours.
