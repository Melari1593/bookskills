## Core Techniques & Principles

**Sugar concentration is measured, not guessed.** Nearly every preserved or frozen preparation in this book (sorbets, jams, jellies, marmalade, candied fruit, pâte de fruits) is built around a target °Brix reading from a refractometer, not a fixed recipe ratio — natural sugar varies by fruit and ripeness, so the target is what's consistent. Sorbet bases: 27-30° Brix. Marmalade: 63° Brix. Quince jelly: 70° Brix. Fruit jellies (pâte de fruits): 73-74° Brix. Jam's finished target is 60-65% total sugar. See `cheatsheet.md` for the full table.

**Climacteric vs. non-climacteric fruit governs storage.** Climacteric fruits (apricots, avocados, bananas, peaches, nectarines, apples, pears, plums, tomatoes) keep ripening after picking and emit ethylene gas — use this to speed-ripen fruit by bagging it with a banana or apple. Non-climacteric fruits (citrus, berries) must be picked ripe and are *sensitive* to that ethylene — never store them next to climacteric fruit, or they'll spoil faster.

**Oxidation-prone fruit needs acid, immediately.** Apples, bananas, peaches, pears, and apricots brown fast once cut. The fix is always the same: coat with lemon, vinegar, or another citrus juice right after cutting, and prep as close to serving as possible rather than ahead of time.

**The core cutting vocabulary**: *brunoise* (~2mm fine dice), *julienne* (~2mm matchsticks), *mirepoix cut* (~1cm dice, this book's term for a large fruit-salad dice), *canelling* (decorative zest-groove striping before slicing), *historier* (the zigzag "fruit crown" cut for melon/citrus/kiwi), and *peeling and segmenting* (supreming) citrus. All are in ch. 3 with full steps.

**Candying and preserving are staged, not rushed.** Candying kumquats takes 7 days of gradually increasing sugar concentration — jumping straight to a high-sugar syrup shrivels the fruit by pulling water out too fast. Marmalade needs the peel blanched in fresh water 3-4 times to mellow bitterness before poaching. Jam benefits from macerating fruit and sugar 6-12 hours before cooking. These "slow down first" patterns repeat throughout ch. 4.

**Gelatin and butter have hard limits worth remembering.** Gelatin must dissolve into liquid no hotter than 158°F/70°C or it loses its set. Standard butter should be ≥82% butterfat; laminated doughs (puff pastry, croissant, brioche) specifically need 84% for clean layers. Full detail in ch. 11 (General Advice).

**Safety knife/prep notes**: apricot kernels contain trace cyanogenic compounds — adults max 3 kernels at a time, children max half a kernel; prickly pears must be handled with thick gloves when juicing (thorns); pomegranate seeds should be separated by hand over a bowl to avoid staining and crushing.
